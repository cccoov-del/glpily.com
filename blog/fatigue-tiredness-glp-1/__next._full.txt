19:T6b3,<p>Feeling unusually tired while taking a GLP-1 medicine can be frustrating, especially when you expected to feel more energetic as your health changed. Fatigue is a listed side effect: in the US prescribing information it was reported by 11% of adults on Wegovy 2.4 mg versus 5% on placebo, and by 5–7% on Zepbound versus 3% on placebo. But fatigue is not specific to one cause. It may coincide with treatment, and it can also reflect eating much less than usual, dehydration, poor sleep, anemia, an infection, thyroid problems, blood-glucose changes or another medicine.</p>
<h2 id="look-at-the-whole-pattern">Look at the whole pattern</h2>
<p>Ask yourself when the tiredness began and whether it follows nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, low food intake, a change in sleep, or a treatment change. People whose appetite drops a lot may unintentionally eat too little or struggle to keep a varied diet. This is not an argument for forcing food or setting a universal calorie target; it is a reason to discuss intake, symptoms and nutrition if fatigue persists. Some people find <a href="/blog/high-protein-meals-glp-1/">protein-forward meals</a> easier to manage when appetite is small.</p>
<h2 id="do-not-assume-it-is-only-the-medication">Do not assume it is only the medication</h2>
<p>If you have diabetes and also use insulin or a sulfonylurea, low blood glucose is a recognized risk when a GLP-1 medicine is added, and its symptoms can include weakness and shakiness. Other conditions can also cause fatigue. A clinician can consider your full medication list and whether evaluation or lab testing makes sense. Avoid starting iron, vitamin or stimulant supplements without knowing whether they fit the cause.</p>
