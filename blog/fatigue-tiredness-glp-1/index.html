Fatigue on GLP-1: Why You Might Feel So Tired

Feeling tired on a GLP-1 medicine? Explore contributors like low intake, dehydration and sleep, and what to discuss with your clinician.

♡By GLPily Editorial Team
Oct 11, 2026

  2. Do not assume it is only the medication

Feeling unusually tired while taking a GLP-1 medicine can be frustrating, especially when you expected to feel more energetic as your health changed. Fatigue is a listed side effect: in the US prescribing information it was reported by 11% of adults on Wegovy 2.4 mg versus 5% on placebo, and by 5–7% on Zepbound versus 3% on placebo. But fatigue is not specific to one cause. It may coincide with treatment, and it can also reflect eating much less than usual, dehydration, poor sleep, anemia, an infection, thyroid problems, blood-glucose changes or another medicine.

Look at the whole pattern

Ask yourself when the tiredness began and whether it follows nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, low food intake, a change in sleep, or a treatment change. People whose appetite drops a lot may unintentionally eat too little or struggle to keep a varied diet. This is not an argument for forcing food or setting a universal calorie target; it is a reason to discuss intake, symptoms and nutrition if fatigue persists. Some people find protein-forward meals easier to manage when appetite is small.

Do not assume it is only the medication

If you have diabetes and also use insulin or a sulfonylurea, low blood glucose is a recognized risk when a GLP-1 medicine is added, and its symptoms can include weakness and shakiness. Other conditions can also cause fatigue. A clinician can consider your full medication list and whether evaluation or lab testing makes sense. Avoid starting iron, vitamin or stimulant supplements without knowing whether they fit the cause.

What may help you prepare for an appointment

For several days, note sleep, fluid intake, whether meals are being skipped, activity, timing of fatigue and any accompanying symptoms. Record whether you feel mildly sleepy, physically weak, short of breath, dizzy, or unable to do usual tasks, because these descriptions mean different things. This log can support a conversation without encouraging obsessive tracking.

When to get help sooner

Seek prompt medical attention if fatigue is severe or rapidly worsening, or comes with fainting, confusion, chest pain, shortness of breath, persistent vomiting, very little urine, severe abdominal pain or symptoms of low blood glucose. Do not wait for a routine visit if something feels acutely wrong.

You do not need to prove that you can tolerate treatment. Your energy and ability to function are valid parts of the treatment conversation.
