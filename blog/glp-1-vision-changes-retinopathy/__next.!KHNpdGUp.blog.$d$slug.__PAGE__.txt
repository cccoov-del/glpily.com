1a:T64a,<p>Vision changes have many possible causes, and people with diabetes already have a higher risk of eye disease. Two separate topics come up with GLP-1 medicines: diabetic retinopathy getting worse when blood sugar improves quickly, and a rare optic-nerve condition called NAION that European regulators have linked to semaglutide. Neither means GLP-1 medicines routinely damage eyesight—but both are worth understanding.</p>
<h2 id="what-is-diabetic-retinopathy">What is diabetic retinopathy?</h2>
<p>The US National Eye Institute describes diabetic retinopathy as diabetes-related damage to the blood vessels in the retina, the light-sensitive tissue at the back of the eye. It often causes no symptoms early on, which is why the NEI recommends a dilated eye exam at least once a year for people with diabetes.</p>
<h2 id="why-rapid-glucose-improvement-matters">Why rapid glucose improvement matters</h2>
<p>The US labels for Mounjaro and Zepbound note that temporary worsening of diabetic retinopathy has been reported with rapid improvement in glucose control. The Ozempic label reports that in a 2-year trial of people with type 2 diabetes at high cardiovascular risk, diabetic retinopathy complications occurred in 3.0% of people on semaglutide versus 1.8% on placebo. The increase was concentrated in people who already had retinopathy (8.2% vs 5.2%), compared with those who didn&#39;t (0.7% vs 0.4%).</p>
<p>The labels advise that people with a history of diabetic retinopathy be monitored for progression. So if you have retinopathy, eye care and diabetes care belong in the same conversation.</p>
1b:T904,<h2 id="what-about-naion">What about NAION?</h2>
<p>Non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy (NAION) happens when blood flow to the front of the optic nerve drops, and it can cause sudden vision loss, often in one eye.</p>
<p>In June 2025, the European Medicines Agency&#39;s safety committee concluded that NAION is a very rare side effect of semaglutide medicines (Ozempic, Rybelsus and Wegovy)—affecting up to 1 in 10,000 people taking them. The EMA said several large observational studies suggested that adults with type 2 diabetes taking semaglutide had about double the risk of NAION compared with non-users, which works out to roughly one extra case per 10,000 person-years of treatment. That&#39;s a relative doubling of a rare event, not a common risk.</p>
<p>Regulators don&#39;t always move in step. As of October 2026, the current US labels for Ozempic and Wegovy that we reviewed don&#39;t list NAION, so warnings can differ depending on where you live.</p>
<h2 id="which-symptoms-should-not-wait">Which symptoms should not wait?</h2>
<p>Sudden loss of vision, a curtain or shadow across your sight, new severe eye pain, a sudden shower of floaters, flashes of light, or rapidly worsening eyesight all need urgent assessment—the EMA advises contacting a doctor right away for sudden vision loss. Don&#39;t assume these are ordinary side effects. For milder changes, such as blurriness that comes and goes, contact your eye-care professional or clinician and mention which medicine you take and when the change started.</p>
<h2 id="what-to-discuss-before-and-during-treatment">What to discuss before and during treatment</h2>
<p>Tell your prescriber if you have diabetic retinopathy or other eye disease, and keep up with recommended eye exams. Ask whether improving glucose control should change your eye-check schedule. Please don&#39;t stop or change treatment because of a headline—but do treat sudden vision symptoms as a reason to get seen promptly.</p>
<p>Accurate wording matters here: there are specific signals and a rare risk, but no evidence supports a blanket claim that GLP-1 medicines cause blindness. For a broader look at diabetes outcomes, see <a href="/blog/glp-1-diabetes-complications-evidence/">what GLP-1 medicines can do for diabetes complications</a>.</p>
