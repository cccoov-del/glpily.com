Medication Reminder Not Working? A 10-Minute Phone Checkup

Your shot reminder did not go off, and it probably was not the app's fault. Here is a calm, setting-by-setting checkup for iPhone and Android, plus a test you can run tonight.

If your medication reminder is not working, the cause is usually a phone setting, not the reminder itself: notifications turned off for the app, a Focus or Do Not Disturb mode silencing it, a battery limit on Android, or a schedule that drifted with a time zone change. Work through the checklist below, then set a test reminder for two minutes from now.

A weekly GLP-1 shot is exactly the kind of thing a reminder should catch, because a once-a-week habit is easy to lose. So it is worth ten minutes to make sure yours actually rings.

Step 1: Is the reminder itself set correctly?

Boring, but the most common fix:

  • Right day and time? Especially if your dose day changed recently.
  • AM vs PM? 9:00 p.m. and 9:00 a.m. look very similar in a hurry.
  • Saved? Close and reopen the app to make sure the change stuck.
  • On the right device? If you use an app on both a phone and a tablet, check which one is supposed to alert you.

If you want a whole system around the reminder (where to log, how to note the site), see How to Track Your GLP-1 Shots.

Step 2: iPhone notification settings to check

Go to Settings > Notifications, then tap the app.

  • Allow Notifications must be on.
  • Under alerts, make sure at least Lock Screen, Notification Center or Banners is selected, and check Sounds if you want to hear it.
  • Time Sensitive Notifications: if the app offers this toggle, turning it on lets its time-sensitive alerts come through right away, including during many Focus settings.
  • Scheduled Summary: if you use Scheduled Summary, check the app's Notification Delivery. If it says Scheduled Summary, your reminder may arrive bundled hours later. Switch it to Immediate Delivery.

Step 3: Is Focus or Do Not Disturb hiding it? (iPhone)

Focus is the stealthiest reminder-killer, because Sleep or Work Focus can turn on by schedule without you thinking about it.

  • Go to Settings > Focus, tap each Focus you use (Do Not Disturb, Sleep, Work, Personal).
  • Under allowed notifications, make sure Allow Notifications From includes your medication app (tap Add Apps).
  • Check each Focus's schedule. A Sleep Focus that runs until 8:00 a.m. will quietly swallow a 7:30 a.m. reminder.
  • Note: Apple says you cannot allow app notifications in the Driving Focus.

If you also wear an Apple Watch, the Watch app's notification settings can mirror your iPhone or be customized per app, so check there too.

Step 4: Android notification settings to check

Menu names vary by phone maker, but on most phones:

  • Settings > Notifications > App notifications, then pick the app, and make sure notifications are on. You can also go through Settings > Apps > [app] > Notifications.
  • Inside the app's notification settings, some apps have categories (channels). Make sure the reminder category specifically is on and allowed to make sound.
  • Android 13 and later: apps must ask permission to send notifications, and Google's developer docs say notifications are off by default for newly installed apps. If you tapped "Don't allow" when you first opened the app, that is your answer. Turn it on in the app's notification settings.

Step 5: Is Do Not Disturb or a Mode blocking it? (Android)

On Android 14 and later, Google's help says to go to Settings > Modes, pick a mode (such as Do Not Disturb or a bedtime mode), then open Notification filters and, under Apps, choose which apps can still notify you. Add your medication app.

Some phone makers use different names (for example, a bedtime or sleep mode in the display or digital well-being settings). If your phone looks different, search Settings for "Do Not Disturb."

Step 6: Battery and background limits (Android)

Android can hold back apps that run in the background, which can delay or drop reminders.

  • Battery: Settings > Apps > [app] > App battery usage (on some phones, then Allow background usage). Google describes three levels: Unrestricted, Optimized (the default) and Restricted. Restricted can stop an app from running in the background, and Google notes some notifications might be delayed. If reminders are unreliable, try Optimized or Unrestricted for your medication app.
  • Alarms & reminders: On Android 14 and later, apps that need exact-time alerts may need special permission at Settings > Apps > Special app access > Alarms & reminders. Google says this permission is denied by default for many newly installed apps, and stays denied after restoring a backup onto a new phone. If your app is listed there, allow it.
  • Unused apps: Android can pause app activity if unused, which removes permissions and stops notifications for apps you have not opened in a long time. If you only rely on the alert and rarely open the app, check [app] > Unused apps and turn off Pause app activity if unused.

Samsung, Xiaomi and other makers often add their own battery savers (for example, lists of "sleeping" apps). If your phone has one, make sure the medication app is not on it.

Step 7: Time zones and the clock change

Reminders follow your phone's clock, and clocks move.

  • Daylight saving time ends on Sunday, November 1, 2026 at 2:00 a.m. local time in most of the US. Most phone reminders keep the same local time (9:00 a.m. stays 9:00 a.m.), but it is a good weekend to glance at the next alert time.
  • Traveling across time zones? A Monday 8:00 a.m. reminder may fire at an odd local hour, or on what feels like the wrong day. Decide before you go whether you will follow home time or local time, and ask your prescriber or pharmacist if the trip shifts your dose day. Traveling With GLP-1 Medication covers the rest of the trip.
  • Make sure your phone's date and time are set automatically, or at least correctly.

Step 8: Run a test (the step everyone skips)

  1. Create a test reminder in the app for two minutes from now.
  2. Lock the phone and put it face down, the way it usually sits.
  3. Wait. Did it show on the lock screen? Make a sound? Vibrate?

Repeat the test after any of these: a new phone, a restore from backup, a major OS update, a new Focus or Mode, switching battery settings, or travel.

What a reminder can and cannot do

A reminder is a nudge, not a medical instruction.

  • It does not prove you took the dose. Log the dose when you take it, not when the alert fires.
  • It does not decide what to do if you are late. If you missed a dose or are unsure whether you took it, check the label's instructions for your specific medicine (Missed GLP-1 Dose? What the Label Says) and call your prescriber or pharmacist if anything is unclear. Never take an extra dose just because an alert went off.
  • Backups are fine, but keep one schedule. A calendar alert as a second nudge can help. Two different apps with two different times will not.

When to contact the app's support team

If the test reminder fails even with everything above checked, send support:

  • Your phone model and OS version.
  • The app version.
  • The reminder time you set and what happened instead.
  • Which of the steps above you already tried.

Keep health details out of public reviews or forums. "Weekly reminder did not fire on Pixel 8, Android 16" is all anyone needs.

Common questions

Why are my medication reminders not showing on iPhone?

Usually because notifications are off for the app, a Focus (often Sleep) is silencing it, or Scheduled Summary is delaying it. Check Settings > Notifications > [app] and Settings > Focus.

Why do Android app reminders arrive late or not at all?

Battery restrictions, a missing notification permission (Android 13+), or "Alarms & reminders" access being off are the usual causes. Set App battery usage to Optimized or Unrestricted and check Special app access.

Will daylight saving time mess up my weekly shot reminder?

Most phone reminders keep the same local clock time across the change. In 2026, US daylight saving time ends November 1; it is worth checking the next alert time that weekend.

Should I take my dose when the reminder goes off even if I am not sure of the schedule?

No. Follow your prescriber's instructions and your medicine's label. If you are unsure, call your prescriber or pharmacist before taking anything.
