18:T1fdf,<p>To qualify for the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge you need an eligible type of Medicare drug coverage, you must be 18 or older, and your BMI at the start of GLP-1 treatment has to fit one of three paths (35+, or 30+ or 27+ with certain conditions). You also can&#39;t have type 2 diabetes, moderate-to-severe sleep apnea or noncirrhotic fatty liver disease, which route you to your regular Part D plan. Below are six checks to run, plus a fill-in sheet so your call with the plan, pharmacy or prescriber is short.</p>
<p>If you need the basics first (what the Bridge is, the $50 copay, the dates), start with our <a href="/blog/medicare-glp-1-bridge-2026/">Medicare GLP-1 Bridge overview</a>. This page is the hands-on part.</p>
<h2 id="who-is-eligible-for-the-medicare-glp-1-bridge">Who is eligible for the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge?</h2>
<p>CMS and Medicare.gov list the rules in a few places. Here they are as six yes/no checks. You need a &quot;yes&quot; on 1, 2, 5 and 6, and a &quot;no&quot; on 3 and 4.</p>
<h3 id="1-do-you-have-an-eligible-type-of-medicare-drug-coverage">☐ 1. Do you have an eligible type of Medicare drug coverage?</h3>
<p>You need Medicare drug coverage, and the type of plan matters. For 2026, CMS lists:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Eligible</th>
<th>Not eligible (unless you <em>also</em> have a standalone Part D plan)</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody><tr>
<td>Standalone Part D plans (PDPs)</td>
<td>Medicare Advantage private fee-for-service (PFFS)</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Medicare Advantage HMO, HMO-POS, local or regional PPO plans with drug coverage</td>
<td>Cost plans</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Special Needs Plans (SNPs)</td>
<td>Prepayment plans</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Employer/union group plans (EGWPs)</td>
<td>PACE</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>LI NET</td>
<td>Fallback and religious fraternal benefit plans</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>
<p><strong>Where to find it:</strong> the front of your plan card, your Evidence of Coverage, or your Medicare.gov account. If your card says &quot;PFFS,&quot; check the right-hand column before going further.</p>
<p><strong>Open enrollment heads-up:</strong> Medicare Open Enrollment runs <strong>October 15 – December 7</strong>. If you&#39;re thinking about switching plans for 2027, check that the new plan type is still on the eligible side.</p>
<h3 id="2-are-you-18-and-does-your-starting-bmi-fit-one-of-the-three-paths">☐ 2. Are you 18+ and does your starting BMI fit one of the three paths?</h3>
<p>CMS measures BMI <strong>at the start of GLP-1 therapy</strong>, even if you started before July 1, 2026 or before you had Part D. There are three ways in:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Path</th>
<th>BMI at start of GLP-1 therapy</th>
<th>Plus at least one of these</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody><tr>
<td>A</td>
<td>35 or higher</td>
<td>Nothing else needed</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>B</td>
<td>30 or higher</td>
<td>Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (diastolic heart failure); uncontrolled high blood pressure (above 140/90 despite two blood-pressure medicines, per the CMS form); or chronic kidney disease stage 3a or higher</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>C</td>
<td>27 or higher</td>
<td>Prediabetes (by ADA criteria); previous heart attack; previous stroke; or symptomatic peripheral artery disease</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>
<p><strong>What to dig up:</strong> the date you started your first GLP-1 and the height and weight recorded around then. The CMS form tells prescribers that if your BMI has dropped since you started, they should pick your <em>initial</em> BMI. Today&#39;s number isn&#39;t the one that counts.</p>
<h3 id="3-do-you-have-a-condition-that-sends-you-to-part-d-instead">☐ 3. Do you have a condition that sends you to Part D instead?</h3>
<p>The Bridge excludes people with <strong>type 2 diabetes</strong>, <strong>moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea</strong> or <strong>noncirrhotic MASH</strong> (fatty liver disease), even if they meet the BMI criteria. That isn&#39;t necessarily bad news. Medicare.gov notes your Part D plan may cover GLP-1s for those conditions. The CMS prior authorization form adds one more: if the medicine is prescribed to lower the risk of heart attack and stroke in someone with established cardiovascular disease, the request goes to the Part D plan. CMS also says it may check submitted information against Medicare data, for example whether you have a type 2 diabetes diagnosis. Our <a href="/blog/medicare-bridge-vs-part-d-glp-1/">Bridge vs Part D guide</a> explains that path.</p>
<h3 id="4-are-you-already-getting-a-glp-1-through-your-part-d-plan">☐ 4. Are you already getting a GLP-1 through your Part D plan?</h3>
<p>If your Part D plan already covers your GLP-1, you&#39;re not eligible for the Bridge. CMS also tells pharmacies that if someone fills a GLP-1 through Part D, later Bridge refills will be rejected. Don&#39;t experiment with &quot;let&#39;s try it both ways&quot; at the counter.</p>
<h3 id="5-is-your-prescription-for-a-covered-product">☐ 5. Is your prescription for a covered product?</h3>
<p>Only <strong>Wegovy</strong> (pen or tablet), <strong>Foundayo</strong>, and the <strong>Zepbound KwikPen</strong> count. Zepbound single-dose vials and single-dose pens don&#39;t. If your current prescription is for one of those, talk to your prescriber about whether a different format makes sense. That decision belongs to them.</p>
<h3 id="6-can-your-prescriber-sign-the-attestation">☐ 6. Can your prescriber sign the attestation?</h3>
<p>Your prescriber has to certify, under penalty of perjury, that the medicine is prescribed to reduce and maintain body weight <strong>alongside ongoing lifestyle changes</strong>, including structured nutrition and physical activity. There&#39;s no separate program certificate to upload, but the attestation has to be true. Your prescriber doesn&#39;t need to be enrolled in Medicare, but they can&#39;t be on Medicare&#39;s Preclusion List.</p>

<h2 id="how-the-process-actually-runs">How the process actually runs</h2>
<p>This is the order CMS describes for providers and pharmacies. Knowing it helps you spot where things are stuck:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Prescription goes to the pharmacy first.</strong> If the prescriber submits the prior authorization before the pharmacy runs a claim, the system returns &quot;patient not found.&quot;</li>
<li><strong>The pharmacy submits a claim to the Bridge.</strong> It doesn&#39;t need a Part D denial first.</li>
<li><strong>A prior authorization request goes to your prescriber</strong>, usually within 24–72 hours. If nothing arrives after 72 hours, the prescriber can download the fax form from CMS.</li>
<li><strong>A decision comes within 72 hours</strong> of submission, sent to the prescriber. You get the decision by mail.</li>
<li><strong>Once approved, it&#39;s good through December 31, 2027</strong>, including refills and dose changes, unless you switch to a different GLP-1.</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="if-the-answer-is-no">If the answer is no</h2>
<p>There&#39;s <strong>no formal appeals process</strong> for the Bridge. If the denial came from wrong or incomplete information (an old BMI, a missing condition, a typo), your prescriber can <strong>resubmit</strong> the form with corrected or new information. If you simply don&#39;t meet the criteria, ask your prescriber whether a Part D path applies for another approved use, and compare plans during open enrollment. Our guide to <a href="/blog/read-health-plan-formulary-glp-1/">reading a formulary</a> helps with that comparison.</p>
<h2 id="common-questions">Common questions</h2>
<h3 id="does-medicare-advantage-qualify-for-the-glp-1-bridge">Does Medicare Advantage qualify for the GLP-1 Bridge?</h3>
<p>Many plans do. Medicare Advantage HMO, HMO-POS and PPO plans with drug coverage, Special Needs Plans and employer group plans are eligible. Private fee-for-service, cost, PACE and a few other plan types aren&#39;t, unless you also have a standalone Part D plan.</p>
<h3 id="my-bmi-is-lower-now-than-when-i-started-do-i-still-qualify">My BMI is lower now than when I started. Do I still qualify?</h3>
<p>CMS applies the BMI criteria at the start of GLP-1 therapy, including for people who started before July 1, 2026 or before enrolling in Part D. Have the records from your start date ready.</p>
<h3 id="who-submits-the-prior-authorization">Who submits the prior authorization?</h3>
<p>Your prescriber does, after the pharmacy runs a claim. The CMS form says only prescribing clinicians can submit it, so you can&#39;t send it yourself. CMS strongly encourages electronic prior authorization; fax is also accepted.</p>
<h3 id="who-can-help-me-for-free">Who can help me for free?</h3>
<p>Call 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227; TTY 1-877-486-2048) or your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP).</p>
