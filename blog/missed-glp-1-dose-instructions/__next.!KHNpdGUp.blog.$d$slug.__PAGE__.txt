1a:Tc1d,<p>A missed GLP-1 dose can happen for lots of ordinary reasons: travel, a busy week, illness, a pharmacy delay or simple confusion about the schedule. The key thing to know is that missed-dose instructions differ by product and formulation. A rule you saw online for one brand shouldn&#39;t be assumed to apply to another medicine—or to a daily tablet.</p>
<h2 id="why-the-exact-product-matters">Why the exact product matters</h2>
<p>Each product&#39;s official instructions spell out how long after a missed dose you can still take it, when to skip it instead, and how to get back to your regular schedule. These details genuinely differ. For example, the current US labels for Ozempic, Mounjaro/Zepbound and Wegovy injection each describe a different time window, and Wegovy tablets have their own separate instruction because they are taken daily. Some labels also say that after several missed doses in a row, the prescriber may need to restart dose escalation at a lower dose.</p>
<p>Instructions can also change when labels are updated, and they may differ between countries. So rather than relying on a friend&#39;s routine, a forum post or an AI answer, check the current patient leaflet or Instructions for Use for your exact product, or ask your pharmacist.</p>
<h2 id="what-not-to-do">What not to do</h2>
<ul>
<li>Don&#39;t double the next dose or take extra medicine to make up for the missed one.</li>
<li>Don&#39;t shorten the interval between doses unless your product&#39;s official instructions or your prescriber tell you to.</li>
<li>Don&#39;t simply restart at your previous dose after a longer break without asking—see <a href="/blog/restarting-glp-1-after-break/">restarting a GLP-1 after a break</a> for why that conversation matters.</li>
</ul>
<p>If several doses have been missed, or the break was caused by side effects or a supply problem, ask your prescriber whether the plan needs to change.</p>
<h2 id="what-information-should-you-have-ready">What information should you have ready?</h2>
<p>When you call a pharmacist or prescriber, it helps to know:</p>
<ul>
<li>The medication name, strength and formulation (pen, vial or tablet)</li>
<li>The date of your last dose and the date the missed one was due</li>
<li>How many doses were missed</li>
<li>Any symptoms, illness or access problems around that time</li>
<li>If you use the medicine for diabetes: recent glucose readings and your other diabetes medicines</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="make-future-doses-easier-to-remember">Make future doses easier to remember</h2>
<p>A calendar reminder or a <a href="/blog/how-to-track-glp-1-shots/">simple shot-tracking system</a> can reduce accidental gaps. Store medicine as instructed and keep the official leaflet somewhere easy to find. If reminders aren&#39;t enough because of cost, shift work, travel or side effects, talk with your care team about the real barrier.</p>
<p>A missed dose is a practical problem, not a reason to improvise. Check your exact product&#39;s instructions first, and get individualized advice when the gap is longer or the situation is unclear.</p>
