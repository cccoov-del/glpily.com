Missed GLP-1 Dose? What the Label Says for Each Medicine

Missed a GLP-1 dose? The rule depends on your medicine: Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro and Zepbound labels each set a different window. How to check yours.

A missed GLP-1 dose has no single rule: what to do depends on your exact medicine, because each product's label sets its own time window for taking a late dose, skipping it, and getting back on schedule. The safe first step is to read the missed-dose line in your product's current leaflet (or ask a pharmacist), and never double up to catch up. Missed doses happen for ordinary reasons: travel, a busy week, illness, a pharmacy delay or simple confusion about the schedule. But a rule you saw online for one brand shouldn't be assumed to apply to another medicine—or to a daily tablet.

Why the missed-dose rule differs by medicine

Each product's label spells out how long after a missed dose you can still take it, when to skip it instead, and how to get back to your regular schedule. These details genuinely differ. For example, the current US labels for Ozempic, Mounjaro/Zepbound and Wegovy injection each describe a different time window, and Wegovy tablets have their own separate instruction because they are taken daily. Some labels also say that after several missed doses in a row, the prescriber may need to restart dose escalation at a lower dose.

Instructions can also change when labels are updated, and they may differ between countries. So rather than relying on a friend's routine, a forum post or an AI answer, check the current patient leaflet or Instructions for Use for your exact product, or ask your pharmacist.

What not to do

  • Don't double the next dose or take extra medicine to make up for the missed one.
  • Don't shorten the interval between doses unless your product's official instructions or your prescriber tell you to.
  • Don't simply restart at your previous dose after a longer break without asking—see restarting a GLP-1 after a break for why that conversation matters.

If several doses have been missed, or the break was caused by side effects or a supply problem, ask your prescriber whether the plan needs to change.

What information should you have ready?

When you call a pharmacist or prescriber, it helps to know:

  • The medication name, strength and formulation (pen, vial or tablet)
  • The date of your last dose and the date the missed one was due
  • How many doses were missed
  • Any symptoms, illness or access problems around that time
  • If you use the medicine for diabetes: recent glucose readings and your other diabetes medicines

Make future doses easier to remember

A calendar reminder or a simple shot-tracking system can reduce accidental gaps. Store medicine as instructed and keep the official leaflet somewhere easy to find. If reminders aren't enough because of cost, shift work, travel or side effects, talk with your care team about the real barrier.

Common questions

What should I do if I miss a dose of my weekly GLP-1?

Look up the missed-dose line in the current leaflet for your exact product. The labels for Ozempic, Mounjaro/Zepbound and Wegovy injection each describe a different time window for taking a late dose, when to skip it instead, and how to return to your regular day. Don't double the next dose, and if you're unsure or more than one dose was missed, call your pharmacist or prescriber.

Does Zepbound have a 96-hour missed-dose window?

Zepbound and Mounjaro share the same missed-dose instruction, and the label says there is a specific window after the due date in which a late dose can still be taken, after which it should be skipped. Check the exact number in the current Zepbound leaflet rather than a figure from a forum, because labels get updated and can differ between countries.

What is the Wegovy missed dose rule?

The label says the rule depends on which Wegovy you use: the weekly injection has its own time window, and Wegovy tablets have a separate instruction because they are taken daily. Read the leaflet for your formulation, and note that some labels say that after several missed doses in a row, the prescriber may need to restart dose escalation at a lower dose.

What should I do if I miss a dose of liraglutide?

This article covers the weekly pens and Wegovy tablets, and liraglutide has its own leaflet with its own missed-dose instruction, so check that leaflet or ask your pharmacist. The same principles apply: don't take extra medicine to make up for the missed dose, and get individualized advice if the gap is long.

Is it OK to take two GLP-1 doses to catch up?

No. Don't double the next dose or take extra medicine to make up for a missed one, and don't shorten the interval between doses unless your product's official instructions or your prescriber tell you to. If the break was long or caused by side effects or a supply problem, ask your prescriber whether the plan needs to change.

The bottom line

A missed dose is a practical problem, not a reason to improvise. Check your exact product's instructions first, and get individualized advice when the gap is longer or the situation is unclear.
