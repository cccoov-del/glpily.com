<h2 id="fact-check-what-39-s-real-behind-the-jokes">Fact check: what&#39;s real behind the jokes</h2>
<p><strong>Exenatide was first, and the lizard is real.</strong> Exenatide was approved in 2005 as the first GLP-1 receptor agonist. It&#39;s based on exendin-4, a peptide found in the saliva of the Gila monster. Native human GLP-1 lasts only about two minutes in the blood before an enzyme called DPP-4 breaks it down. The lizard version resists that, and that&#39;s what made it useful.</p>
<p><strong>Generics are real too.</strong> FDA approved the first generic of Byetta (exenatide) in November 2024 and the first generic of Victoza (liraglutide) on December 23, 2024.</p>
<p><strong>Semaglutide really is several brands.</strong> Ozempic (injection) is for adults with type 2 diabetes. Wegovy is the weight-management brand and now comes as a weekly injection or a daily tablet. The current oral semaglutide label covers Rybelsus <em>and</em> Ozempic tablets for type 2 diabetes, and notes they aren&#39;t interchangeable milligram for milligram. Our <a href="/blog/semaglutide-vs-liraglutide/">semaglutide vs liraglutide guide</a> covers the older-vs-newer story.</p>
<p><strong>Tirzepatide works on two receptors.</strong> It&#39;s a GIP and GLP-1 receptor agonist, sold as Mounjaro (type 2 diabetes) and Zepbound (weight management and, in adults with obesity, moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnea). More in <a href="/blog/what-is-gip-tirzepatide/">what GIP is</a>.</p>
<p><strong>The pill rules really differ.</strong> The Wegovy tablet is taken on an empty stomach with up to 4 ounces of water, followed by a 30-minute wait before food, drinks or other oral medicines. Foundayo (orforglipron), approved April 1, 2026, is a small-molecule pill taken with or without food. See <a href="/blog/new-weight-loss-medicines-2026/">all the 2026 approvals</a>.</p>
<p><strong>The two blocked &quot;members&quot; are real problems.</strong> FDA says compounded drugs aren&#39;t FDA-approved, and that salt forms such as semaglutide sodium and semaglutide acetate are different active ingredients with no lawful basis for compounding. FDA has also warned sellers of semaglutide, tirzepatide and retatrutide labeled &quot;for research purposes&quot; but marketed to consumers, and says their quality is unknown.</p>
<p><strong>Retatrutide is still investigational.</strong> It&#39;s designed to act on GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon receptors, but it isn&#39;t FDA-approved. Our <a href="/blog/triple-agonist-retatrutide-explained/">triple-agonist explainer</a> covers what&#39;s known.</p>
<p><strong>&quot;None of us work for everyone&quot; is just true.</strong> In the 68-week trial on the Wegovy label, the average weight change was −14.9%, and 47.9% of people reached 15% or more. That&#39;s a range, not a promise.</p>

1c:T6dd,<h2 id="why-turn-drug-facts-into-a-group-chat">Why turn drug facts into a group chat?</h2>
<p>Because the real differences between GLP-1 medicines (how often you take them, whether it&#39;s a shot or a pill, what the label says about food, which ones are approved at all) get lost when everything is called &quot;Ozempic.&quot; A silly format makes those differences stick.</p>
<p>If you&#39;re taking one of these medicines, the most important chat is the one between you and your prescriber. It helps to bring notes. Logging your doses, symptoms and weight in one place, in an app like GLPily or a notebook, makes that conversation a lot more specific than &quot;it&#39;s been fine, I think?&quot;</p>
<h2 id="common-questions">Common questions</h2>
<h3 id="is-this-group-chat-based-on-real-conversations">Is this group chat based on real conversations?</h3>
<p>No. It&#39;s entirely fictional. Molecules don&#39;t talk, and no real person, company or patient is quoted. Only the fact check is meant to be taken literally.</p>
<h3 id="what-39-s-the-difference-between-all-these-glp-1-medicines">What&#39;s the difference between all these GLP-1 medicines?</h3>
<p>Mainly the molecule, how often you take it (daily or weekly), the form (injection or pill), what it&#39;s approved for, and what the label says about food and timing. Compare labels on DailyMed, and ask your prescriber which one fits you.</p>
<h3 id="why-does-everyone-call-every-glp-1-ozempic">Why does everyone call every GLP-1 &quot;Ozempic&quot;?</h3>
<p>It was one of the first widely known brand names, so it became shorthand. But Ozempic is specifically semaglutide for type 2 diabetes. Using the right name matters when you&#39;re talking about labels, coverage or side effects.</p>
