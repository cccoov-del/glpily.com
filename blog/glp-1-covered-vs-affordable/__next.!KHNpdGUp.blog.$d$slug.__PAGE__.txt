18:T195c,<p>A GLP-1 can be &quot;covered&quot; and still cost hundreds of dollars a month, because coverage only means your plan <strong>shares</strong> the cost. What you actually pay depends on three things: whether the drug is subject to your <strong>deductible</strong>, whether you owe a flat <strong>copay</strong> or a percentage <strong>coinsurance</strong>, and when in the year you hit your <strong>out-of-pocket maximum</strong>. Below is the plain-English version, then a worked 12-month example so you can see the numbers move.</p>

<p>For context, KFF&#39;s 2026 employer survey found that among workers in plans with three or more drug tiers, the <strong>average fourth-tier copay was $136</strong> and the <strong>average fourth-tier coinsurance was 30%</strong> (35% for third-tier drugs). For workers with a general annual deductible on single coverage, the <strong>average deductible was $1,955</strong>.</p>
<h2 id="why-the-same-glp-1-costs-25-in-one-plan-and-1-000-in-another">Why the same GLP-1 costs $25 in one plan and $1,000 in another</h2>
<p>Meet Sam. Sam&#39;s prescriber has a prior authorization approved, and the drug is on the formulary. Good news! Then the first pharmacy receipt arrives.</p>
<p>Whether Sam pays $25 or $1,000 that day depends on plan design, not on the drug. Let&#39;s run three versions of Sam&#39;s plan for a full year.</p>
<p><strong>Assumptions (hypothetical, for illustration only):</strong> the plan&#39;s negotiated price is <strong>$1,000 per monthly fill</strong>, Sam fills 12 times, and Sam has no other medical costs this year. Real negotiated prices vary and are rarely shown to members.</p>

<ul>
<li>Tier 4, <strong>$136 copay</strong> per fill</li>
<li><strong>Yearly total: 12 × $136 = $1,632</strong></li>
</ul>
<p>Boring, predictable, and easy to budget.</p>

<h2 id="how-savings-cards-and-copay-accumulators-change-the-math">How savings cards and &quot;copay accumulators&quot; change the math</h2>
<p>Manufacturer savings cards can help, but read the caps:</p>
<ul>
<li>Lilly&#39;s card for commercially insured people whose plan <strong>covers</strong> the Zepbound single-dose pen says you may pay &quot;as little as $25.&quot; But savings are capped at <strong>$100 per month</strong> and <strong>$1,300 per calendar year</strong>.</li>
<li>Novo Nordisk&#39;s Wegovy offer for commercially insured people is likewise &quot;as little as $25,&quot; with a <strong>$100 per month</strong> maximum.</li>
</ul>
<p>So in Plan B, a $300 coinsurance bill becomes roughly <strong>$200</strong> with a $100-capped card, not $25. In Plan A, a $136 copay could get much closer to $25.</p>
<p>There&#39;s another wrinkle. Some plans <strong>don&#39;t count</strong> what the card pays toward your deductible or out-of-pocket maximum. These are often called copay accumulator or alternate funding programs. Lilly&#39;s own terms say it can reduce or end savings if a plan doesn&#39;t apply card payments to your cost-sharing. Ask your plan directly: &quot;Do manufacturer card payments count toward my deductible?&quot;</p>
<p>Government-insured people (Medicare, Medicaid, TRICARE and others) generally can&#39;t use these cards. Our <a href="/blog/glp-1-savings-discounts-five-details/">savings offer checklist</a> covers the fine print.</p>

2a:Te84,<h2 id="when-does-self-pay-beat-covered">When does self-pay beat &quot;covered&quot;?</h2>
<p>Sometimes. In Plan B, Sam pays $300 a month after the deductible. The manufacturer&#39;s self-pay price for many Wegovy pen doses was <strong>$349</strong> in October 2026, and the Zepbound KwikPen ran <strong>$299–$449</strong> depending on dose (with the higher-dose price requiring a refill within 45 days). For a few months the gap can be small. But self-pay money generally <strong>doesn&#39;t count toward your deductible or out-of-pocket max</strong>, so it doesn&#39;t help if you expect other big medical bills. Run both columns before you decide.</p>

<h2 id="common-questions">Common questions</h2>
<h3 id="why-is-my-glp-1-so-expensive-if-insurance-covers-it">Why is my GLP-1 so expensive if insurance covers it?</h3>
<p>Usually because your deductible applies to the drug, or because you owe coinsurance (a percentage) instead of a flat copay. Early in the plan year you may be paying the full negotiated price until the deductible is met.</p>
<h3 id="does-a-glp-1-copay-count-toward-my-out-of-pocket-maximum">Does a GLP-1 copay count toward my out-of-pocket maximum?</h3>
<p>In-network cost-sharing for covered drugs generally counts. Premiums, drugs your plan doesn&#39;t cover and out-of-network costs don&#39;t. Manufacturer card payments may or may not count, depending on your plan.</p>
<h3 id="what-39-s-the-out-of-pocket-limit-for-2026">What&#39;s the out-of-pocket limit for 2026?</h3>
<p>For ACA-compliant plans, the 2026 in-network limit is $10,600 for an individual and $21,200 for a family. Many plans set lower limits.</p>
<h3 id="can-i-use-a-manufacturer-savings-card-on-medicare">Can I use a manufacturer savings card on Medicare?</h3>
<p>Generally no. Manufacturer card terms exclude people with government coverage like Medicare and Medicaid.</p>
