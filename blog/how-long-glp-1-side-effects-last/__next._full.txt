17:T8e9,<p>&quot;How long will this last?&quot; is one of the first questions people ask once nausea or an unsettled stomach shows up on a GLP-1 medicine. The honest answer: there is no single timeline that fits everyone. Trials do show a pattern for the group as a whole, and that pattern is genuinely reassuring for many people. But it can&#39;t tell you exactly what will happen in your body, and some symptoms should never be &quot;waited out.&quot;</p>
<h2 id="what-the-trials-show-about-timing">What the trials show about timing</h2>
<p>In the large weight-management trials, digestive side effects tended to cluster around the start of treatment and dose increases:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Semaglutide 2.4 mg (Wegovy):</strong> a pooled analysis of the STEP 1–3 trials found nausea in 43.9% of people on semaglutide vs 16.1% on placebo over 68 weeks. Most digestive side effects were mild to moderate, described as transient, and happened most often during or shortly after dose escalation. About 4.3% stopped treatment permanently because of them.</li>
<li><strong>Tirzepatide (Zepbound):</strong> the US label says the majority of nausea, vomiting and diarrhea events occurred during dose escalation and decreased over time.</li>
</ul>
<p>That is why many prescribing schedules start low and step up gradually. If you want to understand why things can flare after a step-up, we cover that in <a href="/blog/glp-1-side-effects-dose-escalation/">why GLP-1 side effects can flare during dose escalation</a>.</p>
<h2 id="why-your-timeline-may-not-match-the-average">Why your timeline may not match the average</h2>
<p>Those numbers describe groups of trial participants, not individuals. The exact medicine and formulation, your dose schedule, other health conditions, other medicines and plain individual variation all matter. Some people barely notice anything; others have symptoms that linger, come back after each step-up, or turn into something different.</p>
<p>It&#39;s also worth remembering that nausea, diarrhea, constipation, reflux and stomach pain have plenty of causes besides GLP-1 treatment, such as a stomach bug, another prescription or an underlying digestive condition. A symptom that starts after a dose may be related, but timing alone doesn&#39;t prove it.</p>

1e:T937,<h2 id="a-short-log-beats-guessing">A short log beats guessing</h2>
<p>A few notes can make your next conversation with your prescriber much more useful:</p>
<ul>
<li>when the symptom started, and the date of your last dose change</li>
<li>how often it happens and whether it&#39;s getting better or worse</li>
<li>whether you can eat and drink enough</li>
<li>how much it affects sleep, work or daily life</li>
</ul>
<p>Keep it brief. The goal is a clear picture for your care team, not monitoring every sensation all day. Our guide on <a href="/blog/track-glp-1-side-effects/">how to track GLP-1 side effects</a> has a simple format.</p>
<h2 id="when-not-to-wait-it-out">When not to wait it out</h2>
<p>The US labels for Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound and Mounjaro include a warning that gastrointestinal reactions can sometimes be severe. In the Wegovy injection weight-management trials, severe digestive reactions were reported in 4.1% of people vs 0.9% on placebo.</p>
<p>Seek urgent medical care, rather than waiting for an &quot;adjustment period,&quot; if you have:</p>
<ul>
<li>severe stomach pain that doesn&#39;t go away, with or without vomiting (it can spread to the back)</li>
<li>repeated vomiting or being unable to keep fluids down</li>
<li>signs of dehydration, such as very little urine, dizziness or feeling faint</li>
<li>signs of a serious allergic reaction, such as swelling of the face, lips or throat, or trouble breathing</li>
<li>any other symptom that feels severe or frightening</li>
</ul>
<p>Symptoms that aren&#39;t an emergency but keep interfering with eating, drinking, sleep or daily life also deserve a call to your prescriber. &quot;Common&quot; doesn&#39;t mean you have to just put up with it.</p>
<h2 id="don-39-t-self-adjust-the-schedule">Don&#39;t self-adjust the schedule</h2>
<p>It can be tempting to skip a dose, stretch the time between doses, take extra, or lean on over-the-counter remedies for weeks. Instructions differ by medicine and situation, so talk to your prescriber or pharmacist first. They can explain the options for your exact prescription and check whether something else is going on.</p>
