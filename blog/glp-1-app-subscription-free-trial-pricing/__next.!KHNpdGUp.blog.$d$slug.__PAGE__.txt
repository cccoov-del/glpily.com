18:T1518,<p>The fastest way to compare GLP-1 app pricing is to turn every plan into a <strong>yearly cost</strong>, then check what the free version already does, how the trial renews, and where you cancel. A small weekly number can end up being the most expensive way to pay.</p>
<p>App paywalls are designed to make one button feel obvious. This guide gives you a calmer, five-minute way to decide whether you need to pay at all, and if so, which plan.</p>

<p>Those example prices are GLPily&#39;s own PRO prices in the US, and we use them because we can vouch for them. Notice what the math shows: the weekly plan costs more than five times the yearly plan over a full year. Weekly plans make sense if you only want a few weeks of a paid feature, say before an appointment. They make little sense as a way to pay for a whole year.</p>
<p>The yearly plan has its own catch: you pay up front. If you are not sure you will still be using the app in three months, a monthly plan may cost more per month but less in total.</p>
<p>A quick decision rule:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Trying it out for a few weeks?</strong> Weekly or monthly, with a calendar reminder to review.</li>
<li><strong>Fairly sure you will track for many months?</strong> Yearly is usually cheapest.</li>
<li><strong>Not sure you need paid features?</strong> Start with the free version and pay nothing.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="what-does-a-free-trial-actually-mean-in-an-app-store">What does a &quot;free trial&quot; actually mean in an app store?</h2>
<p>On Apple&#39;s App Store, a free trial is one of three kinds of <strong>introductory offer</strong> a developer can set up. Apple&#39;s developer documentation lists:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Free trial:</strong> free access for a set period.</li>
<li><strong>Pay as you go:</strong> a discounted price each billing period for a set time.</li>
<li><strong>Pay up front:</strong> one discounted payment for a set time.</li>
</ul>
<p>All three roll into the regular price automatically unless you cancel. Apple also says customers can redeem one introductory offer per subscription group, so you usually cannot restart a trial on the same app.</p>
<p>Google Play works the same way in practice. Its help page says that when the trial ends, the first billing period begins and you are charged automatically; to avoid that, cancel before the trial expires. Google also says you will get an email when your trial is about to end.</p>
<p>Apple requires developers to show the <strong>full renewal price</strong> clearly on the sign-up screen and, for free trials, how long the trial lasts and what you will pay after. If you cannot find those three things on a paywall, scroll down or tap the fine print before you tap the big button.</p>
<h2 id="what-should-you-check-before-starting-a-trial">What should you check before starting a trial?</h2>
<p>Run through this list on the paywall itself:</p>
<ul>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> What is the price after the trial, and how often is it billed?</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> When exactly does the trial end? (Put it in your calendar now.)</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> Is the offer introductory, meaning the second year costs more?</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> Which features are paid, and which stay free if you cancel?</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> Can you export your logs if you leave? (More on that in <a href="/blog/export-delete-glp-1-tracking-data/">Can You Export or Delete Your GLP-1 Tracking Data?</a>)</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> Is the subscription billed through Apple or Google, or on the company&#39;s website?</li>
</ul>
<p>That last item matters because it decides where you cancel.</p>
<h2 id="what-do-you-actually-need-to-pay-for">What do you actually need to pay for?</h2>
<p>Before paying, write down the three things you open the app for most. For many people on a GLP-1, that list is something like: log the shot, log the weight, get a reminder.</p>
<p>In GLPily, shot and pill logging with reminders, weight tracking and other core logging are free. PRO adds extras on top. Other apps draw the free/paid line in different places, which is exactly why &quot;which is cheapest&quot; is the wrong first question. The right one is: does the free version already cover my three things?</p>

2a:T10b8,<h2 id="how-do-you-cancel-an-app-subscription-on-iphone-or-android">How do you cancel an app subscription on iPhone or Android?</h2>
<p>Deleting the app does <strong>not</strong> cancel the subscription; Google&#39;s help page says so plainly. On iPhone, too, subscriptions are managed in your Apple Account, not inside the app. Cancel in your account:</p>
<p><strong>iPhone or iPad (Apple&#39;s steps):</strong></p>
<ol>
<li>Open <strong>Settings</strong> and tap your name.</li>
<li>Tap <strong>Subscriptions</strong>.</li>
<li>Tap the subscription.</li>
<li>Tap <strong>Cancel Subscription</strong> (you may need to scroll). If there is no cancel button, or you see an expiration message in red, it is already canceled.</li>
</ol>
<p>Apple advises canceling a free trial <strong>at least 24 hours before it ends</strong> if you do not want it to renew.</p>
<p><strong>Android (Google Play&#39;s steps):</strong></p>
<ol>
<li>Open the Google Play subscriptions page, signed in to the account that pays.</li>
<li>Select the subscription.</li>
<li>Tap <strong>Cancel subscription</strong> and follow the prompts.</li>
</ol>
<p>On Google Play, you keep access until the end of the period you already paid for. Google&#39;s own example: buy a one-year plan on January 1, cancel on July 1, and you keep access until December 31 without being charged again.</p>
<p><strong>Subscribed on a website?</strong> Cancel through that company&#39;s account page and save the confirmation email.</p>
<h2 id="what-if-you-were-charged-when-you-meant-to-cancel">What if you were charged when you meant to cancel?</h2>
<p>On Apple, you can ask for a refund at <strong>reportaproblem.apple.com</strong>: sign in, choose &quot;Request a refund,&quot; pick a reason, then select the item. Apple says it typically responds within 24 to 48 hours. Refunds are not guaranteed, so a calendar reminder before the renewal date is still the better plan. On Google Play, the help center says contacting the app&#39;s developer is often the quickest fix for purchase problems, and it has a separate &quot;Request a refund on Google Play&quot; process for the rest.</p>

<h2 id="common-questions">Common questions</h2>
<h3 id="is-a-weekly-app-subscription-a-bad-deal">Is a weekly app subscription a bad deal?</h3>
<p>Not always. It is the most expensive way to pay for a whole year, but it can be the cheapest way to try a paid feature for two or three weeks. Annualize it and decide based on how long you will really use it.</p>
<h3 id="does-deleting-a-glp-1-app-cancel-my-subscription">Does deleting a GLP-1 app cancel my subscription?</h3>
<p>No, not on Google Play, and on iPhone the subscription lives in your Apple Account regardless. Cancel in Settings &gt; your name &gt; Subscriptions on iPhone, or in Google Play subscriptions on Android. If you subscribed on a website, cancel there.</p>
<h3 id="when-should-i-cancel-a-free-trial-so-i-am-not-charged">When should I cancel a free trial so I am not charged?</h3>
<p>Apple recommends at least 24 hours before the trial ends. Google says to cancel before the trial period expires. Set a reminder a couple of days early to be safe.</p>
<h3 id="will-i-lose-my-logs-if-i-cancel">Will I lose my logs if I cancel?</h3>
<p>It depends on the app. Many keep free features working after a paid plan ends, but check before you subscribe, and export your data if the app offers it.</p>
