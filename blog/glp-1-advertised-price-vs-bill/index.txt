15:T19de,<p>The advertised GLP-1 price is usually the price of <strong>one specific dose, in one form, paid one way</strong>, sometimes only if you <strong>refill on time</strong>, and often <strong>without</strong> the prescriber visit, membership, shipping or tax. Your bill is all of that added together. &quot;Starting at $149&quot; can be completely true and still not be what you pay in month three.</p>
<p>Here&#39;s where the gap comes from, using the manufacturers&#39; own self-pay terms as of October 2026, plus a quote checklist to fill in before you hand over a card number.</p>
<h2 id="why-is-the-advertised-glp-1-price-lower-than-what-i-pay">Why is the advertised GLP-1 price lower than what I pay?</h2>
<p>There are six usual suspects. Most bills involve at least two.</p>
<h3 id="1-the-price-is-for-the-starting-dose">1. The price is for the starting dose</h3>
<p>Self-pay prices often climb with the dose. From the manufacturers&#39; pages:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Product (self-pay)</th>
<th>Lowest listed</th>
<th>Higher doses</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody><tr>
<td>Zepbound KwikPen or vials</td>
<td>$299 (2.5 mg)</td>
<td>$399 (5 mg); $449 (7.5–15 mg) with on-time refills; regular price up to $699</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Wegovy pill</td>
<td>$149 (1.5 mg)</td>
<td>$199 (4 mg); $299 (9 mg, 25 mg)</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Foundayo pill</td>
<td>$149 (0.8 mg; 2.5 mg temporarily)</td>
<td>$199–$299; top doses $349 without on-time refills</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Wegovy pen</td>
<td>$199 intro (first 2 months, starter doses, new patients)</td>
<td>$349 for most doses; $399 for Wegovy HD</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>
<p>Many people move up doses over the first months. Zepbound&#39;s label, for example, starts at 2.5 mg and increases in 2.5 mg steps after at least 4 weeks, as the prescriber decides. Your price can rise right along with it.</p>
<p><strong>Worked example (illustration only, not a dosing plan):</strong> say a prescriber moves someone on the Zepbound KwikPen from 2.5 mg to 5 mg to 7.5 mg to 10 mg over the first months, then holds at 10 mg. Six months of self-pay would be $299 + $399 + $449 + $449 + $449 + $449 = <strong>$2,494</strong>. Six months at the headline &quot;$299&quot; would be $1,794. That&#39;s a <strong>$700 gap</strong> before any fees, and nobody lied.</p>
<h3 id="2-the-refill-window">2. The refill window</h3>
<p>Lilly&#39;s terms say the $449 price for higher Zepbound doses applies only if you complete each refill <strong>within 45 days</strong> of receiving the last one. Refill later and the regular price applies, <strong>$699</strong> for 10–15 mg. Foundayo&#39;s top doses work the same way ($299 on time, $349 otherwise). A vacation, a delayed appointment or a shipping hiccup can cost real money.</p>
<p>A refill reminder helps more than you&#39;d think. GLPily&#39;s shot and pill log has reminders, and seeing your last dose date makes the 45-day math easy.</p>
<h3 id="3-the-form-vial-pen-kwikpen-or-pill">3. The form: vial, pen, KwikPen or pill</h3>
<p>Different forms can be priced and covered differently:</p>
<ul>
<li>Lilly&#39;s self-pay prices now match for Zepbound <strong>vials and KwikPen</strong>, but its <strong>savings card</strong> for commercially insured people applies only to the KwikPen and single-dose pen, not vials.</li>
<li>Medicare&#39;s GLP-1 Bridge covers the Zepbound <strong>KwikPen</strong> but not vials or single-dose pens.</li>
<li>Multi-dose pens use separate pen needles, a small extra cost.</li>
<li>A &quot;month&quot; isn&#39;t always the same length: Lilly defines a Zepbound month as <strong>28 days</strong>, and Foundayo&#39;s as <strong>30 days</strong>.</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="4-the-membership-or-visit-fee-is-separate">4. The membership or visit fee is separate</h3>
<p>Many telehealth programs charge for the prescriber relationship (an intake fee, a monthly membership, or per-visit fees) <strong>on top of</strong> the medicine. A low drug price paired with a monthly membership is still two charges. Ask what the fee covers: follow-ups, messaging, dose changes, cancellation. Our <a href="/blog/glp-1-telehealth-appointment-questions/">telehealth appointment checklist</a> has the full list.</p>
<h3 id="5-shipping-taxes-and-fees-may-apply">5. Shipping, taxes and &quot;fees may apply&quot;</h3>
<p>Both Lilly&#39;s Zepbound and Foundayo terms say &quot;additional taxes and fees may apply.&quot; Cold-chain shipping for injectables, expedited delivery and state tax can add to the total. Ask for the <strong>delivered</strong> price.</p>
<h3 id="6-insurance-vs-cash-they-don-39-t-mix-the-way-you-39-d-hope">6. Insurance vs cash: they don&#39;t mix the way you&#39;d hope</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Self-pay payments generally don&#39;t count toward your insurance deductible or out-of-pocket maximum.</strong></li>
<li><strong>Savings cards for insured people have caps.</strong> Novo&#39;s Wegovy offer and Lilly&#39;s single-dose-pen card advertise &quot;as little as $25,&quot; but each caps savings at <strong>$100 a month</strong> (Lilly also at <strong>$1,300 a year</strong>). If your plan leaves you owing $400, a $100-capped card leaves $300.</li>
<li><strong>Government coverage usually rules out manufacturer cards</strong> (Medicare, Medicaid, TRICARE and others).</li>
</ul>
<p>We do the insurance math step by step in <a href="/blog/glp-1-covered-vs-affordable/">covered vs affordable</a>.</p>
<h2 id="what-about-the-really-low-prices">What about the really low prices?</h2>
<p>If an offer is far below the manufacturers&#39; own self-pay prices, ask what the product actually is. Compounded GLP-1 drugs <strong>are not FDA-approved</strong>, and the FDA doesn&#39;t review their safety, effectiveness or quality before marketing. As of May 31, 2026, the FDA had received <strong>990</strong> adverse event reports for compounded semaglutide and <strong>over 730</strong> for compounded tirzepatide, and believes those are underreported. On <strong>March 3, 2026</strong>, the FDA sent warning letters to <strong>30 telehealth companies</strong> over misleading claims, including suggesting compounded products were the same as approved ones. More in our breakdown of <a href="/blog/fda-telehealth-glp-1-warning-letters-2026/">the FDA&#39;s telehealth warning letters</a>.</p>
<p>The FDA also advises being wary of sellers offering deep discounts or skipping proper screening. You can look up whether an online pharmacy is state-licensed through the FDA&#39;s BeSafeRx resources; our guide on <a href="/blog/check-legitimate-online-pharmacy-glp-1/">checking an online pharmacy</a> walks through it.</p>

<h2 id="common-questions">Common questions</h2>
<h3 id="why-does-my-zepbound-cost-more-than-299">Why does my Zepbound cost more than $299?</h3>
<p>$299 is Lilly&#39;s self-pay price for the 2.5 mg dose. Higher doses are $399 (5 mg) and $449 (7.5–15 mg) when refilled within 45 days, and regular prices up to $699 apply if not.</p>
<h3 id="is-the-wegovy-pill-cheaper-than-the-pen">Is the Wegovy pill cheaper than the pen?</h3>
<p>On self-pay, the pill is listed at $149–$299 a month depending on dose; the pen is $349 for most doses after the introductory offer. Which is right for you is a prescriber decision, not a price decision.</p>
<h3 id="do-telehealth-glp-1-prices-include-the-medication">Do telehealth GLP-1 prices include the medication?</h3>
<p>Sometimes, sometimes not. Many programs bill the prescriber membership separately from the medicine. Ask for both numbers, in writing.</p>
<h3 id="does-a-self-pay-glp-1-count-toward-my-deductible">Does a self-pay GLP-1 count toward my deductible?</h3>
<p>Generally no. Payments made outside your insurance usually don&#39;t count toward your deductible or out-of-pocket maximum. Ask your plan.</p>
