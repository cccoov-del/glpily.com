GLP-1 Price vs Your Bill: Why "From $149" Isn't What You Pay

"Starting at $149" is real, for one dose, one form, one payment method and sometimes one refill window. Here's where the gap between the advertised GLP-1 price and the bill comes from, with a quote checklist to fill in before you pay.

♡By GLPily Editorial Team

The advertised GLP-1 price is usually the price of one specific dose, in one form, paid one way, sometimes only if you refill on time, and often without the prescriber visit, membership, shipping or tax. Your bill is all of that added together. "Starting at $149" can be completely true and still not be what you pay in month three.

Here's where the gap comes from, using the manufacturers' own self-pay terms as of October 2026, plus a quote checklist to fill in before you hand over a card number.

Why is the advertised GLP-1 price lower than what I pay?

There are six usual suspects. Most bills involve at least two.

1. The price is for the starting dose

Self-pay prices often climb with the dose. From the manufacturers' pages:

Product (self-pay) Lowest listed Higher doses
Zepbound KwikPen or vials $299 (2.5 mg) $399 (5 mg); $449 (7.5–15 mg) with on-time refills; regular price up to $699
Wegovy pill $149 (1.5 mg) $199 (4 mg); $299 (9 mg, 25 mg)
Foundayo pill $149 (0.8 mg; 2.5 mg temporarily) $199–$299; top doses $349 without on-time refills
Wegovy pen $199 intro (first 2 months, starter doses, new patients) $349 for most doses; $399 for Wegovy HD

Many people move up doses over the first months. Zepbound's label, for example, starts at 2.5 mg and increases in 2.5 mg steps after at least 4 weeks, as the prescriber decides. Your price can rise right along with it.

Worked example (illustration only, not a dosing plan): say a prescriber moves someone on the Zepbound KwikPen from 2.5 mg to 5 mg to 7.5 mg to 10 mg over the first months, then holds at 10 mg. Six months of self-pay would be $299 + $399 + $449 + $449 + $449 + $449 = $2,494. Six months at the headline "$299" would be $1,794. That's a $700 gap before any fees, and nobody lied.

2. The refill window

Lilly's terms say the $449 price for higher Zepbound doses applies only if you complete each refill within 45 days of receiving the last one. Refill later and the regular price applies, $699 for 10–15 mg. Foundayo's top doses work the same way ($299 on time, $349 otherwise). A vacation, a delayed appointment or a shipping hiccup can cost real money.

A refill reminder helps more than you'd think. GLPily's shot and pill log has reminders, and seeing your last dose date makes the 45-day math easy.

3. The form: vial, pen, KwikPen or pill

Different forms can be priced and covered differently:

  • Lilly's self-pay prices now match for Zepbound vials and KwikPen, but its savings card for commercially insured people applies only to the KwikPen and single-dose pen, not vials.
  • Medicare's GLP-1 Bridge covers the Zepbound KwikPen but not vials or single-dose pens.
  • Multi-dose pens use separate pen needles, a small extra cost.
  • A "month" isn't always the same length: Lilly defines a Zepbound month as 28 days, and Foundayo's as 30 days.

4. The membership or visit fee is separate

Many telehealth programs charge for the prescriber relationship (an intake fee, a monthly membership, or per-visit fees) on top of the medicine. A low drug price paired with a monthly membership is still two charges. Ask what the fee covers: follow-ups, messaging, dose changes, cancellation. Our telehealth appointment checklist has the full list.

5. Shipping, taxes and "fees may apply"

Both Lilly's Zepbound and Foundayo terms say "additional taxes and fees may apply." Cold-chain shipping for injectables, expedited delivery and state tax can add to the total. Ask for the delivered price.

6. Insurance vs cash: they don't mix the way you'd hope

  • Self-pay payments generally don't count toward your insurance deductible or out-of-pocket maximum.
  • Savings cards for insured people have caps. Novo's Wegovy offer and Lilly's single-dose-pen card advertise "as little as $25," but each caps savings at $100 a month (Lilly also at $1,300 a year). If your plan leaves you owing $400, a $100-capped card leaves $300.
  • Government coverage usually rules out manufacturer cards (Medicare, Medicaid, TRICARE and others).

What about the really low prices?

If an offer is far below the manufacturers' own self-pay prices, ask what the product actually is. Compounded GLP-1 drugs are not FDA-approved, and the FDA doesn't review their safety, effectiveness or quality before marketing. As of May 31, 2026, the FDA had received 990 adverse event reports for compounded semaglutide and over 730 for compounded tirzepatide, and believes those are underreported. On March 3, 2026, the FDA sent warning letters to 30 telehealth companies over misleading claims, including suggesting compounded products were the same as approved ones. More in our breakdown of the FDA's telehealth warning letters.

The FDA also advises being wary of sellers offering deep discounts or skipping proper screening. You can look up whether an online pharmacy is state-licensed through the FDA's BeSafeRx resources; our guide on checking an online pharmacy walks through it.

Common questions

Why does my Zepbound cost more than $299?

$299 is Lilly's self-pay price for the 2.5 mg dose. Higher doses are $399 (5 mg) and $449 (7.5–15 mg) when refilled within 45 days, and regular prices up to $699 apply if not.

Is the Wegovy pill cheaper than the pen?

On self-pay, the pill is listed at $149–$299 a month depending on dose; the pen is $349 for most doses after the introductory offer. Which is right for you is a prescriber decision, not a price decision.

Do telehealth GLP-1 prices include the medication?

Sometimes, sometimes not. Many programs bill the prescriber membership separately from the medicine. Ask for both numbers, in writing.

Does a self-pay GLP-1 count toward my deductible?

Generally no. Payments made outside your insurance usually don't count toward your deductible or out-of-pocket maximum. Ask your plan.

Quick tips

  • The drug price and the prescriber or membership fee are often separate charges, so get both in writing.
