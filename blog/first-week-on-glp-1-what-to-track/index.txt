1b:T742,<p>The first week on a GLP-1 medicine can look different for different people. Some notice appetite changes or digestive symptoms; others notice very little at first. There is no single feeling that proves the medicine is working, and social-media timelines are not a personal schedule. Follow the instructions for your specific product and rely on your prescriber for individualized advice.</p>
<p>It also helps to know that most of these medicines start at a low dose that is increased gradually. For example, the US labels for Wegovy injection and Zepbound both begin with a starting dose for the first 4 weeks before stepping up. The starting dose is mainly there to help your body adjust, so a quiet first week is not a sign that something is wrong.</p>
<h2 id="make-the-routine-clear">Make the routine clear</h2>
<p>Know the name and form of your medicine (weekly injection or daily pill), the prescribed schedule, storage instructions, how to use the pen or device, and where to find the official patient information. If anything is unclear, ask the pharmacist or prescriber before guessing. Don&#39;t copy someone else&#39;s dose or move your dose day because another person&#39;s routine looks easier; ask your prescriber first. A simple log of <a href="/blog/how-to-track-glp-1-shots/">when you take each dose</a> makes follow-up much easier.</p>
<h2 id="track-symptoms-simply">Track symptoms simply</h2>
<p>A brief note about nausea, reflux, bowel changes, fluids, appetite, sleep and how symptoms affect your day may help you explain things at follow-up. You don&#39;t need to record every mouthful or weigh yourself several times a day. The goal is to notice useful patterns, not to turn treatment into a full-time job. Our guide to <a href="/blog/track-glp-1-side-effects/">tracking side effects for your clinician</a> has a simple format.</p>
1c:T5e5,<h2 id="keep-food-and-fluids-in-the-picture">Keep food and fluids in the picture</h2>
<p>If your appetite drops sharply, pay attention to whether you are eating and drinking enough to feel well. Vomiting or diarrhea can lead to dehydration, which the labels note can affect the kidneys. Contact your clinician promptly if you can&#39;t keep fluids down, have persistent vomiting, or notice signs of dehydration such as very dark urine, peeing much less than usual, or dizziness when standing. Seek urgent care for severe abdominal pain that doesn&#39;t go away (with or without vomiting), as that can be a sign of a serious problem such as pancreatitis or gallbladder disease. Feeling unwell is not proof that the medicine is working better.</p>
<h2 id="avoid-the-comparison-trap">Avoid the comparison trap</h2>
<p>People on different products, doses and stages of treatment can have very different experiences. A first-week weigh-in says little about longer-term response, and day-to-day weight naturally fluctuates. If weighing is part of your plan, look at the trend over weeks rather than any single number.</p>
<h2 id="keep-a-list-for-follow-up">Keep a list for follow-up</h2>
<p>Write down questions, side effects, supply or cost problems and anything that makes the routine difficult. Ask when your first review should happen and which outcomes matter most for you.</p>
<p>Your first week is for learning the routine and noticing how you feel—not for meeting a stranger&#39;s weight-loss target.</p>
