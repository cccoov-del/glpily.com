15:T1b30,<p><strong>Should you track your GLP-1 on paper, in a spreadsheet or in an app?</strong> Choose <strong>paper</strong> if you want simple and offline, a <strong>spreadsheet</strong> if you love custom charts and control, and an <strong>app</strong> if you need reminders and want everything in one place with little effort. Answer the six questions below and the right fit usually becomes obvious.</p>
<p>There is no prize for the fanciest system. In a long-running trial comparing paper and electronic self-monitoring for weight loss, how consistently people logged mattered more than which tool they used. So the goal here is the setup you will still be using in month four.</p>
<h2 id="the-6-question-decision-guide">The 6-question decision guide</h2>
<p>Go through these in order. Each answer nudges you toward one option. Tally them as you go.</p>
<h3 id="1-have-you-missed-or-nearly-missed-a-dose-day-in-the-last-two-months">1. Have you missed or nearly missed a dose day in the last two months?</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Yes →</strong> App (+2). Reminders are the single biggest thing paper and spreadsheets cannot do on their own. You <em>can</em> pair paper with a phone alarm, but that is two systems.</li>
<li><strong>No →</strong> No points. Your memory or routine is already doing the job.</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="2-do-you-want-to-see-charts-without-making-them-yourself">2. Do you want to see charts without making them yourself?</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Yes, automatically →</strong> App (+1).</li>
<li><strong>Yes, and I want to design them →</strong> Spreadsheet (+2).</li>
<li><strong>Not really →</strong> Paper (+1).</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="3-how-do-you-feel-about-your-health-data-being-stored-by-a-company">3. How do you feel about your health data being stored by a company?</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Uneasy →</strong> Paper (+2). Nothing leaves your house.</li>
<li><strong>Fine if I control the file →</strong> Spreadsheet (+1). On your own computer, or in a cloud drive you manage.</li>
<li><strong>Fine if the policy is clear →</strong> App (+1). Our <a href="/blog/glp-1-app-privacy-policy-checklist/">privacy policy checklist</a> shows what &quot;clear&quot; looks like.</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="4-where-are-you-when-you-take-your-shot-or-pill">4. Where are you when you take your shot or pill?</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Same place every week, with a drawer nearby →</strong> Paper (+1).</li>
<li><strong>Usually at a computer →</strong> Spreadsheet (+1).</li>
<li><strong>All over the place: work, travel, the couch →</strong> App (+1). Your phone is with you; your notebook may not be.</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="5-do-you-track-more-than-doses-and-weight">5. Do you track more than doses and weight?</h3>
<p>Food, water, symptoms, measurements, photos, mood?</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Yes, several →</strong> App (+2). Paper gets crowded, and spreadsheets get many tabs.</li>
<li><strong>Just one or two things →</strong> Paper or spreadsheet (+1 each).</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="6-will-you-share-this-with-your-prescriber">6. Will you share this with your prescriber?</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Yes, regularly →</strong> App or spreadsheet (+1 each). Both can produce a tidy summary; paper needs photocopying or photos.</li>
<li><strong>Rarely →</strong> No points.</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="your-result">Your result</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Paper wins:</strong> you value privacy and simplicity, and your routine is steady.</li>
<li><strong>Spreadsheet wins:</strong> you like control, custom views and owning the file.</li>
<li><strong>App wins:</strong> you want reminders, many kinds of tracking and less effort.</li>
<li><strong>A tie:</strong> consider a hybrid (below).</li>
</ul>

<p>Tips that save you later:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Use ISO dates (2026-10-20).</strong> They sort correctly and avoid month/day mix-ups.</li>
<li><strong>Always fill the Unit column.</strong> &quot;82&quot; means something very different in lb and kg.</li>
<li><strong>Copy the dose exactly as your prescription says.</strong> Do not calculate or convert. If anything about your dose is unclear, ask your pharmacist.</li>
<li><strong>Check the sharing setting.</strong> In Google Drive, &quot;Anyone with the link&quot; means anyone on the internet with the link can open the file without signing in. For a health log, keep it on &quot;Restricted,&quot; which allows only the people you add.</li>
<li><strong>Back it up.</strong> A spreadsheet on one laptop is one spilled coffee from gone.</li>
</ul>
<p>The trade-off: you are the developer now. Reminders, charts and formulas are your job, and a broken formula will quietly give you wrong trends.</p>

<h2 id="can-you-mix-them">Can you mix them?</h2>
<p>Hybrids are common and sensible:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>App + paper:</strong> app for doses, reminders and weight; notebook for feelings and questions.</li>
<li><strong>App + spreadsheet:</strong> app for daily logging; export to a spreadsheet monthly for your own analysis.</li>
<li><strong>Paper + phone alarm:</strong> the low-tech way to get reminders.</li>
</ul>
<p>The one rule for hybrids: give each kind of data <strong>one home</strong>. If side effects end up half in a notebook and half in an app, neither will tell the full story. For a full list of what is worth tracking, see <a href="/blog/what-to-track-on-glp-1/">what to track on a GLP-1</a>.</p>
<h2 id="common-questions">Common questions</h2>
<h3 id="is-a-spreadsheet-more-private-than-an-app">Is a spreadsheet more private than an app?</h3>
<p>It can be, if the file stays on your device or in a cloud folder with restricted sharing. A spreadsheet shared with &quot;anyone with the link&quot; can be less private than a well-run app.</p>
<h3 id="can-i-switch-from-paper-to-an-app-later">Can I switch from paper to an app later?</h3>
<p>Yes. You will probably start fresh in the app, so keep the paper log as your record of the earlier months and bring both to appointments.</p>
<h3 id="what-is-the-minimum-i-should-track">What is the minimum I should track?</h3>
<p>Dose dates (and sites, for injections), any missed doses, and side effects with dates. Weight is useful if you want to see trends, but optional.</p>
<h3 id="how-long-should-i-try-a-system-before-switching">How long should I try a system before switching?</h3>
<p>Two to three weeks is usually enough to know whether it fits your routine.</p>
