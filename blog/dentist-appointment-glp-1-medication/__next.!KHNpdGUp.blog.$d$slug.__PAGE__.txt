1a:T15ea,<p>Do you need to tell your dentist you take a GLP-1? Yes. Put it on the health-history form like any other medicine, and say it out loud if you are having sedation, because the drug labels warn that slower stomach emptying matters for deep sedation and general anesthesia. For a regular cleaning, it mainly helps the dentist understand dry mouth, reflux and enamel changes.</p>
<p>Here is the scene most people want to avoid: you are already reclined, bib on, mouth open, and the hygienist asks &quot;Any new medications?&quot; You say &quot;mm-hmm&quot; around the suction tube. Let&#39;s do it at check-in instead.</p>
<h2 id="why-does-your-dentist-care-about-a-glp-1">Why does your dentist care about a GLP-1?</h2>
<p>Three reasons, from most to least urgent:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Sedation and anesthesia.</strong> GLP-1 medicines slow stomach emptying. The Wegovy and Zepbound labels both include a warning about <strong>pulmonary aspiration during general anesthesia or deep sedation</strong>: rare post-marketing reports of people who had food left in the stomach despite following fasting rules. The labels tell patients to inform healthcare providers before any planned surgery or procedure.</li>
<li><strong>Your mouth environment.</strong> Nausea, vomiting and reflux can lower mouth pH and wear enamel, and dry mouth reduces saliva&#39;s protection. The American Dental Association&#39;s news coverage of a SmileCon 2025 session urged dental teams to ask about these medicines at intake for exactly this reason.</li>
<li><strong>Everything connected.</strong> Diabetes medicines, healing, implants and weight-related changes all shape a dental plan.</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="is-dental-sedation-different-on-a-glp-1">Is dental sedation different on a GLP-1?</h2>
<p>It can be. This is the part to raise early, ideally when the procedure is booked, not the morning of.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Local anesthetic only (numbing shots).</strong> The label warnings are about general anesthesia and deep sedation, which affect your airway reflexes. Routine numbing is a different situation, but still list your medicines.</li>
<li><strong>Deep sedation or general anesthesia</strong> (some oral surgery, wisdom teeth, extensive work). This is where the label warning and anesthesia guidance apply.</li>
</ul>
<p>In 2024, five societies, including the American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) and the American Gastroenterological Association, published joint guidance. The ASA&#39;s patient page summarizes it this way: most people can keep taking their GLP-1, decisions should involve the anesthesia provider, the surgeon or dentist, and your prescriber, and you will likely be asked to avoid solid food for 24 hours, with clear liquids until two hours before. Symptoms like nausea, vomiting, stomach pain or constipation may mean the procedure should wait, as can the early dose-escalation weeks.</p>
<p>The ASA page also points out something many people do not know: full-stomach airway precautions are available with general anesthesia, not with sedation. So for higher-risk patients, an anesthesiologist may prefer general anesthesia, check the stomach with ultrasound on the day, or reschedule. A 2025 review in <em>Anesthesia Progress</em>, a dental anesthesia journal, applied the same guidance to office-based dental sedation and stressed shared decision-making between the sedation provider, prescriber and patient.</p>
<p><strong>What that means for you:</strong> do not stop or skip your medicine based on a dental office&#39;s general instructions or an online post. Ask the dentist or oral surgeon, &quot;Who is coordinating my GLP-1 plan with my prescriber?&quot; and get the answer in writing. Our longer guide on <a href="/blog/glp-1-before-surgery-anesthesia-team/">GLP-1s before surgery and anesthesia</a> walks through that conversation.</p>

2e:Teb1,<h2 id="scheduling-tips-that-make-the-day-easier">Scheduling tips that make the day easier</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Avoid the first days after a dose increase</strong> for long or elective appointments if you know that is when nausea peaks for you. Your own symptom log is the best guide.</li>
<li><strong>If you have diabetes and use insulin or a sulfonylurea,</strong> ask your prescriber how to handle fasting before a sedation appointment. The Wegovy label notes a higher low-blood-sugar risk when GLP-1s are combined with these medicines.</li>
<li><strong>Morning appointments</strong> can be easier if you are asked to fast, so you are not hungry all day.</li>
<li><strong>Bring water</strong> for after, and plan soft, protein-rich food you can manage with a numb mouth.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="what-your-dentist-may-check-or-suggest">What your dentist may check or suggest</h2>
<p>Based on ADA guidance and recent dental reviews, a dentist may:</p>
<ul>
<li>Look for enamel wear on the inner and chewing surfaces, a classic sign of acid from reflux or vomiting</li>
<li>Ask about dry mouth and suggest sugar-free gum, saliva substitutes, or prescription-strength fluoride toothpaste</li>
<li>Keep an eye on gums and, if you have implants, on the bone around them; the ADA session noted rapid weight loss may raise bone resorption concerns</li>
<li>Prefer non-opioid pain relief when possible after a procedure, since opioids can worsen constipation and other stomach side effects</li>
<li>If you wear braces or aligners, know that early research suggests these medicines may slow tooth movement in adults</li>
</ul>
<p>For the research behind these points, see <a href="/blog/glp-1-oral-health-research/">what scientists are finding about GLP-1s and oral health</a>. For daily habits, see <a href="/blog/dry-mouth-bad-breath-glp-1/">dry mouth and bad breath on GLP-1</a>.</p>

<h2 id="common-questions">Common questions</h2>
<h3 id="do-i-need-to-stop-ozempic-or-wegovy-before-dental-work">Do I need to stop Ozempic or Wegovy before dental work?</h3>
<p>For most routine dental visits, nothing in the label asks you to stop. For deep sedation or general anesthesia, the 2024 multi-society guidance says most people can continue, with an individual plan made by the anesthesia provider, dentist or surgeon, and prescriber. Do not stop on your own.</p>
<h3 id="is-it-safe-to-get-a-numbing-shot-on-a-glp-1">Is it safe to get a numbing shot on a GLP-1?</h3>
<p>The label warnings focus on general anesthesia and deep sedation, not local numbing. Still list your GLP-1 on your health history, along with every other medicine, so the dentist has the full picture.</p>
<h3 id="why-does-my-dentist-ask-about-reflux-and-vomiting">Why does my dentist ask about reflux and vomiting?</h3>
<p>Stomach acid softens enamel, and lost enamel does not grow back. Knowing about reflux or vomiting helps your dentist spot early erosion and suggest protection like fluoride.</p>
<h3 id="should-i-tell-my-dentist-if-my-glp-1-is-compounded">Should I tell my dentist if my GLP-1 is compounded?</h3>
<p>Yes. Say where it comes from and the exact product. The ADA has raised concerns about fraudulent and improperly compounded GLP-1 products, and your dentist may want to note it.</p>
