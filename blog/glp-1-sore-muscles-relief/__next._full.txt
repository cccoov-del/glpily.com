15:T1a1b,<p>Mild muscle soreness is a normal part of easing into movement, especially for adults starting or restarting exercise while on GLP-1 medications. The right mix of gentle stretches, steady hydration, and smart protein choices can help keep you comfortable—and support your progress as you build activity into your routine.</p>
<h2 id="why-muscle-soreness-happens-on-glp-1s">Why Muscle Soreness Happens on GLP-1s</h2>
<p>GLP-1 receptor agonists—like semaglutide, liraglutide, and tirzepatide—help support weight loss, but they can also lead to reductions in both fat and lean muscle mass, particularly early in treatment or during dose increases, according to the FDA label for tirzepatide. This means your muscles might feel unfamiliar levels of fatigue or soreness as you become more active, even with gentle movement (<a href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2024/215256s011lbl.pdf?aid=coachcandicehall" target="_blank" rel="noopener">FDA label</a>).</p>
<p>It’s also common for people to unintentionally move less after starting GLP-1 therapy. In a 2026 retrospective study of nearly 2,000 adults with obesity, moderate-to-vigorous activity and daily steps both declined after beginning GLP-1 medicines (<a href="https://www.endocrine.org/news-and-advocacy/news-room/2026/maharjan-press-release-endo-2026" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Endocrine Society, 2026</a>). That makes gentle encouragement and practical support even more important.</p>
<h2 id="the-gentle-start-movement-without-intimidation">The Gentle Start: Movement Without Intimidation</h2>
<p>If you’re just getting started (or starting again), your body may need a little extra kindness. Consensus guidelines recommend beginning with light, low-impact activity—like 10–15 minutes of walking or cycling per day—and gradually increasing as you feel able (<a href="https://health.clevelandclinic.org/exercise-for-glp-1-use" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Cleveland Clinic</a>). Yoga, Pilates, and dedicated stretching routines are excellent entry points: they help prime muscles, reduce stress, and support flexibility without overwhelming your system.</p>
<p>If you’d like, check out our <a href="/blog/glp-1-exercise-routines/">GLP-1 exercise routines guide</a> for more gentle starting points.</p>
<h3 id="building-up-safely">Building Up Safely</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Start small:</strong> Gentle stretching, easy walks, or light stationary cycling are ideal for the first weeks, especially during dose changes.</li>
<li><strong>Listen to your body:</strong> If soreness, nausea, or dizziness appear, pause and allow time to recover. For severe or persistent symptoms, speak with your clinician.</li>
<li><strong>Increase slowly:</strong> As you adapt, gradually add repetitions, sets, or light resistance bands to strengthen muscles. Even 4,000–6,000 steps per day offers health benefits if you can’t yet meet higher activity targets (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13258902" target="_blank" rel="noopener">PMC13258902</a>).</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="hydration-quiet-but-crucial">Hydration: Quiet but Crucial</h2>
<p>GLP-1 medicines often reduce thirst, which can increase your risk of dehydration—especially when you’re moving more or if you experience GI side effects like nausea. Adequate hydration supports muscle function, helps clear waste products, and can ease symptoms like fatigue or constipation (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13258902" target="_blank" rel="noopener">PMC13258902</a>).</p>
<p><strong>How much?</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Aim for 1.5–2.5 liters (about 50–85 oz) of fluids daily, or higher (up to 2–4 liters) if you’re active or live in a hot climate. Individual needs vary, and it’s best to sip water between meals to avoid fullness and GI upset (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12768930" target="_blank" rel="noopener">PMC12768930</a>).</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Practical hydration tips:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Keep a water bottle nearby and use reminders or bottles with timers to prompt regular sips.</li>
<li>If water is unappealing, try herbal teas, flavored water, or low-sugar electrolyte drinks.</li>
<li>Hydrate before, during, and after movement sessions—even gentle ones.</li>
</ul>
<p>For more on hydration, see <a href="/blog/hydration-on-glp-1/">hydration on GLP-1</a> and <a href="/blog/hydration-at-work-glp-1/">hydration at work</a>.</p>
<h2 id="protein-support-for-soreness-and-strength">Protein: Support for Soreness and Strength</h2>
<p>When your appetite is smaller (a common GLP-1 effect), it can be tough to get enough protein—yet protein is key for muscle repair and soreness relief. During active weight loss, a daily protein goal of 1.2–1.6 grams per kilogram of body weight (typically 80–120 grams per day) is commonly recommended for adults on GLP-1 therapy (<a href="https://store.mayoclinic.com/education/glp-1-medications-and-muscle-loss-what-to-know-about-nutrition-and-supplements/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Mayo Clinic</a>, <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12264624/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">PMC12264624</a>).</p>
<p><strong>Simple strategies:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Prioritize protein first</strong> in your meal: eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, tofu, beans, or lean meats are great options.</li>
<li><strong>Aim for 20–30 grams of protein per meal</strong>—spreading intake throughout the day is more effective than one large serving (<a href="https://apnews.com/article/41e4c84a7fed9586057b9b49fc4738dc" target="_blank" rel="noopener">AP News</a>).</li>
<li><strong>Try high-protein beverages or shakes</strong> if you struggle to meet targets with solid food (<a href="https://www.urmc.rochester.edu/news/publications/health-matters/why-nutrition-counseling-is-vital-with-glp-1-medication" target="_blank" rel="noopener">URMC</a>).</li>
<li><strong>Smaller, frequent meals</strong> may be easier to tolerate if your appetite is low (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13258902" target="_blank" rel="noopener">PMC13258902</a>).</li>
</ul>
<p>Not sure what to try? Our <a href="/blog/easy-at-home-protein-snacks-glp-1/">easy at-home protein snacks guide</a> and <a href="/blog/high-protein-meals-glp-1/">high-protein meals for GLP-1</a> have practical ideas.</p>
<p><strong>A key point:</strong> Higher protein alone isn’t enough to preserve muscle—you’ll get the best results when it’s paired with resistance or strength training at least three times per week (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12264624/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">PMC12264624</a>).</p>

1c:Tec1,<h2 id="gentle-stretches-and-recovery-moves">Gentle Stretches and Recovery Moves</h2>
<p>Stretching isn’t just for yogis. Gentle flexibility work can ease mild soreness and help prevent future discomfort. Yoga, Pilates, or simple stretching routines on rest days can keep muscles supple and support your return to activity (<a href="https://health.clevelandclinic.org/exercise-for-glp-1-use" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Cleveland Clinic</a>).</p>
<p><strong>Try these approaches:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Dynamic stretching</strong> before activity: arm circles, gentle leg swings, or cat-cow stretches.</li>
<li><strong>Static stretching</strong> after movement: hold each stretch for 15–30 seconds, focusing on major muscle groups you used.</li>
<li><strong>Active recovery days:</strong> on days when you’re sore, opt for light movement instead of total rest—think gentle walking or a beginner yoga video.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="relieving-mild-soreness-everyday-tips">Relieving Mild Soreness: Everyday Tips</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Hydrate regularly</strong>—even minor dehydration can intensify soreness.</li>
<li><strong>Nourish with protein</strong>, especially after activity.</li>
<li><strong>Use gentle movement</strong> to promote circulation and healing.</li>
<li><strong>Respect rest needs:</strong> quality sleep and occasional rest days help your body adapt.</li>
<li><strong>Monitor your signals:</strong> If you notice severe pain, persistent swelling, breathlessness, or dizziness, stop and consult your healthcare provider.</li>
</ul>
<p>You might find it helpful to track your symptoms and activity in an app like GLPily, which can make it easier to discuss patterns with your clinician if soreness or fatigue becomes a concern.</p>
<h2 id="what-s-still-being-studied">What’s Still Being Studied</h2>
<p>Researchers are currently testing whether combining resistance exercise with higher protein intake preserves muscle mass and function during GLP-1 therapy; results from the LEAN-PREP randomized trial are pending (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42020128/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">PubMed 2024</a>). The best way to personalize protein targets—by actual, adjusted, or lean body weight—is also still being debated. Direct evidence on how best to relieve muscle soreness for new movers on GLP-1s is limited, so much of the advice here is based on broader clinical consensus and studies in general populations.</p>
