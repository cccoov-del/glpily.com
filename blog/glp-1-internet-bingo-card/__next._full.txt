17:T1bba,<p>This GLP-1 bingo card is a joke with a purpose. It&#39;s a printable grid of 25 internet tropes you&#39;ve almost certainly seen in GLP-1 posts, comments and ads. This piece is lighthearted opinion, not data: we&#39;re not claiming how often any square shows up. Under the card you&#39;ll find the real fact behind the squares, so every &quot;BINGO!&quot; comes with a tiny upgrade to your skepticism.</p>
<p>The golden rule: you mark <strong>tropes</strong>, never <strong>people</strong>. Someone sharing their side effects or their journey isn&#39;t a square. A gold badge that says &quot;FDA Registered&quot; absolutely is.</p>
<h2 id="how-to-play-glp-1-internet-bingo">How to play GLP-1 internet bingo</h2>
<ol>
<li>Print or screenshot the card below.</li>
<li>Open your feed, your group chat or the comments under any GLP-1 headline.</li>
<li>Mark a square when you spot the trope in the wild.</li>
<li>Five in a row (across, down or diagonal) wins. The center square is free, because someone has definitely already called a different drug &quot;Ozempic&quot; today.</li>
<li>Blackout means you&#39;ve been scrolling too long. Close the app and go refill your water bottle.</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="the-cheat-sheet-the-real-fact-behind-the-squares">The cheat sheet: the real fact behind the squares</h2>
<p>You don&#39;t need these to win, but they make you the most useful person in the comments.</p>
<p><strong>&quot;FDA Registered!&quot; badge.</strong> Federal rules say registering a facility or listing a drug does <em>not</em> mean FDA approved it. Implying approval from registration counts as misbranding. Our <a href="/blog/spot-glp-1-misinformation-quiz/">misinformation quiz</a> has more badge traps.</p>
<p><strong>FREE square: every GLP-1 called &quot;Ozempic.&quot;</strong> Ozempic is semaglutide for adults with type 2 diabetes. The weight-management semaglutide brand is Wegovy, and tirzepatide is Mounjaro or Zepbound. As of 2026 there are even Ozempic <em>tablets</em>. Nicknames are fine at brunch, less fine in medical advice.</p>
<p><strong>&quot;Research use only&quot; vial with a dosing chart.</strong> FDA has warned companies selling semaglutide, tirzepatide and retatrutide labeled &quot;for research purposes&quot; but marketed to consumers with dosing instructions. The agency says their quality is unknown.</p>
<p><strong>&quot;Same as Wegovy, but cheaper!&quot;</strong> Compounded drugs aren&#39;t FDA-approved, and FDA says salt forms like semaglutide sodium have no lawful basis for compounding. &quot;Same as&quot; is doing a lot of work in that sentence.</p>
<p><strong>Discount code + &quot;#collab.&quot;</strong> The FTC says vague tags like &quot;collab&quot; or &quot;ambassador&quot; alone aren&#39;t clear disclosures. &quot;Ad&quot; or &quot;sponsored,&quot; placed where you can&#39;t miss it, is the standard.</p>
<p><strong>&quot;Nature&#39;s Ozempic&quot; supplement.</strong> FDA doesn&#39;t approve supplements before they&#39;re sold, and a product marketed to treat a disease is legally a drug.</p>
<p><strong>20% risk drop, no baseline.</strong> The famous SELECT result was a 20% <em>relative</em> reduction: 6.5% vs 8.0% had a major heart event over about 40 months. Same truth, very different vibe. More in <a href="/blog/relative-vs-absolute-risk-glp-1/">relative vs absolute risk</a>.</p>
<p><strong>&quot;Everyone loses 15%.&quot;</strong> On the Wegovy label, the trial average was −14.9%, and 47.9% of people reached 15% or more. An average isn&#39;t a guarantee.</p>
<p><strong>&quot;Hundreds of reports!&quot; as proof.</strong> FDA says a report to its adverse event system doesn&#39;t establish that the drug caused the event.</p>
<p><strong>Recycled 2023 headline.</strong> Classic example: in January 2026 FDA asked for the suicidal-ideation warning to be <em>removed</em> from the Saxenda, Wegovy and Zepbound labels after reviewing 91 trials. Check the date before you panic.</p>
<p><strong>&quot;I stopped and gained it ALL back&quot; vs &quot;gained NOTHING.&quot;</strong> Both are one person&#39;s story. In the STEP 1 extension, the average was about two-thirds regained within a year of stopping, with wide individual variation.</p>
<p><strong>Before-and-after with no dates.</strong> No timeline, no dose, no context. See <a href="/blog/before-after-glp-1-stories-limitations/">why before-and-after stories don&#39;t tell the whole story</a>.</p>
<p><strong>A brand-new drug &quot;available now&quot; that isn&#39;t approved.</strong> Retatrutide is still investigational. If someone is selling it to you, they&#39;re selling something outside the approved system.</p>

20:T9a9,<h2 id="house-rules-for-keeping-it-funny">House rules for keeping it funny</h2>
<p>Bingo works because it laughs at <em>patterns</em>: overconfidence, recycled headlines, badges that mean nothing. It stops being funny when it targets a person. So:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Don&#39;t screenshot real people&#39;s posts</strong> to &quot;prove&quot; a square. Describe the trope instead.</li>
<li><strong>Side effects aren&#39;t tropes.</strong> Someone saying they feel awful deserves a kind reply, not a dabber.</li>
<li><strong>Bodies are never squares.</strong> No square on this card is about how anyone looks.</li>
<li><strong>Correct gently.</strong> If you drop a fact from the cheat sheet in the comments, link the source and skip the dunk.</li>
</ul>
<p>Want the version without the comment-section chaos? The <a href="/blog/enjoy-glp-1-stories-without-comparison/">guide to enjoying GLP-1 stories without comparison</a> is the calmer cousin of this card.</p>
<h2 id="make-your-own-card">Make your own card</h2>
<p>Swap in squares from your corner of the internet. A few ideas to get you started: &quot;a &#39;what I eat in a day&#39; with no portions,&quot; &quot;the influencer who &#39;can&#39;t say the brand,&#39;&quot; and &quot;someone discovers protein.&quot; Keep the rules: patterns, not people. Facts, not dunks.</p>
<p>If you&#39;re on treatment yourself, the best antidote to internet noise is your own record. A note of your dose, your weight trend and how you actually feel beats any viral claim. GLPily keeps those in one place, which makes the comments section a lot easier to scroll past.</p>
<h2 id="common-questions">Common questions</h2>
<h3 id="is-this-bingo-card-making-fun-of-people-who-take-glp-1s">Is this bingo card making fun of people who take GLP-1s?</h3>
<p>No. It makes fun of misleading <em>content</em>: badges, spin and recycled headlines. The house rules ban squares about bodies, side effects or real people.</p>
