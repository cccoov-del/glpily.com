Still Hungry on a GLP-1? Appetite Reduction Is Not Identical for Everyone

Still hungry on a GLP-1? Appetite effects vary from person to person. Explore possible reasons and questions to ask your clinician.

♡By GLPily Editorial Team

Hunger is human ♡

"I thought I'd stop feeling hungry" is one of the most common expectations people bring to GLP-1 treatment. But these medicines don't switch appetite off the same way for everyone, and hunger runs on more than one biological pathway. Sleep, stress, meal timing, what and how much you eat, activity, hormones, health conditions and other medicines can all nudge how hungry you feel.

Hunger isn't a pass-or-fail test

Some people notice they fill up sooner or that the constant food noise quiets down; others notice subtler changes. Appetite can shift across the day and over the course of treatment. One hungry evening doesn't prove the medicine has stopped working. And having almost no appetite isn't automatically the "ideal" outcome either—not if it makes it hard to eat enough to stay nourished.

What could be part of the picture?

  • Where you are in dosing. Many medicines start at a low dose and step up gradually. Wegovy, for example, starts at 0.25 mg weekly and Zepbound at 2.5 mg weekly, each for 4 weeks, before increasing under the prescriber's direction.
  • Why it was prescribed. Some people take a GLP-1 mainly for type 2 diabetes or another approved use rather than weight, and treatment goals may differ.
  • Individual biology. A 2026 systematic review of low weight-loss response linked smaller responses with factors such as higher HbA1c and insulin resistance, genetics, and how consistently and how long treatment is taken. These are group-level patterns that can't explain any one person's appetite.
  • Everyday factors. Short sleep, stress, long gaps between meals and low-protein or low-fiber meals can all leave people hungrier. Our high-protein meal ideas might help with satisfying options.

What's worth noticing?

Rather than trying to suppress hunger at all costs, notice when it shows up, whether it feels like physical hunger or more like a craving for a specific food, how satisfying your meals are, and whether you're eating regularly. Write down your exact medicine, dose and start dates for your clinician. If you have diabetes, bring along the glucose readings you already track and mention any symptoms that could suggest low blood sugar, such as shakiness or sweating.

The conversation to have

Ask your prescriber how your response is being judged, whether you're at the intended stage of your plan, and what other causes of persistent hunger are worth checking. Please don't increase your dose, use someone else's medicine or add appetite suppressants without medical guidance. If hunger comes with intense thirst, frequent urination, shakiness, confusion or other worrying symptoms, contact a clinician promptly.

The goal isn't to erase normal hunger. It's to check whether treatment is meeting its health goals while keeping your eating, hydration and wellbeing sustainable.
