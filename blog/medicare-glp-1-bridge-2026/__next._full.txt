17:T15f3,<p>The Medicare GLP-1 Bridge is a temporary federal program that lets eligible people with Medicare drug coverage get certain GLP-1 medicines for weight management for a <strong>$50 copay per one-month supply</strong>. It started <strong>July 1, 2026</strong>, runs through <strong>December 31, 2027</strong>, and covers Wegovy (injection or tablet), Foundayo, and the Zepbound KwikPen. Nothing else.</p>
<p>That&#39;s the short version. The longer version has some fine print, and fine print is where most of the &quot;wait, why did the pharmacy say that?&quot; moments come from. Let&#39;s go through it.</p>
<h2 id="what-is-the-medicare-glp-1-bridge">What is the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge?</h2>
<p>For years the standard Medicare answer to &quot;will Part D pay for my weight-loss medicine?&quot; was no. Part D plans generally can&#39;t cover drugs used only for weight loss. Medicare plans <em>could</em> cover some of these medicines for other approved uses, like type 2 diabetes, but not for weight management by itself.</p>
<p>The Bridge works around that. CMS calls it a short-term <strong>demonstration</strong>, and it runs <em>outside</em> the normal Part D benefit. In practice that means:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Your Part D plan isn&#39;t paying.</strong> A single central processor contracted by CMS handles prior authorization, claims and pharmacy payment for 2026. Your plan doesn&#39;t carry the cost, and it didn&#39;t have to opt in.</li>
<li><strong>It isn&#39;t a new plan.</strong> You don&#39;t sign up for &quot;the Bridge&quot; the way you&#39;d pick a Part D plan. Your prescriber sends a prescription, the pharmacy runs a claim, and a prior authorization decides whether you qualify.</li>
<li><strong>It&#39;s nationwide.</strong> Medicare.gov says it&#39;s available in all states and U.S. territories.</li>
</ul>
<p>Think of it as a side door Medicare built next to Part D, with its own rules on the door.</p>
<h2 id="which-drugs-does-the-medicare-glp-1-bridge-cover">Which drugs does the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge cover?</h2>
<p>There are only three brands, and for one of them only one format counts.</p>

<p>Some details worth knowing:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Foundayo was added April 6, 2026</strong>, before the program started. CMS says the product list may be updated, so check before assuming anything changes.</li>
<li><strong>The Zepbound split confuses people.</strong> If your prescription is for Zepbound vials, the Bridge won&#39;t pay for it. Ask your prescriber whether the KwikPen is an option, but don&#39;t switch formats on your own.</li>
<li><strong>Pen needles aren&#39;t included.</strong> Multi-dose pens like the KwikPen use separate pen needles, and CMS tells pharmacies not to bill them to the Bridge.</li>
<li><strong>Coverage is for weight management only.</strong> If you take a GLP-1 for type 2 diabetes, that goes through your regular Part D plan.</li>
</ul>
<p>New to the pills? Our guides to <a href="/blog/foundayo-orforglipron-guide/">Foundayo</a> and <a href="/blog/oral-wegovy-vs-injection/">oral vs injectable Wegovy</a> cover the differences.</p>
<h2 id="how-much-does-the-medicare-glp-1-bridge-cost">How much does the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge cost?</h2>
<p>The copay is <strong>$50 per one-month supply</strong> (28 or 30 days, depending on the drug), paid at the pharmacy. That number is simple. What it <em>doesn&#39;t</em> do takes a bit more explaining:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>The Part D deductible doesn&#39;t apply.</strong> You don&#39;t have to meet it first.</li>
<li><strong>The $50 doesn&#39;t count toward your Part D deductible or yearly out-of-pocket limit.</strong> It won&#39;t show up on your Part D Explanation of Benefits either.</li>
<li><strong>Extra Help can&#39;t lower it.</strong> There&#39;s no low-income subsidy on Bridge claims.</li>
<li><strong>You can&#39;t spread it out</strong> with the Medicare Prescription Payment Plan.</li>
<li><strong>Coupons and discount cards can&#39;t be applied</strong>, whoever funds them.</li>
<li><strong>No 60- or 90-day fills.</strong> Every fill is one month. Pharmacies can process a vacation fill with a specific override, but there are no overrides for lost or stolen medicine.</li>
</ul>
<p>The yearly math is easy: 12 fills × $50 = <strong>$600 a year</strong>. For comparison, the manufacturers&#39; self-pay prices in October 2026 run from <strong>$299 to $699 a month</strong> for the Zepbound KwikPen depending on dose, and <strong>$349 a month</strong> for most Wegovy pen doses. At the lowest of those, a year of self-pay is over $3,500. So for someone who qualifies, $50 is a big drop. Those cash prices change, so look them up again before you compare.</p>
<p>One budgeting tip: since the $50 lives outside Part D, it won&#39;t help you reach your Part D out-of-pocket cap. Budget for it as a separate line, the same way you&#39;d plan for a <a href="/blog/full-cost-glp-1-treatment/">full GLP-1 treatment budget</a>.</p>

<p>The approval detail is good news. Medicare.gov says an approved prior authorization stays valid through <strong>December 31, 2027</strong>, including refills and dose changes, unless you switch to a different GLP-1. So a move from a starting dose to a higher one on the same drug doesn&#39;t send you back to square one. Switching from Wegovy to Zepbound does.</p>
<h2 id="what-the-bridge-does-not-do">What the Bridge does not do</h2>
<p>A few points that trip people up:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>It doesn&#39;t cover everyone on Medicare.</strong> It&#39;s tied to having Medicare drug coverage through certain plan types, plus specific BMI and health criteria. People who already get a GLP-1 through their Part D plan, or who have type 2 diabetes, moderate-to-severe sleep apnea or (noncirrhotic) fatty liver disease, are sent to their Part D plan instead. Our <a href="/blog/medicare-glp-1-bridge-eligibility-checklist/">Bridge eligibility checklist</a> goes through each criterion.</li>
<li><strong>There&#39;s no formal appeals process.</strong> CMS says plainly that the Bridge has none. If a request is denied because of wrong or missing information, your prescriber can <strong>resubmit</strong> the prior authorization form with corrected or new information.</li>
<li><strong>It isn&#39;t the same as Part D coverage.</strong> Some people may qualify for a GLP-1 through their regular plan for a different approved use. We compare the two paths in <a href="/blog/medicare-bridge-vs-part-d-glp-1/">Medicare Bridge or regular Part D?</a></li>
<li><strong>It isn&#39;t permanent.</strong> It&#39;s a demonstration with an end date.</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="what-happens-after-december-2027">What happens after December 2027?</h2>
<p>Nobody can say yet. CMS extended the Bridge partly to gather data on GLP-1 use and share it with Part D plans ahead of a possible BALANCE Model in Part D. Whether anything replaces the Bridge in 2028, and on what terms, hasn&#39;t been announced.</p>
<p>What you can do now is keep good records: the approval letter Medicare mails you, your fill dates, and which product and dose you&#39;re on. If the rules change, you and your prescriber will be planning from facts instead of memory. A shot or pill log with refill reminders, like the one in GLPily, makes the &quot;when did I last fill this?&quot; question quick to answer.</p>
<h2 id="common-questions">Common questions</h2>
<h3 id="do-i-have-to-enroll-in-the-medicare-glp-1-bridge-separately">Do I have to enroll in the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge separately?</h3>
<p>No separate plan enrollment. Your prescriber sends a prescription for a covered drug, the pharmacy submits the claim, and your prescriber completes a prior authorization when asked. If approved, you get a letter in the mail confirming coverage under the Bridge.</p>
<h3 id="does-the-50-copay-count-toward-my-part-d-out-of-pocket-maximum">Does the $50 copay count toward my Part D out-of-pocket maximum?</h3>
<p>No. Medicare.gov says it doesn&#39;t count toward your yearly Part D deductible or out-of-pocket limit, and it won&#39;t appear on your Part D Explanation of Benefits.</p>
<h3 id="can-i-use-a-manufacturer-coupon-to-lower-the-50">Can I use a manufacturer coupon to lower the $50?</h3>
<p>No. CMS tells pharmacies that coupons and discount programs can&#39;t be applied to Bridge claims, whatever their funding source.</p>
<h3 id="who-do-i-call-with-questions-about-the-bridge">Who do I call with questions about the Bridge?</h3>
<p>Call 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227; TTY 1-877-486-2048) for eligibility questions, prior authorization status or to report suspected fraud. Your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) can also help for free.</p>
