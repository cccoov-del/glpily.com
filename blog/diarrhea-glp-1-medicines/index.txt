18:Tadd,<p>Diarrhea is a recognized digestive side effect of several GLP-1 and related medicines. In the US prescribing information it was reported by 30% of adults on Wegovy 2.4 mg versus 16% on placebo, by 19–23% on Zepbound versus 8% on placebo, and by about 9% on Ozempic 0.5 mg or 1 mg versus 2% on placebo in its diabetes trials. It can appear when treatment begins or during dose escalation, but timing and duration vary. It may also come from an infection, another medicine, food intolerance or an unrelated illness, so do not assume every episode is medication-related, especially if symptoms are severe or different from your usual pattern.</p>
<h2 id="why-it-matters-beyond-inconvenience">Why it matters beyond inconvenience</h2>
<p>Frequent loose stools can lead to fluid and electrolyte loss. That matters especially if nausea or vomiting also makes drinking difficult, or if you have kidney disease or take medicines that affect fluid balance. US labels for these medicines warn that acute kidney injury has been reported, mostly in people whose gastrointestinal side effects led to dehydration (more in <a href="/blog/glp-1-dehydration-kidney-health/">dehydration and kidney health</a>). A single mild episode is different from ongoing diarrhea that prevents normal hydration or daily activity.</p>
<h2 id="what-to-track-and-discuss">What to track and discuss</h2>
<p>Record when diarrhea began, approximate frequency, whether you can drink and urinate normally, and any fever, blood, pain or vomiting. Note recent treatment changes and other medicines, but do not make dose changes based on your own log. A clinician or pharmacist can help assess whether supportive care or a treatment review is needed. Check with a professional before taking antidiarrheal medicines if you have fever, bloody stools or significant abdominal pain.</p>
<h2 id="when-to-seek-help">When to seek help</h2>
<p>Contact a healthcare professional promptly if diarrhea is persistent, severe, repeatedly interrupts sleep, or comes with reduced urination, marked dizziness, a dry mouth, inability to keep fluids down, blood in the stool, high fever or worsening abdominal pain. Seek urgent care for severe weakness, fainting, confusion, or signs of serious dehydration such as very little or no urine. Severe or persistent abdominal pain needs evaluation even if diarrhea is also present.</p>
<h2 id="the-practical-takeaway">The practical takeaway</h2>
<p>You do not need to &quot;push through&quot; symptoms to prove the medicine is working. Keep a clear, simple record and tell your prescriber if diarrhea is affecting hydration, nutrition or everyday life. The right next step depends on severity, duration, your medical history and the specific medicine, not on a general social-media tip.</p>
