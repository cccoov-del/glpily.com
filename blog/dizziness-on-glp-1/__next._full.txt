19:Tbe5,<p>Dizziness is a symptom, not a diagnosis. It is a listed side effect in US prescribing information: reported by 8% of adults on Wegovy 2.4 mg versus 4% on placebo, and by 4–5% on Zepbound versus 2%. If it appears during GLP-1 treatment, possible contributors include dehydration from vomiting or diarrhea, eating or drinking less, lower blood pressure, low blood glucose when combined with certain diabetes medicines, or an unrelated problem. The medicine may be part of the picture, but timing alone cannot establish the cause.</p>
<h2 id="notice-what-the-dizziness-feels-like">Notice what the dizziness feels like</h2>
<p>&quot;Dizzy&quot; can mean feeling faint, seeing spots after standing, a spinning sensation, unsteadiness or weakness. Note which description fits, how long it lasts, whether it appears after standing, and whether you have vomiting, diarrhea, a racing heart, headache or vision changes. If you feel faint, sit or lie somewhere safe and avoid driving until you feel well and understand what is happening.</p>
<h2 id="blood-pressure-hydration-and-food">Blood pressure, hydration and food</h2>
<p>Weight loss can lower blood pressure. In the Wegovy trials, low-blood-pressure-related reactions (including dizziness on standing) were reported in 1.3% of adults versus 0.4% on placebo, and fainting in 0.8% versus 0.2%; these were more common in people also taking blood pressure medicines. When appetite and thirst patterns change, some people also drink or eat less without noticing, and dehydration becomes more likely during a stomach bug (see <a href="/blog/glp-1-dehydration-kidney-health/">dehydration and kidney health</a>). Still, water is not a complete answer to every kind of dizziness; heart rhythm, infection, anemia, glucose levels and other medicines may need consideration. People with fluid restrictions or kidney or heart conditions should follow their clinician&#39;s individualized guidance. If you use insulin or a sulfonylurea, read about <a href="/blog/glp-1-medicines-low-blood-sugar/">low blood sugar on GLP-1 medicines</a>.</p>
<h2 id="when-to-seek-urgent-care">When to seek urgent care</h2>
<p>Call emergency services for dizziness with chest pain, trouble breathing, fainting, new weakness on one side, difficulty speaking, severe headache, confusion, or sudden vision loss. Contact a clinician promptly for repeated episodes, persistent vomiting, reduced urination, or possible low blood glucose. Do not change diabetes or blood pressure medicines yourself based on a single reading or symptom.</p>
<h2 id="make-the-next-conversation-specific">Make the next conversation specific</h2>
<p>Tell your clinician the medicine name, other prescriptions, timing of symptoms, whether it happens when standing, changes in fluid intake and any blood-glucose or blood-pressure readings you already take. They can decide whether an examination, medication review or tests are needed. Dizziness deserves assessment based on its features, not a blanket assumption that every GLP-1 symptom is harmless.</p>
