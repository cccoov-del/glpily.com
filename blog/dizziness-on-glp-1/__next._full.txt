1b:T9ee,<p>Feeling dizzy on GLP-1 is common enough to be listed on the label, and the usual contributors are dehydration from vomiting or diarrhea, eating or drinking less, lower blood pressure, or low blood glucose when the medicine is combined with certain diabetes medicines. The safest first step is to sit or lie down, sip fluids, note when it happens, and get urgent help if it comes with chest pain, weakness on one side or fainting. Dizziness is a symptom, not a diagnosis. It is a listed side effect in US prescribing information: reported by 8% of adults on Wegovy 2.4 mg versus 4% on placebo, and by 4–5% on Zepbound versus 2%. If it appears during GLP-1 treatment, possible contributors include dehydration from vomiting or diarrhea, eating or drinking less, lower blood pressure, low blood glucose when combined with certain diabetes medicines, or an unrelated problem. The medicine may be part of the picture, but timing alone cannot establish the cause.</p>
<h2 id="notice-what-the-dizziness-feels-like">Notice what the dizziness feels like</h2>
<p>GLP-1 dizziness is not one thing: &quot;dizzy&quot; can mean feeling faint, seeing spots after standing, a spinning sensation, unsteadiness or weakness. Note which description fits, how long it lasts, whether it appears after standing, and whether you have vomiting, diarrhea, a racing heart, headache or vision changes. If you feel faint, sit or lie somewhere safe and avoid driving until you feel well and understand what is happening.</p>
<h2 id="blood-pressure-hydration-and-food">Blood pressure, hydration and food</h2>
<p>Weight loss can lower blood pressure. In the Wegovy trials, low-blood-pressure-related reactions (including dizziness on standing) were reported in 1.3% of adults versus 0.4% on placebo, and fainting in 0.8% versus 0.2%; these were more common in people also taking blood pressure medicines. When appetite and thirst patterns change, some people also drink or eat less without noticing, and dehydration becomes more likely during a stomach bug (see <a href="/blog/glp-1-dehydration-kidney-health/">dehydration and kidney health</a>). Still, water is not a complete answer to every kind of dizziness; heart rhythm, infection, anemia, glucose levels and other medicines may need consideration. People with fluid restrictions or kidney or heart conditions should follow their clinician&#39;s individualized guidance. If you use insulin or a sulfonylurea, read about <a href="/blog/glp-1-medicines-low-blood-sugar/">low blood sugar on GLP-1 medicines</a>.</p>

25:Td9b,<h2 id="when-to-seek-urgent-care">When to seek urgent care</h2>
<p>Call emergency services for dizziness with chest pain, trouble breathing, fainting, new weakness on one side, difficulty speaking, severe headache, confusion, or sudden vision loss. Contact a clinician promptly for repeated episodes, persistent vomiting, reduced urination, or possible low blood glucose. Do not change diabetes or blood pressure medicines yourself based on a single reading or symptom.</p>
<h2 id="make-the-next-conversation-specific">Make the next conversation specific</h2>
<p>Tell your clinician the medicine name, other prescriptions, timing of symptoms, whether it happens when standing, changes in fluid intake and any blood-glucose or blood-pressure readings you already take. They can decide whether an examination, medication review or tests are needed. Dizziness deserves assessment based on its features, not a blanket assumption that every GLP-1 symptom is harmless.</p>
<h2 id="common-questions">Common questions</h2>
<h3 id="why-am-i-dizzy-on-glp-1">Why am I dizzy on GLP-1?</h3>
<p>There is rarely a single answer. Common contributors are dehydration from vomiting or diarrhea, eating or drinking less than usual, lower blood pressure as weight comes off, and low blood glucose if you also take insulin or a sulfonylurea. An unrelated problem such as infection, anemia or a heart rhythm issue is also possible, so timing alone cannot tell you the cause.</p>
<h3 id="is-dizziness-a-known-side-effect-of-wegovy-and-zepbound">Is dizziness a known side effect of Wegovy and Zepbound?</h3>
<p>Yes. In US prescribing information, dizziness was reported by 8% of adults on Wegovy 2.4 mg versus 4% on placebo, and by 4–5% on Zepbound versus 2%. That makes it a recognised side effect, but still one that deserves a look at its features rather than an assumption that it is harmless.</p>
<h3 id="why-do-i-feel-dizzy-when-i-stand-up-on-wegovy-or-ozempic">Why do I feel dizzy when I stand up on Wegovy or Ozempic?</h3>
<p>Weight loss can lower blood pressure, and standing up quickly is when a drop shows. In the Wegovy trials, low-blood-pressure-related reactions including dizziness on standing were reported in 1.3% of adults versus 0.4% on placebo, and fainting in 0.8% versus 0.2%; these were more common in people also taking blood pressure medicines. Note whether your episodes follow standing and tell your clinician, especially if you take a blood pressure medicine.</p>
<h3 id="will-drinking-more-water-fix-glp-1-dizziness">Will drinking more water fix GLP-1 dizziness?</h3>
<p>Sometimes, but not always. Drinking and eating less without noticing is a real contributor, and dehydration becomes more likely during a stomach bug. Water is not a complete answer, though: heart rhythm, infection, anemia, glucose levels and other medicines may need consideration, and people with fluid restrictions or kidney or heart conditions should follow their clinician&#39;s guidance.</p>
<h3 id="when-is-dizziness-on-glp-1-an-emergency">When is dizziness on GLP-1 an emergency?</h3>
<p>Call emergency services if dizziness comes with chest pain, trouble breathing, fainting, new weakness on one side, difficulty speaking, severe headache, confusion or sudden vision loss. Contact a clinician promptly for repeated episodes, persistent vomiting, reduced urination or possible low blood glucose.
