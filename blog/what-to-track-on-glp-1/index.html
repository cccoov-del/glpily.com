What to Track on GLP-1: A Practical Checklist

A practical GLP-1 tracking checklist for medication reminders, weight trends, meals, water and symptoms to discuss with your clinician.

♡By GLPily Editorial Team
Oct 11, 2026

Little notes, big picture ♡

  5. 5. Questions and wins

Tracking can help you remember details between appointments, but it should make life easier rather than turn every day into homework. You do not need to measure everything. Choose information that helps you follow your prescribed plan, understand your routine and have more useful conversations with your healthcare professional.

1. Medication schedule details

Record the information you have been instructed to track, dates of injections if applicable, and reminders tied to your prescribed schedule. Use your prescription label and official medication guide for instructions. A tracker is a reminder tool, not a source of dose changes or missed-dose advice. It can also help to keep an up-to-date list of all your medicines and supplements to share at appointments.

2. Weight trends, if tracking feels helpful

A single weigh-in can be affected by ordinary short-term variation. If you choose to track weight, look at trends over time instead of treating every change as a verdict. Some people prefer daily entries; others prefer less frequent tracking or a different progress measure. Choose a method that works for you and step back if it causes distress.

3. Food, fluids and appetite notes

You might note meals, foods that were easy to prepare, appetite changes or whether it was difficult to meet your needs. A brief note can be enough. Avoid turning a log into a strict list of foods you are “allowed” to eat.

4. Symptoms and how you feel

If you notice a symptom, record when it started, how often it occurs, how it affects your day and any questions you want to ask. Do not use a log to diagnose the cause. Contact your healthcare professional about persistent or concerning symptoms and follow official medication instructions for urgent warning signs. Get medical help right away for severe stomach pain that won't go away (it may spread to your back, with or without vomiting), swelling of the face, mouth or throat, trouble breathing or swallowing, or noticeably decreased urination. Your medication's official information lists the full set of warning signs.

5. Questions and wins

A journal can capture questions for your next appointment, routine barriers, energy or other changes that matter to you. Progress is not limited to a scale number. A more consistent routine, remembering an appointment question or finding a meal that works can be meaningful too.

A simple weekly check-in

  • Did I follow the schedule provided by my prescriber?
  • What was easy this week, and what was difficult?
  • Were there symptoms I should discuss?
  • What question do I want to bring to my next appointment?
  • Is the amount of tracking I do still helpful?

Your log belongs to you. Keep only what is useful, store it securely and share details with a clinician when relevant. If tracking becomes overwhelming, simplify it or discuss another approach with a professional.
