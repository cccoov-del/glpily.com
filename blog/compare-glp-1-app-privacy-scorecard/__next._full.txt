17:T1750,<p><strong>How do you compare two GLP-1 apps&#39; privacy quickly?</strong> Spend one minute on each app&#39;s store privacy label, then three minutes searching each privacy policy for eight key words (sell, advertis, train, share, retain, delete, export, and the names of AI or analytics companies). Mark each row clear, unclear or concerning on the scorecard below. That is enough to spot the meaningful differences without reading every paragraph.</p>
<p>This is not a legal audit, and it will not award anyone a &quot;safe&quot; badge. It is a fast way to see which app gives you straight answers about the data you are about to hand over: weight, doses, symptoms, meals and maybe photos.</p>
<h2 id="what-you-need">What you need</h2>
<ul>
<li>Two (or three) apps you are deciding between.</li>
<li>Their App Store or Google Play pages.</li>
<li>Their privacy policies, open in a browser.</li>
<li>The <strong>find in page</strong> function (Ctrl+F or Cmd+F on a computer; in phone browsers it is usually in the share or menu button).</li>
<li>A timer, if you enjoy a challenge.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="minute-1-the-store-label">Minute 1: The store label</h2>
<p>Open each app&#39;s store page and find the privacy section.</p>
<p><strong>On the App Store</strong>, scroll to <strong>App Privacy</strong>. Look for:</p>
<ul>
<li>Is there a <strong>Data Used to Track You</strong> box? Apple defines tracking as linking data from the app with other companies&#39; data for targeted advertising or ad measurement, or sharing it with a data broker.</li>
<li>Which purposes appear next to <strong>Health &amp; Fitness</strong> under <strong>Data Linked to You</strong>?</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>On Google Play</strong>, tap <strong>Data safety → See details</strong>. Look for:</p>
<ul>
<li>What is listed under <strong>Data shared</strong>.</li>
<li>Which purposes appear next to <strong>Health and fitness</strong> under <strong>Data collected</strong>, and whether it is marked optional.</li>
<li>Whether <strong>Security practices</strong> mention encryption in transit and a way to request deletion.</li>
</ul>
<p>Remember that both labels are written by the developer. Apple&#39;s label says outright that the information has not been verified by Apple. For a deeper walk-through, see our guides to <a href="/blog/apple-app-privacy-label-health-app/">Apple&#39;s App Privacy labels</a> and <a href="/blog/google-play-data-safety-health-app/">Google Play Data safety</a>.</p>
<h2 id="minutes-2-to-4-the-eight-word-search">Minutes 2 to 4: The eight-word search</h2>
<p>Open the privacy policy and search for each term. You are not reading the whole document, just the sentences around each hit.</p>

<p>Why these words? Because they map to the questions that matter most for health data. The FTC&#39;s case against GoodRx in 2023, for example, centered on prescription and health information shared with advertising platforms. &quot;Advertis&quot; is doing a lot of work in that search.</p>
<h2 id="minute-5-fill-in-the-scorecard">Minute 5: Fill in the scorecard</h2>
<p>Use three marks only:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>✓ Clear:</strong> the policy or label answers the question specifically.</li>
<li><strong>? Unclear:</strong> you could not find it, or the wording is vague.</li>
<li><strong>✗ Concerning:</strong> it answers, and you do not like the answer.</li>
</ul>

20:Tecd,<h2 id="how-should-you-read-the-results">How should you read the results?</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Mostly ✓ for both apps?</strong> Privacy is probably not your deciding factor. Choose on features and feel; our <a href="/blog/glpily-vs-shotsy-vs-meagain-2026/">7-day test-drive scorecard</a> helps with that.</li>
<li><strong>One app has more &quot;?&quot; marks?</strong> That is not proof of bad behavior. It may just be a vague policy. But it is a reason to email the developer before logging sensitive data.</li>
<li><strong>Any ✗ on advertising, selling or tracking?</strong> For health data, many people treat that as a deal-breaker.</li>
<li><strong>Store label and policy contradict each other?</strong> Ask which is correct. A good developer will fix whichever one is out of date.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="what-does-this-5-minute-method-miss">What does this 5-minute method miss?</h2>
<p>Speed has trade-offs. This method does not check:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Security in practice.</strong> Labels describe intentions, not how well systems hold up.</li>
<li><strong>What the app actually does on your phone.</strong> On iPhone, turning on <strong>App Privacy Report</strong> (Settings → Privacy &amp; Security) shows which domains each app contacts over 7 days.</li>
<li><strong>Legal coverage.</strong> Most consumer tracking apps are not covered by HIPAA. Our post on <a href="/blog/hipaa-health-apps-myths/">HIPAA and health apps myths</a> explains which rules apply instead.</li>
<li><strong>The finer points</strong> like breach notification, children&#39;s data or state-specific rights. For the full picture, use our <a href="/blog/glp-1-app-privacy-policy-checklist/">10-point privacy policy checklist</a>.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="re-run-it-when-apps-change">Re-run it when apps change</h2>
<p>Privacy answers are not permanent. Developers can update store labels at any time, and policies change when apps add new features. Re-run the five minutes when an app adds AI features, a new integration, or sends you a &quot;we&#39;ve updated our privacy policy&quot; notice.</p>
<h2 id="common-questions">Common questions</h2>
<h3 id="is-five-minutes-really-enough">Is five minutes really enough?</h3>
<p>Enough to compare and to spot red flags. Not enough to fully understand a policy. If an app passes the quick check and you plan to log a lot of sensitive data, the full checklist is worth fifteen more minutes.</p>
<h3 id="what-if-a-policy-uses-different-words-than-my-search-terms">What if a policy uses different words than my search terms?</h3>
<p>Try synonyms: &quot;disclose&quot; for share, &quot;keep&quot; or &quot;store&quot; for retain, &quot;erase&quot; or &quot;remove&quot; for delete, &quot;marketing&quot; for advertising.</p>
<h3 id="should-i-avoid-any-app-with-a-mark">Should I avoid any app with a &quot;?&quot; mark?</h3>
<p>Not automatically. Ask the developer. A quick, specific reply is a good sign; no reply tells you something too.</p>
<h3 id="do-privacy-labels-and-policies-get-checked-by-apple-or-google">Do privacy labels and policies get checked by Apple or Google?</h3>
<p>Both stores set the rules and definitions, but the developer is responsible for accuracy. Apple&#39;s label notes the information has not been verified by Apple, and Google says it cannot determine on its own how developers handle data.</p>
