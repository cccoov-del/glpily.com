15:Te38,<p>GLP-1 delayed gastric emptying means food, and the pills you swallow, sit in the stomach longer, so US labels say these medicines have the potential to change how oral medications are absorbed. Most people won&#39;t need changes; the ones to check are pills where timing and blood levels really matter (warfarin and thyroid hormone are the examples labels give), plus birth control pills if you take tirzepatide. Slower stomach emptying is part of how GLP-1 medicines help people feel full, and the details of what to watch differ by product.</p>
<h2 id="what-the-us-labels-actually-say">What the US labels actually say</h2>
<p>All the major US labels mention that delayed stomach emptying has the <em>potential</em> to affect how other oral medicines are absorbed. Beyond that, the wording differs:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Ozempic (semaglutide injection):</strong> in clinical pharmacology studies, semaglutide did not reduce the absorption of oral medications to any clinically relevant degree. The label still advises caution.</li>
<li><strong>Wegovy (semaglutide):</strong> asks clinicians to monitor the effects of oral medicines and consider extra clinical or lab monitoring for drugs with a narrow therapeutic index (where small changes in blood levels matter) or that need monitoring anyway. It also notes that in a study with the semaglutide <em>tablet</em>, levothyroxine (thyroid hormone) exposure rose by 33%.</li>
<li><strong>Mounjaro and Zepbound (tirzepatide):</strong> ask clinicians to monitor people taking oral medicines that depend on reaching a threshold level, or that have a narrow therapeutic index, with warfarin given as an example.</li>
</ul>
<p>So &quot;it may affect absorption&quot; isn&#39;t the same as &quot;every tablet stops working.&quot; It&#39;s a prompt to check the medicines where timing and blood levels really matter.</p>
<h2 id="can-glp-1-make-birth-control-pills-less-effective-tirzepatide-has-a-specific-instruction">Can GLP-1 make birth control pills less effective? Tirzepatide has a specific instruction</h2>
<p>This one is specific and worth knowing. The US Mounjaro and Zepbound labels advise people using oral hormonal contraceptives to switch to a non-oral method, or add a barrier method such as condoms, for 4 weeks after starting tirzepatide and for 4 weeks after each dose increase. Non-oral hormonal methods (such as an implant, IUD, patch or ring) shouldn&#39;t be affected, according to the labels. Ask your prescriber or pharmacist what applies to you.</p>
<h2 id="oral-semaglutide-has-its-own-rules">Oral semaglutide has its own rules</h2>
<p>If your GLP-1 is itself a tablet, such as Wegovy tablets, the instructions are different again. The Wegovy label says to take the tablet on an empty stomach with a small amount of water and wait at least 30 minutes before eating, drinking anything else, or taking other oral medicines. Our <a href="/blog/glp-1-pills-vs-injections/">comparison of GLP-1 pills and injections</a> explains why absorption works differently.</p>
<h2 id="diabetes-medicines-deserve-a-closer-look">Diabetes medicines deserve a closer look</h2>
<p>This isn&#39;t about stomach emptying, but it&#39;s one of the most important combinations to review. GLP-1 medicines can raise the risk of low blood sugar when used with insulin or sulfonylureas, and labels suggest clinicians consider lowering those doses when starting. Reduced appetite, vomiting or diarrhea can also change your usual routine. Follow your diabetes team&#39;s monitoring plan, and see our guide to <a href="/blog/glp-1-medicines-low-blood-sugar/">GLP-1 medicines and low blood sugar</a>.</p>

1c:T1133,<h2 id="before-surgery-or-sedation">Before surgery or sedation</h2>
<p>Slowed stomach emptying also matters before procedures. Labels mention rare reports of pulmonary aspiration (stomach contents entering the lungs) in people on GLP-1 medicines having general anesthesia or deep sedation, even when they had followed fasting instructions. Always tell the surgical or procedure team that you take a GLP-1 medicine, well before the day.</p>
<h2 id="should-the-timing-of-oral-medications-change-on-a-glp-1-what-to-do-and-not-do">Should the timing of oral medications change on a GLP-1? What to do (and not do)</h2>
<p>Give your prescriber and pharmacist a complete list: prescriptions, over-the-counter products and supplements, including how and when you take them. Ask specifically whether any of them need timing changes or extra monitoring.</p>
<p>Please don&#39;t move doses, crush tablets, change food timing or stop a prescription based on a general article, including this one. Keep following each medicine&#39;s own instructions until you get individual advice.</p>
<h2 id="when-to-get-in-touch">When to get in touch</h2>
<p>Contact your care team promptly if a treated condition suddenly seems poorly controlled (for example, seizures, irregular heart rhythm or unusual bleeding on a blood thinner), or if you have repeated vomiting, can&#39;t keep fluids down or have severe stomach pain. Emergency symptoms need urgent care. An interaction isn&#39;t the only possible explanation, which is why a clinician should assess it.</p>
<p>The goal isn&#39;t to fear every pill, but to check the combinations that matter. A pharmacist is often the fastest way to find out whether yours do.</p>
<h2 id="common-questions">Common questions</h2>
<h3 id="how-should-the-timing-of-oral-medications-be-modified-in-patients-on-glp-1">How should the timing of oral medications be modified in patients on GLP-1?</h3>
<p>There is no single rule, and the US labels don&#39;t set a blanket schedule change. Give your prescriber and pharmacist the full list of what you take, including when, and ask specifically whether any medicine needs timing changes or extra monitoring. Until you get individual advice, keep following each medicine&#39;s own instructions rather than moving doses on your own.</p>
<h3 id="does-semaglutide-reduce-absorption-of-oral-medications">Does semaglutide reduce absorption of oral medications?</h3>
<p>For Ozempic (semaglutide injection), clinical pharmacology studies found no clinically relevant effect on the absorption of oral medicines, though the label still advises caution. The Wegovy label asks clinicians to monitor oral medicines with a narrow therapeutic index, and notes that in a study with the semaglutide tablet, levothyroxine exposure rose by 33%. If you take Wegovy tablets, the label also says to wait at least 30 minutes before other oral medicines.</p>
<h3 id="can-glp-1-make-birth-control-pills-less-effective">Can GLP-1 make birth control pills less effective?</h3>
<p>For tirzepatide (Mounjaro and Zepbound), the US labels say yes, it can, and they advise switching to a non-oral method or adding a barrier method such as condoms for 4 weeks after starting and for 4 weeks after each dose increase. Non-oral hormonal methods, such as an implant, IUD, patch or ring, shouldn&#39;t be affected according to the labels. Ask your prescriber or pharmacist what applies to you.</p>
<h3 id="does-zepbound-affect-oral-medications-through-delayed-gastric-emptying">Does Zepbound affect oral medications through delayed gastric emptying?</h3>
<p>The Zepbound and Mounjaro labels say slowed stomach emptying has the potential to affect how oral medicines are absorbed. They ask clinicians to monitor people taking oral medicines that depend on reaching a threshold level or have a narrow therapeutic index, with warfarin given as the example. For most other pills, no change is expected, but your pharmacist can confirm for your list.</p>
<h3 id="why-does-delayed-stomach-emptying-matter-before-surgery">Why does delayed stomach emptying matter before surgery?</h3>
<p>Labels mention rare reports of pulmonary aspiration (stomach contents entering the lungs) in people on GLP-1 medicines having general anesthesia or deep sedation, even when they had followed fasting instructions. Tell the surgical or procedure team that you take a GLP-1 medicine well before the day.</p>
