15:T1172,<p>KFF&#39;s 2026 Employer Health Benefits Survey, published <strong>October 22, 2026</strong>, found that <strong>18% of employers with 200 or more workers cover GLP-1 drugs when used primarily for weight loss</strong>, about the same as in 2025 (19%) and 2024 (18%). Bigger employers cover them more often, many of those that cover them add requirements, and among large firms that don&#39;t cover them, very few plan to start. Below are the actual numbers, what each one measures, and what they mean if you&#39;re a worker trying to figure out your own plan.</p>
<h2 id="what-is-the-kff-employer-health-benefits-survey">What is the KFF Employer Health Benefits Survey?</h2>
<p>It&#39;s the long-running annual benchmark for job-based health insurance in the U.S.; 2026 is the 28th edition. The 2026 survey included <strong>1,937 interviews</strong> with randomly selected non-federal public and private employers with 10 or more workers. Davis Research conducted the fieldwork from <strong>January to July 2026</strong>, and the overall response rate was 11%.</p>
<p>One detail matters for every GLP-1 number below: the GLP-1 questions went to <strong>firms with 200 or more workers</strong> that offer health benefits, about their <strong>largest plan</strong>. So these are employer-level answers, not &quot;X% of Americans are covered.&quot;</p>

<p>What jumps out:</p>
<ul>

<li><strong>Smaller firms often don&#39;t know.</strong> 19% of firms with 200–999 workers said they didn&#39;t know whether their plan covers GLP-1s for weight loss, versus 8% of larger firms. If your HR team gives a vague answer, that may be why. Ask the plan directly.</li>
</ul>

1c:T1373,<h2 id="are-employers-dropping-glp-1-coverage">Are employers dropping GLP-1 coverage?</h2>
<p>Two numbers answer this, both about firms with 200+ workers that <strong>don&#39;t</strong> currently cover GLP-1s for weight loss in their largest plan:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>12%</strong> say they <strong>stopped</strong> covering them within the last 12 months.</li>
<li>On starting coverage in the next 12 months: <strong>1%</strong> very likely, <strong>13%</strong> somewhat likely, <strong>79%</strong> not likely, <strong>7%</strong> don&#39;t know.</li>
</ul>
<p>Put plainly: if your employer doesn&#39;t cover them now, it&#39;s unlikely to start next year. And some workers lost coverage this past year. If that&#39;s you, our <a href="/blog/employer-changed-glp-1-coverage/">first-7-days plan</a> walks through what to do.</p>
<h2 id="what-else-in-the-2026-survey-matters-for-glp-1-users">What else in the 2026 survey matters for GLP-1 users?</h2>
<p>A few broader findings shape what you&#39;ll pay:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Premiums:</strong> average annual premiums are <strong>$9,804</strong> for single coverage and <strong>$28,303</strong> for family coverage, each up 5%. Workers contribute on average <strong>$1,672</strong> (single) and <strong>$7,471</strong> (family).</li>
<li><strong>Deductibles:</strong> the average deductible for single coverage (among workers with one) is <strong>$1,955</strong>, and 88% of workers with single coverage have a general deductible.</li>
<li><strong>Drug cost-sharing:</strong> in plans with three or more tiers, the average copay for fourth-tier drugs is <strong>$136</strong>, and the average fourth-tier coinsurance is <strong>30%</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>Self-funding:</strong> <strong>71%</strong> of covered workers are in self-funded plans, where the employer pays claims directly and sets more of the rules.</li>
<li><strong>Direct-to-consumer prices:</strong> KFF notes that lower direct-to-consumer prices, plus the drugs&#39; significant cost, may lead employers to keep adapting their coverage. It also notes these programs give employers new options, such as funding access through account contributions.</li>
</ul>
<p>For how deductibles and coinsurance turn into real monthly bills, see <a href="/blog/glp-1-covered-vs-affordable/">covered vs affordable</a>.</p>
<h2 id="how-to-read-these-numbers-without-over-reading-them">How to read these numbers without over-reading them</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Every percentage has a denominator.</strong> &quot;40%&quot; means 40% of <em>covering</em> firms, not of all firms. &quot;12%&quot; means 12% of <em>non-covering</em> firms.</li>
<li><strong>These are firms, not people.</strong> A 5,000-worker company and a 250-worker company each count as one firm in a share like &quot;18%,&quot; though KFF weights results to represent employers.</li>
<li><strong>&quot;Similar&quot; means not statistically different.</strong> KFF uses a 0.05 significance threshold, so a 33% vs 43% gap can still be &quot;similar.&quot;</li>
<li><strong>Your plan is the only one that decides your coverage.</strong> The survey explains the landscape; your Summary of Benefits and Coverage and formulary explain your bill.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="what-this-means-for-workers">What this means for workers</h2>
<p>If you work for a large employer, the odds of coverage are better, but so are the odds of conditions attached. If you work for a mid-sized employer, coverage is less common, and HR may not know offhand, so go straight to the plan&#39;s formulary and policy. Either way, the time to check is <strong>before open enrollment closes</strong>; our <a href="/blog/open-enrollment-glp-1-coverage-2027/">2027 open-enrollment question list</a> is built for that. For the structural &quot;why,&quot; read <a href="/blog/employer-glp-1-coverage-2026/">why employer coverage varies</a>.</p>
<h2 id="common-questions">Common questions</h2>
<h3 id="what-percentage-of-employers-cover-glp-1s-for-weight-loss-in-2026">What percentage of employers cover GLP-1s for weight loss in 2026?</h3>
<p>KFF found 18% of firms with 200 or more workers cover GLP-1s for weight loss in their largest plan, ranging from 16% at firms with 200–999 workers to 33% at firms with 5,000 or more.</p>
<h3 id="did-employer-glp-1-coverage-go-down-in-2026">Did employer GLP-1 coverage go down in 2026?</h3>
<p>Overall, it was about flat: 18% in 2026, 19% in 2025 and 18% in 2024. Among non-covering large firms, 12% said they stopped covering these drugs for weight loss within the last 12 months.</p>
<h3 id="does-the-kff-survey-include-small-businesses">Does the KFF survey include small businesses?</h3>
<p>The survey covers employers with 10 or more workers, but the GLP-1 weight-loss questions were asked of firms with 200 or more workers.</p>

<p>KFF published the 2026 Employer Health Benefits Survey on October 22, 2026.</p>
