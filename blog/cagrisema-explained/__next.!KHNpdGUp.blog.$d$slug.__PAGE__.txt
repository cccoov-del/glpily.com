1d:T1118,<p>CagriSema is an investigational once-weekly injection that combines two medicines: semaglutide, which activates the GLP-1 receptor (the same ingredient as Wegovy and Ozempic), and cagrilintide, a long-acting analogue of the hormone amylin. The idea is to influence appetite and fullness through two different hormone pathways at once.</p>
<p>As of October 2026, CagriSema is <strong>not FDA-approved</strong>. Novo Nordisk submitted it to the FDA for chronic weight management in December 2025 and says it expects a US decision in the fourth quarter of 2026. A submission means the regulator is reviewing the evidence; it isn&#39;t a green light.</p>
<h2 id="why-combine-two-mechanisms">Why combine two mechanisms?</h2>
<p>Amylin is a hormone released alongside insulin after meals. It slows stomach emptying, dampens the post-meal rise in glucagon and adds to feelings of fullness. GLP-1 signaling affects appetite and blood sugar through a separate receptor. Researchers wanted to know whether using both together would do more than either alone. That&#39;s a reasonable hypothesis, and trials are what test it. (For a deeper look, see <a href="/blog/what-is-amylin-cagrilintide/">what amylin is and how it differs from GLP-1</a>.)</p>
<h2 id="what-have-the-trials-found">What have the trials found?</h2>
<p><strong>Peer-reviewed Phase 3 results (NEJM, 2025), dose 2.4 mg of each medicine weekly, 68 weeks:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>REDEFINE 1:</strong> 3,417 adults with obesity or overweight without diabetes. Average weight change was −20.4% with CagriSema versus −3.0% with placebo. Gastrointestinal side effects (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, abdominal pain) affected 79.6% on CagriSema versus 39.9% on placebo, mostly mild to moderate and temporary.</li>
<li><strong>REDEFINE 2:</strong> 1,206 adults with obesity or overweight and type 2 diabetes. Average weight change was −13.7% versus −3.4% with placebo, and gastrointestinal side effects affected 72.5% versus 34.4%.</li>
</ul>
<p>Notice the gap between those two trials: people with type 2 diabetes often lose less weight on these medicines on average, which is one reason trial populations matter so much.</p>
<p><strong>Company-reported results (not yet in full peer-reviewed papers):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>REDEFINE 4 (February 2026):</strong> an open-label, 84-week head-to-head trial in 809 adults with obesity compared CagriSema 2.4/2.4 mg with tirzepatide 15 mg. It did <em>not</em> meet its main goal of showing CagriSema was non-inferior to tirzepatide. Average weight change was −20.2% versus −23.6% when counting everyone regardless of whether they stayed on treatment.</li>
<li><strong>REIMAGINE 5 and REDEFINE 9 (September 2026):</strong> Novo reported topline results in type 2 diabetes (against tirzepatide 5 mg, 60 weeks) and in overweight or obesity (lower CagriSema doses against placebo, 68 weeks). These are sponsor announcements, so full details are still to come.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="how-to-read-cagrisema-headlines">How to read CagriSema headlines</h2>
<p>Check which trial a number comes from, who was enrolled, how long it lasted, which dose was used and what the comparison was. Also check the &quot;estimand&quot;: some figures assume everyone stayed on treatment, which makes results look larger than counting everyone who started. And remember that results from separate trials aren&#39;t a head-to-head comparison.</p>
<h2 id="can-you-get-cagrisema-now">Can you get CagriSema now?</h2>
<p>Not as an approved prescription product in the US. Regulatory decisions can also differ between countries. Be wary of any website or clinic selling &quot;CagriSema&quot; or cagrilintide: an investigational product bought online isn&#39;t the same as the medicine studied in trials, and there&#39;s no way to verify what&#39;s inside. Here&#39;s more on <a href="/blog/investigational-vs-approved-weight-loss-drug/">why investigational drugs aren&#39;t the same as approved medicines</a>.</p>
<h2 id="the-takeaway">The takeaway</h2>
<p>CagriSema shows how the field is moving toward combinations that act on more than one appetite pathway. Its results are substantial, but it fell short of tirzepatide in their head-to-head trial, and the FDA decision is still pending. It&#39;s worth following, not something to treat as already on the pharmacy shelf.</p>
