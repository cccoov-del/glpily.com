18:T143f,<p>Some plans will cover a GLP-1 for weight loss only if you also take part in a lifestyle program: coaching, dietitian visits, a digital nutrition program or regular check-ins. In 2026 this is common. KFF&#39;s 2026 Employer Health Benefits Survey found that 40% of firms covering GLP-1s for weight loss require it. The rule itself is usually manageable. The trouble comes from the vague parts, like what counts, who reports it, and by when.</p>
<p>Here&#39;s a familiar scene. Your refill comes back denied, with a note: &quot;Program participation not verified.&quot; You did go to the sessions. The plan just never got the record. This guide is about making sure that doesn&#39;t happen to you.</p>
<h2 id="how-common-are-lifestyle-program-requirements-for-glp-1-coverage">How common are lifestyle program requirements for GLP-1 coverage?</h2>
<p>They&#39;re common, and getting more so. According to KFF&#39;s 2026 survey (published October 22, 2026), among firms with 200 or more workers that cover GLP-1 medicines for weight loss:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>40%</strong> require enrollees to &quot;meet with a dietitian, case manager, or therapist,&quot; or to take part in a lifestyle program. That&#39;s up from 34% the year before.</li>
<li><strong>59%</strong> of the largest firms (5,000 or more workers) have this requirement.</li>
</ul>
<p>One caveat: only 18% of firms with 200 or more workers cover GLP-1s for weight loss in the first place. So the requirement applies to people who already have coverage, and it&#39;s a sign of how plans are managing that coverage. For the bigger employer picture, see our <a href="/blog/kff-2026-employer-glp-1-survey-explained/">KFF 2026 survey explainer</a>.</p>
<h2 id="what-do-these-requirements-look-like-in-real-plans">What do these requirements look like in real plans?</h2>
<p>They come in a few shapes. Two public examples show the range:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Example</th>
<th>What it requires</th>
<th>Who&#39;s exempt</th>
</tr>
</thead>

</tr>

<p>These examples show the two main types of requirement:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Attestation:</strong> A clinician signs off that you&#39;re working on diet and activity. The Medicare Bridge works this way, with your prescriber certifying it on the prior authorization.</li>
<li><strong>Enrollment and engagement:</strong> You have to sign up for a specific vendor program, and sometimes keep participating, for coverage to continue. Maine&#39;s plan is an example of this kind.</li>
</ol>
<p>Neither one is a judgment about you. Zepbound&#39;s own label describes it as used &quot;in combination with a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity,&quot; and plans are building that wording into their coverage rules.</p>
<h2 id="what-should-you-ask-your-plan-before-you-start">What should you ask your plan before you start?</h2>
<p>Call the number on your card, or message the program through your benefits portal. Ask for answers <strong>in writing</strong>, or write down the date, the rep&#39;s name and the call reference number.</p>
<p><strong>About the program:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Which programs or providers qualify: a specific vendor only, any registered dietitian, or my own doctor?</li>
<li>Does virtual or app-based participation count?</li>
<li>Does it cost anything, such as a monthly fee, visit copays or a separate deductible?</li>
<li>Is there a waitlist? How long does enrollment take?</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>About &quot;completion&quot;:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>What exactly counts? A number of sessions, logins, weigh-ins, weeks enrolled, or a signed form?</li>
<li>Is it a one-time requirement, or does it have to continue for coverage to continue?</li>
<li>Is there a deadline, and is it tied to my first fill or to the plan year?</li>
<li>Does missing a session put my prescription on hold right away, or is there a grace period?</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>About reporting:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Who tells the plan that I participated: me, the program or my prescriber?</li>
<li>How often is that information sent, and how long does it take to process?</li>
<li>If the record is missing, who do I contact to fix it?</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>About exceptions:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Are there accommodations for disability, shift work, caregiving, language or no internet access?</li>
<li>Is there an exemption if I take a GLP-1 for diabetes, sleep apnea or another covered condition?</li>
</ul>

<p>A few habits that help:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Download visit summaries</strong> from patient portals the same day. Portals sometimes archive them.</li>
<li><strong>Screenshot program dashboards</strong> that show completed modules or weeks, with the date visible.</li>
<li><strong>Save the requirement itself.</strong> Keep the plan&#39;s letter or the prior authorization approval that names the program rule. If the rule changes mid-year, you want the old version.</li>
<li><strong>Line it up with your prescription dates.</strong> If your PA renews every 6 or 12 months, set a reminder a few weeks early so the documents are ready. Our <a href="/blog/glp-1-prior-authorization-paperwork-checklist/">prior authorization paperwork checklist</a> covers the rest of the packet.</li>
</ul>
<p>Your own notes can help too. A daily check-in, meal log or journal in an app like GLPily is useful to bring to a dietitian visit. Just be aware that the plan almost never accepts your personal tracker as proof of participation. It wants records from the program or the clinician.</p>
<h2 id="what-if-the-requirement-doesn-39-t-fit-your-life">What if the requirement doesn&#39;t fit your life?</h2>
<p>Program rules are written for a &quot;typical&quot; member, and lots of people aren&#39;t one. If the only qualifying sessions are at 2 p.m. on weekdays and you work nights, or the program is offered only in English, or a disability makes group classes difficult, ask for an accommodation or an alternative. Do it <strong>before</strong> a deadline, and do it in writing.</p>
<p>If coverage is denied for a missing program step, it&#39;s usually an administrative problem, not a medical one, and those can often be fixed. Ask for the specific reason in writing, send in your proof, and follow the plan&#39;s appeal process if you need to. Our guide to <a href="/blog/glp-1-coverage-denial-appeal-organize/">organizing a GLP-1 appeal</a> walks through it.</p>
<p>Rules can also change at renewal. A plan might add a program, switch vendors or drop weight-loss coverage. If your employer announced changes, read <a href="/blog/employer-changed-glp-1-coverage/">what to check when your employer changes GLP-1 coverage</a>.</p>
<h2 id="common-questions">Common questions</h2>
<h3 id="do-i-have-to-do-a-lifestyle-program-to-get-wegovy-or-zepbound-covered">Do I have to do a lifestyle program to get Wegovy or Zepbound covered?</h3>
<p>Only if your plan requires it. In KFF&#39;s 2026 survey, 40% of firms that cover GLP-1s for weight loss had a dietitian, case manager, therapist or lifestyle program requirement. On the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge, your prescriber certifies lifestyle program participation as part of the prior authorization.</p>
<h3 id="does-the-requirement-apply-if-i-take-a-glp-1-for-diabetes">Does the requirement apply if I take a GLP-1 for diabetes?</h3>
<p>Usually not. The public examples, Maine&#39;s state employee plan and the Medicare Bridge, apply the rule to weight-management prescriptions only. Diabetes and other covered conditions go through a separate process. Check your own plan&#39;s wording.</p>
<h3 id="can-my-own-doctor-or-dietitian-count-as-the-program">Can my own doctor or dietitian count as the program?</h3>
<p>Sometimes. Some plans accept any qualifying clinician, while others require a specific vendor. Ask which providers qualify and how their records get to the plan before you book.</p>
<h3 id="what-happens-if-i-miss-a-session">What happens if I miss a session?</h3>
<p>It depends on the plan. Some have a grace period, while others may pause coverage until participation is confirmed. Ask about this before you start, and keep dated records so you can fix a mistake quickly.</p>
