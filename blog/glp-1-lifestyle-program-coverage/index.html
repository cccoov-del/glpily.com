GLP-1 Coverage With a Lifestyle Program Requirement: What Counts

40% of employers that cover GLP-1s for weight loss now require a dietitian, case manager, therapist or lifestyle program. Here's how to find out exactly what yours wants, and how to prove you did it.

Some plans will cover a GLP-1 for weight loss only if you also take part in a lifestyle program: coaching, dietitian visits, a digital nutrition program or regular check-ins. In 2026 this is common. KFF's 2026 Employer Health Benefits Survey found that 40% of firms covering GLP-1s for weight loss require it. The rule itself is usually manageable. The trouble comes from the vague parts, like what counts, who reports it, and by when.

Here's a familiar scene. Your refill comes back denied, with a note: "Program participation not verified." You did go to the sessions. The plan just never got the record. This guide is about making sure that doesn't happen to you.

How common are lifestyle program requirements for GLP-1 coverage?

They're common, and getting more so. According to KFF's 2026 survey (published October 22, 2026), among firms with 200 or more workers that cover GLP-1 medicines for weight loss:

  • 40% require enrollees to "meet with a dietitian, case manager, or therapist," or to take part in a lifestyle program. That's up from 34% the year before.
  • 59% of the largest firms (5,000 or more workers) have this requirement.

One caveat: only 18% of firms with 200 or more workers cover GLP-1s for weight loss in the first place. So the requirement applies to people who already have coverage, and it's a sign of how plans are managing that coverage. For the bigger employer picture, see our KFF 2026 survey explainer.

What do these requirements look like in real plans?

They come in a few shapes. Two public examples show the range:

Example What it requires Who's exempt
Medicare GLP-1 Bridge (from July 1, 2026) When prior authorization is requested, your prescriber certifies that you're using the drug as part of a lifestyle program focused on diet and exercise People with type 2 diabetes, moderate-to-severe sleep apnea or fatty liver disease aren't eligible for the Bridge (their Part D plan handles these)
State of Maine employee plan (2026) Members taking GLP-1s for weight loss must enroll in a nutrition and lifestyle program. The change took effect in July, and current users had until the end of October to enroll Members taking GLP-1s for diabetes or other qualifying conditions

These examples show the two main types of requirement:

  1. Attestation: A clinician signs off that you're working on diet and activity. The Medicare Bridge works this way, with your prescriber certifying it on the prior authorization.
  2. Enrollment and engagement: You have to sign up for a specific vendor program, and sometimes keep participating, for coverage to continue. Maine's plan is an example of this kind.

Neither one is a judgment about you. Zepbound's own label describes it as used "in combination with a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity," and plans are building that wording into their coverage rules.

What should you ask your plan before you start?

Call the number on your card, or message the program through your benefits portal. Ask for answers in writing, or write down the date, the rep's name and the call reference number.

About the program:

  • Which programs or providers qualify: a specific vendor only, any registered dietitian, or my own doctor?
  • Does virtual or app-based participation count?
  • Does it cost anything, such as a monthly fee, visit copays or a separate deductible?
  • Is there a waitlist? How long does enrollment take?

About "completion":

  • What exactly counts? A number of sessions, logins, weigh-ins, weeks enrolled, or a signed form?
  • Is it a one-time requirement, or does it have to continue for coverage to continue?
  • Is there a deadline, and is it tied to my first fill or to the plan year?
  • Does missing a session put my prescription on hold right away, or is there a grace period?

About reporting:

  • Who tells the plan that I participated: me, the program or my prescriber?
  • How often is that information sent, and how long does it take to process?
  • If the record is missing, who do I contact to fix it?

About exceptions:

  • Are there accommodations for disability, shift work, caregiving, language or no internet access?
  • Is there an exemption if I take a GLP-1 for diabetes, sleep apnea or another covered condition?

How do you document that you met the requirement?

Assume the paperwork will go missing at some point, and keep your own copy. A simple log works:

Date Session type Provider / program Proof you saved Reported to plan?
____ Dietitian visit (video) ____ Visit summary PDF Yes / pending
____ Group class ____ Attendance email ____
____ App check-in week ____ ____ Screenshot of completion ____

A few habits that help:

  • Download visit summaries from patient portals the same day. Portals sometimes archive them.
  • Screenshot program dashboards that show completed modules or weeks, with the date visible.
  • Save the requirement itself. Keep the plan's letter or the prior authorization approval that names the program rule. If the rule changes mid-year, you want the old version.
  • Line it up with your prescription dates. If your PA renews every 6 or 12 months, set a reminder a few weeks early so the documents are ready. Our prior authorization paperwork checklist covers the rest of the packet.

Your own notes can help too. A daily check-in, meal log or journal in an app like GLPily is useful to bring to a dietitian visit. Just be aware that the plan almost never accepts your personal tracker as proof of participation. It wants records from the program or the clinician.

What if the requirement doesn't fit your life?

Program rules are written for a "typical" member, and lots of people aren't one. If the only qualifying sessions are at 2 p.m. on weekdays and you work nights, or the program is offered only in English, or a disability makes group classes difficult, ask for an accommodation or an alternative. Do it before a deadline, and do it in writing.

If coverage is denied for a missing program step, it's usually an administrative problem, not a medical one, and those can often be fixed. Ask for the specific reason in writing, send in your proof, and follow the plan's appeal process if you need to. Our guide to organizing a GLP-1 appeal walks through it.

Rules can also change at renewal. A plan might add a program, switch vendors or drop weight-loss coverage. If your employer announced changes, read what to check when your employer changes GLP-1 coverage.

Common questions

Do I have to do a lifestyle program to get Wegovy or Zepbound covered?

Only if your plan requires it. In KFF's 2026 survey, 40% of firms that cover GLP-1s for weight loss had a dietitian, case manager, therapist or lifestyle program requirement. On the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge, your prescriber certifies lifestyle program participation as part of the prior authorization.

Does the requirement apply if I take a GLP-1 for diabetes?

Usually not. The public examples, Maine's state employee plan and the Medicare Bridge, apply the rule to weight-management prescriptions only. Diabetes and other covered conditions go through a separate process. Check your own plan's wording.

Can my own doctor or dietitian count as the program?

Sometimes. Some plans accept any qualifying clinician, while others require a specific vendor. Ask which providers qualify and how their records get to the plan before you book.

What happens if I miss a session?

It depends on the plan. Some have a grace period, while others may pause coverage until participation is confirmed. Ask about this before you start, and keep dated records so you can fix a mistake quickly.
