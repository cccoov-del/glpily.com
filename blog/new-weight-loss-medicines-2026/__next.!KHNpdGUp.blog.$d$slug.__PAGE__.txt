19:T1441,<p>The obesity-medication landscape is moving quickly. People have more questions about pills versus injections, how medicines differ, and whether a drug in the news is actually available yet.</p>
<p>This guide is US-focused and reflects regulatory information checked on 11 October 2026. Drug status and availability can differ in the UK, EU and other countries.</p>
<h2 id="oral-wegovy-semaglutide-in-tablet-form">Oral Wegovy: semaglutide in tablet form</h2>
<p>The FDA approved Wegovy tablets in December 2025 for labeled weight-management use in adults, with a cardiovascular risk-reduction indication for a specified population. The tablets contain semaglutide, the active ingredient also found in injectable Wegovy and diabetes-branded Ozempic.</p>
<p>A pill may appeal to people who prefer not to inject, but “oral” does not automatically mean more effective, easier for everyone, less expensive in every plan or suitable for everyone. Follow the product&#39;s administration instructions and discuss options with a prescriber.</p>
<h2 id="foundayo-orforglipron-an-oral-glp-1-medicine">Foundayo (orforglipron): an oral GLP-1 medicine</h2>
<p>The FDA approved Foundayo (orforglipron) on 1 April 2026 for chronic weight management in eligible adults. It is an oral small-molecule GLP-1 receptor agonist. Lilly announced US availability on 9 April 2026.</p>
<p>Foundayo is a different molecule from semaglutide. A useful comparison should cover the approved indication, trial populations and duration, administration instructions, side effects, availability and cost—not just one headline weight-loss percentage. Results from different trials should not be treated as a direct contest.</p>
<h2 id="wegovy-hd-a-higher-dose-injectable-option">Wegovy HD: a higher-dose injectable option</h2>
<p>The FDA approved Wegovy HD, a 7.2 mg semaglutide injection, on 19 March 2026 for a specified adult weight-management population. A higher dose does not mean a person should increase their own dose, and it is not automatically appropriate for everyone using semaglutide.</p>
<p>The current prescribing information and a qualified prescriber&#39;s judgment remain the source of truth for eligibility and use.</p>
<h2 id="cagrisema-combination-treatment-under-regulatory-review">CagriSema: combination treatment under regulatory review</h2>
<p>CagriSema combines cagrilintide, an amylin analogue, with semaglutide. Novo Nordisk announced submission of a US FDA application in December 2025. As of 11 October 2026, the FDA had not approved CagriSema; Novo Nordisk has said it expects a US decision in the fourth quarter of 2026.</p>
<p>A company-reported trial average is not the same as approval, commercial availability or a guarantee about any individual&#39;s results.</p>
<h2 id="retatrutide-exciting-trial-news-but-still-investigational">Retatrutide: exciting trial news, but still investigational</h2>
<p>Retatrutide is an investigational triple hormone-receptor agonist acting on GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon pathways. Eli Lilly reported positive Phase 3 results in 2026; the TRIUMPH-1 obesity trial was published in the New England Journal of Medicine in September 2026. Lilly has said it plans to submit a US application in the first quarter of 2027. More detail: <a href="/blog/retatrutide-2026-trial-results">Retatrutide in 2026</a>.</p>
<p>That is important news, but retatrutide remained investigational in the sources checked as of 11 October 2026. Do not buy products sold online as retatrutide or “research peptides.” A molecule being studied is not the same as an approved, quality-controlled prescription product being available to consumers.</p>
<h2 id="how-to-compare-trial-headlines-fairly">How to compare trial headlines fairly</h2>
<p>Before comparing percentages, check whether trials enrolled people with diabetes, their baseline characteristics, duration, dose escalation, dropout handling and whether the reported number is an on-treatment result or an intention-to-treat result. Company press releases are useful for announcements, but treat their numbers as sponsor-reported until full peer-reviewed results are published.</p>
<h2 id="what-should-readers-do-with-this-news">What should readers do with this news?</h2>
<p>A headline about a bigger average result isn&#39;t a reason to change treatment on your own. If you have side effects or questions about alternatives, talk to your clinician about whether an approved option fits your goals, history, availability and coverage.</p>
<p>For readers following the pipeline, distinguish four milestones: trial recruitment, trial results, regulator submission and approval. Headlines often compress these into “the next miracle drug,” which isn&#39;t accurate.</p>
<h2 id="the-takeaway">The takeaway</h2>
<p>2026 has brought new US options, including oral Wegovy, Foundayo and higher-dose Wegovy HD, while CagriSema and retatrutide require careful status checking. News is exciting, but approval status, direct comparisons and personal suitability are separate questions.</p>
