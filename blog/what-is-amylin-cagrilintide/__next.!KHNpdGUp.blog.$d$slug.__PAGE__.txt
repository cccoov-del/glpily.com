1e:T646,<p>Amylin is a hormone made by the beta cells of the pancreas, the same cells that make insulin. After a meal, the two are released together. Amylin&#39;s job is to help manage what happens next: it slows how quickly food leaves the stomach, damps down the rise in glucagon after eating (which helps limit the liver&#39;s release of glucose), and adds to the feeling of being full through signals in the brain. In people with diabetes, amylin release is reduced.</p>
<p>Researchers have built longer-acting versions of amylin, including <strong>cagrilintide</strong>, and are studying whether this pathway can work alongside GLP-1 medicines.</p>
<h2 id="how-is-amylin-different-from-glp-1">How is amylin different from GLP-1?</h2>
<p>They&#39;re separate hormone systems with overlapping effects. GLP-1 is mostly released by cells in the gut and acts on the GLP-1 receptor. Amylin comes from the pancreas and acts on its own set of receptors. Both affect appetite, fullness and post-meal blood sugar, which is why combining them is interesting, but they aren&#39;t the same signal.</p>
<h2 id="is-amylin-already-used-in-medicine">Is amylin already used in medicine?</h2>
<p>Yes, in a narrow way. Pramlintide (Symlin), an amylin analogue, has been FDA-approved since 2005 as an add-on treatment for adults with type 1 or type 2 diabetes who use mealtime insulin. It&#39;s injected with meals and carries a boxed warning about severe low blood sugar when used with insulin. It isn&#39;t approved for weight management. Cagrilintide is a different, longer-acting molecule designed for once-weekly dosing.</p>
1f:T7b1,<h2 id="what-do-the-cagrilintide-trials-show">What do the cagrilintide trials show?</h2>
<p>The main evidence comes from CagriSema, a weekly injection combining cagrilintide 2.4 mg with semaglutide 2.4 mg. In the peer-reviewed REDEFINE 1 trial (3,417 adults with obesity or overweight without diabetes, 68 weeks), average weight change was −20.4% with the combination versus −3.0% with placebo. The trial also included smaller groups on cagrilintide alone and semaglutide alone. Gastrointestinal side effects were common with the combination (79.6% versus 39.9% on placebo), mostly mild to moderate and temporary.</p>
<p>A combination also has to earn its place against existing options. In a company-reported head-to-head trial (REDEFINE 4), CagriSema did not show it was non-inferior to tirzepatide 15 mg for weight loss. For the full trial picture, see <a href="/blog/cagrisema-explained/">CagriSema explained</a>.</p>
<h2 id="is-this-something-to-try-now">Is this something to try now?</h2>
<p>No. As of October 2026, neither cagrilintide nor CagriSema is FDA-approved; Novo Nordisk submitted CagriSema to the FDA in December 2025 and expects a decision in late 2026. Products sold online as cagrilintide or amylin &quot;research peptides&quot; aren&#39;t the medicine tested in trials, and their identity, purity and dose can&#39;t be verified. Approved GLP-1 medicines each have their own label, and an experimental combination isn&#39;t interchangeable with them.</p>
<h2 id="what-to-watch-next">What to watch next</h2>
<p>Look for full peer-reviewed results of the newer trials, longer follow-up, side-effect and discontinuation rates, and whether benefits extend to health outcomes beyond weight. Regulatory decisions may vary by country, so always check the date on any status update.</p>
<p>Understanding amylin helps explain one direction obesity research is heading. It doesn&#39;t mean every new combination will be better, or right for everyone.</p>
