19:Td1e,<p>It is especially frustrating to feel unwell from a medicine and see little change on the scale. But side effects and treatment effectiveness are different things. Nausea, reflux, diarrhea or fatigue don&#39;t measure how much weight someone will lose. Some people have significant digestive symptoms and limited benefit; others have few side effects and meaningful progress toward their treatment goals.</p>
<h2 id="why-symptoms-don-39-t-predict-the-result">Why symptoms don&#39;t predict the result</h2>
<p>There&#39;s actual data on this. A pooled analysis of the STEP 1–3 trials of semaglutide 2.4 mg (Wegovy&#39;s dose) looked at whether digestive side effects explained weight loss. Average weight loss was similar in people with and without gastrointestinal side effects, and less than 1 percentage point of the extra weight loss compared with placebo was explained by those side effects. That analysis covers one medicine in trial settings, so it can&#39;t be stretched to every product or person, but it undercuts the idea that nausea is the &quot;proof&quot; a GLP-1 is working.</p>
<p>Weight change depends on many interacting factors, including time on treatment, the product and dose, underlying health, other medicines, daily life and individual biology. A 2026 systematic review of low weight-loss response to incretin medicines lists factors such as higher starting weight and HbA1c, insulin resistance, adherence and time on treatment. Those are group-level associations, not a personal formula. For more on this, see Do GLP-1 medicines need to cause side effects to work?</p>
<h2 id="what-matters-if-you-feel-bad-and-see-little-progress">What matters if you feel bad and see little progress</h2>
<p>Don&#39;t keep putting up with symptoms because you hope the discomfort means the drug is working. Note what you experience, how often it happens, how it affects eating and drinking, and whether it gets in the way of work or sleep. A simple log makes the conversation easier; here&#39;s <a href="/blog/track-glp-1-side-effects/">how to track GLP-1 side effects</a> so your clinician gets the full picture.</p>
<p>Contact your prescriber if symptoms are persistent or hard to manage. Get prompt medical care for severe or persistent abdominal pain (with or without vomiting), vomiting that stops you keeping fluids down, signs of dehydration such as very little urine or dizziness when standing, or yellowing of the skin or eyes.</p>
<h2 id="what-a-review-might-consider">What a review might consider</h2>
<p>A clinician can check the medicine and treatment timeline, how response is being measured, whether other conditions or medicines are relevant, and whether the current plan still fits. You can ask what options exist if tolerability is poor or results are below expectations. Don&#39;t raise the dose, add a second weight-loss drug or switch products on your own.</p>
<h2 id="a-more-useful-definition-of-success">A more useful definition of success</h2>
<p>A treatment plan should be judged by its intended benefits, safety, tolerability and your goals, not by whether it makes you nauseous. If side effects make it hard to eat enough, drink enough or live normally, that matters clinically even when the scale is moving.</p>
<p>You deserve both clarity and comfort. Side effects are not a fee you have to pay to earn results.</p>
