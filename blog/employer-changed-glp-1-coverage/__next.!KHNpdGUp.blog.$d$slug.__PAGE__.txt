18:T1367,<p>If your employer just changed or dropped GLP-1 coverage, spend the first week getting four things straight: <strong>the effective date, exactly what changed, how much medicine you have before then, and what your prescriber recommends next.</strong> Don&#39;t stretch doses, stop suddenly or stockpile on your own. Use the time between the notice and the effective date to line up a plan. Here it is, one day at a time.</p>
<p>It happens more than you&#39;d think. In KFF&#39;s 2026 survey of employers with 200+ workers, 12% of those not covering GLP-1s for weight loss said they&#39;d <strong>stopped</strong> covering them in the last 12 months. You&#39;re not the only one reading this email twice.</p>
<h2 id="day-1-what-exactly-changed-in-my-glp-1-coverage">Day 1: What exactly changed in my GLP-1 coverage?</h2>
<p>Read the notice slowly, with a highlighter. You&#39;re looking for:</p>
<ul>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> <strong>Effective date.</strong> Everything else hangs on this.</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> <strong>Which drugs.</strong> All GLP-1s, one brand, or one form (vials vs pens, for example)?</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> <strong>Which use.</strong> Weight management only, or diabetes too?</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> <strong>Who&#39;s affected.</strong> New starts only, or people already on treatment too? Some changes let current users continue for a while; don&#39;t assume yours does.</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> <strong>New hoops.</strong> Sometimes &quot;change&quot; means new prior authorization criteria or a lifestyle-program requirement rather than an outright drop.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Your notice rights:</strong> for job-based plans, if a mid-year change affects what your Summary of Benefits and Coverage says, federal rules require notice <strong>at least 60 days before</strong> it takes effect. If you got far less warning, ask HR when the change was adopted and how the plan delivered the notice.</p>
<h2 id="day-2-get-the-rules-in-writing">Day 2: Get the rules in writing</h2>
<p>Email HR or benefits (email leaves a trail) and ask for:</p>
<ul>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> The updated <strong>Summary of Benefits and Coverage</strong> or <strong>Summary of Material Modifications</strong></li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> The plan&#39;s <strong>coverage policy</strong> for your drug after the change</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> Whether any <strong>transition fill, continuity-of-care or exception</strong> process exists</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> Whether the plan is <strong>self-funded or fully insured</strong> (it affects which rules apply; see <a href="/blog/employer-glp-1-coverage-2026/">why coverage varies by employer</a>)</li>
</ul>
<p>Federal law requires plan administrators to provide the plan description and change notices free of charge.</p>

<p><strong>Don&#39;t:</strong> stretch doses, skip weeks to &quot;make it last,&quot; or try to fill far ahead in ways your plan doesn&#39;t allow. Gaps and restarts have their own label instructions; see restarting after a break.</p>
<h2 id="day-4-talk-to-your-prescriber">Day 4: Talk to your prescriber</h2>
<p>Book the appointment (or portal message) now, before the effective date. Questions to bring:</p>
<ol>
<li>Is there any route under the <strong>new</strong> plan rules, such as an exception, different criteria or a covered alternative?</li>
<li>Is an <strong>appeal</strong> worth trying? (If the plan simply excludes the drug now, probably not. If it&#39;s a criteria issue, maybe. Our <a href="/blog/glp-1-coverage-denial-appeal-organize/">appeal organizer</a> helps.)</li>
<li>If I move to <strong>self-pay</strong>, which product and form makes sense for me?</li>
<li>If I need to pause or stop, <strong>what&#39;s the plan</strong> for doing that safely, and what should I watch for?</li>
</ol>
<p>That last question matters. Research shows weight often returns after stopping semaglutide (see <a href="/blog/what-happens-when-you-stop-semaglutide/">what happens when you stop</a>), so any pause deserves a plan, not an accident.</p>

<h2 id="day-6-check-your-money-tools">Day 6: Check your money tools</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>HSA or FSA:</strong> prescription medicines for a diagnosed condition may qualify. The details are in our IRS-rules explainer.</li>
<li><strong>Budget the year, not the month.</strong> Multiply your likely monthly price by the months left in the plan year, then add visits.</li>
<li><strong>Watch the dose ladder.</strong> Self-pay prices often rise with dose, so the price you&#39;ll pay in three months may differ from today&#39;s.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="day-7-plan-for-the-next-decision-point">Day 7: Plan for the next decision point</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Can I switch plans mid-year?</strong> Usually not because of one drug. IRS cafeteria-plan rules let employers allow election changes when coverage is <em>significantly curtailed</em>, meaning an overall reduction in coverage. Losing one drug often won&#39;t count, but ask HR what your plan allows.</li>
<li><strong>Spouse&#39;s or partner&#39;s plan?</strong> Compare it at the next open enrollment, or sooner if a qualifying event applies.</li>
<li><strong>Open enrollment.</strong> Put the date in your calendar now, then use our <a href="/blog/open-enrollment-glp-1-coverage-2027/">2027 open-enrollment questions</a>.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="common-questions">Common questions</h2>
<h3 id="can-my-employer-drop-glp-1-coverage-in-the-middle-of-the-year">Can my employer drop GLP-1 coverage in the middle of the year?</h3>
<p>Plans can change benefits, but for job-based plans a mid-year change that affects the Summary of Benefits and Coverage generally requires notice at least 60 days before it takes effect.</p>
<h3 id="will-i-get-a-transition-supply">Will I get a transition supply?</h3>
<p>Only if your plan offers one. Ask HR and the plan directly and get the answer in writing.</p>
<h3 id="can-i-use-a-manufacturer-savings-card-if-my-plan-stopped-covering-zepbound">Can I use a manufacturer savings card if my plan stopped covering Zepbound?</h3>
<p>Lilly&#39;s terms include a savings card for people with commercial insurance that doesn&#39;t cover the KwikPen, priced at its self-pay levels. Government-insured people aren&#39;t eligible. Check the current terms.</p>
<h3 id="should-i-stop-my-glp-1-if-i-can-39-t-afford-it-next-month">Should I stop my GLP-1 if I can&#39;t afford it next month?</h3>
<p>Talk to your prescriber before stopping. They can plan the transition and tell you what to watch for.</p>
