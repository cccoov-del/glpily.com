15:T1249,<p>To share GLP-1 tracking data with your doctor in a way that actually helps, bring <strong>one page built around one question</strong>: the date range, your medication history as taken, the trend that matters, symptoms with dates, and clear labels on anything estimated. Clinicians use short summaries and trend graphs; they skim screenshots.</p>
<p>That is not a guess. It is roughly what research on &quot;patient-generated health data&quot; keeps finding.</p>
<h2 id="what-do-clinicians-actually-find-useful-in-patient-tracking-data">What do clinicians actually find useful in patient tracking data?</h2>
<p>A systematic review in <em>Health Informatics Journal</em> (Lordon and colleagues, 2020) pulled together 13 studies on how patient-tracked data affects the patient-clinician relationship. The useful bits, in plain terms:</p>
<ul>
<li>Clinicians did use patient data to <strong>adjust treatment plans</strong> and to understand what happened <strong>between visits</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>Brief summaries reviewed before the appointment</strong> fit into their workflow and saved time.</li>
<li><strong>Trend graphs and summary measures</strong> helped them make sense of the data.</li>
<li>Having the patient <strong>verbally summarize</strong> the data cut down the effort during the visit.</li>
</ul>
<p>And the barriers: short appointments, no time to review data, and <strong>information overload</strong>.</p>
<p>So the goal is not &quot;show everything.&quot; It is &quot;make the important thing obvious in under a minute.&quot;</p>
<h2 id="start-with-the-question-not-the-data">Start with the question, not the data</h2>
<p>Before you export anything, finish this sentence: <em>&quot;At this visit, I want to figure out…&quot;</em></p>
<ul>
<li>&quot;...whether the nausea after each dose increase is normal for me or worth changing something.&quot;</li>
<li>&quot;...why my weight has been flat for six weeks.&quot;</li>
<li>&quot;...whether I can manage the dose schedule around night shifts.&quot;</li>
</ul>
<p>Your question decides what goes in. A nausea question needs symptom timing next to dose dates. A plateau question needs the weight trend and maybe measurements. Neither needs your water intake for the past four months.</p>
<h2 id="what-should-a-glp-1-tracking-report-include">What should a GLP-1 tracking report include?</h2>
<p>Here is a structure that fits on one page:</p>

<h2 id="how-should-you-present-weight-and-symptom-trends">How should you present weight and symptom trends?</h2>
<p><strong>Weight: show the direction, not the noise.</strong> Daily weight jumps around for many reasons (<a href="/blog/why-weight-changes-day-to-day/">Why Your Weight Changes From Day to Day</a>). A weekly average or a simple line over the period is far more readable than 80 single readings.</p>
<p><strong>Symptoms: pair them with dose dates.</strong> &quot;Nausea most days for the first 4 days after moving up to the next dose on Sept 2, easing by Sept 20&quot; is gold. &quot;Felt sick sometimes&quot; is not. Our guide to <a href="/blog/track-glp-1-side-effects/">tracking GLP-1 side effects</a> shows how to log symptoms so they summarize well.</p>
<p><strong>Medication: record what you actually took.</strong> If you were prescribed weekly doses but missed one, or took one two days late, put that in. It is not a confession; it is data your clinician needs to interpret everything else.</p>

1c:T13f8,<h2 id="label-estimates-and-missing-data">Label estimates and missing data</h2>
<p>Tracking apps mix measured, entered and estimated numbers. A report should keep them apart:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Measured:</strong> your scale weight, a blood pressure cuff reading.</li>
<li><strong>Entered:</strong> symptoms, doses, notes.</li>
<li><strong>Estimated:</strong> food nutrition from photos, step-based calorie burn, and especially any <strong>estimated medication-level curve</strong>, which is a model, not a lab value. (<a href="/blog/glp-1-estimated-medication-level-charts/">GLP-1 Medication Level Charts</a> explains why.)</li>
</ul>
<p>Then say where the gaps are. &quot;I stopped logging food in September&quot; is more useful than a report that quietly averages the missing weeks.</p>
<h2 id="how-do-you-send-tracking-data-to-your-doctor">How do you send tracking data to your doctor?</h2>
<p>Ask the office which route they prefer. Common options:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>The patient portal message</strong> with a PDF or short summary attached. Often the easiest for the clinic to file.</li>
<li><strong>A printed page</strong> at check-in.</li>
<li><strong>Apple Health&#39;s sharing feature,</strong> if your health system participates. In the US, the Health app can share selected categories (such as activity, heart rate, blood pressure, sleep and labs) with participating organizations; the data is encrypted end to end, and your provider views it inside their records system. You choose the categories, and you can stop sharing at any time.</li>
</ul>
<p>A few safety habits:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Do not post reports to public links</strong> or social media, even &quot;unlisted.&quot;</li>
<li><strong>Check what the export contains.</strong> Some include your name, email, birth date or photos. Remove what is not needed.</li>
<li><strong>Urgent symptoms do not wait for a report.</strong> Severe abdominal pain, signs of an allergic reaction, or vomiting you cannot keep fluids down are reasons to call your prescriber or 911, not to compile a summary.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="a-worked-example">A worked example</h2>
<p>Here is what a useful report might say, in full:</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Question:</strong> Is the stomach upset after dose increases something to adjust, or wait out?
<strong>Range:</strong> Aug 4 to Oct 20.
<strong>Medication:</strong> Weekly injection, Mondays. Dose increased Aug 4 and Sept 1. One dose taken Wednesday (Sept 15) because of travel.
<strong>Weight:</strong> 214 to 203 lb, steady downward trend; flat Sept 8 to 22.
<strong>Symptoms:</strong> Nausea days 1 to 3 after each increase, worst after Sept 1 (5 of 7 days). Constipation most of September. Better by Oct.
<strong>Other:</strong> Started iron supplement Sept 5. Travel Sept 13 to 17.
<strong>Estimates/gaps:</strong> Food from photo estimates. No weights during travel.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>That is about 90 words. A clinician can read it in under a minute and immediately see the questions worth asking (the iron supplement and the constipation, for example).</p>

<h2 id="where-an-app-helps-and-where-it-does-not">Where an app helps (and where it does not)</h2>
<p>An app is handy here because the dates are already attached to everything. In GLPily, your shot and pill log, weight, measurements, symptoms and daily check-ins are all dated in one place, and the Pily AI helper can answer questions from your own logs, like &quot;when did my nausea start after the last dose change?&quot; That makes the summary faster to write. It does not make the app your clinician; the interpretation is theirs.</p>
<h2 id="common-questions">Common questions</h2>
<h3 id="should-i-bring-my-tracking-app-to-a-doctor-39-s-appointment">Should I bring my tracking app to a doctor&#39;s appointment?</h3>
<p>Bring a summary, and have the app ready in case your clinician wants details. Scrolling through an app together eats time that is better spent on your question.</p>
<h3 id="how-much-tracking-data-should-i-share-with-my-doctor">How much tracking data should I share with my doctor?</h3>
<p>Usually one page: your question, the date range, medication as taken, the main trend, symptoms with dates and any gaps. Offer more if they ask.</p>
<h3 id="do-doctors-want-to-see-my-food-logs">Do doctors want to see my food logs?</h3>
<p>Sometimes, for a specific question like protein intake or blood sugar patterns. Ask first. A one-line summary (&quot;about 70 g protein most days, estimated&quot;) is often enough.</p>
<h3 id="is-it-safe-to-email-my-health-data-to-my-doctor">Is it safe to email my health data to my doctor?</h3>
<p>Ask your clinic for its preferred secure route; a patient portal message is often the safest choice. Avoid public share links and check exports for personal details you do not need to send.</p>
