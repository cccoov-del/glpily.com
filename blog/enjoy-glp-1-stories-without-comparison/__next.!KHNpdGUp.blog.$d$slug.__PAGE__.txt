18:T1608,<p>GLP-1 stories on social media can make you feel less alone or more behind, sometimes in the same ten-minute scroll. You can keep the good part. Read each story as one person&#39;s slice of life, not a benchmark. Check whether it&#39;s sponsored. Then use the built-in Instagram and TikTok controls to shape a feed that leaves you feeling better, not worse.</p>
<p>This guide is about the <em>online</em> side of comparison: reels, transformation posts and &quot;month 3 update&quot; videos. (For the brunch-table version, see <a href="/blog/glp-1-friends-compare-weight-loss/">when friends compare GLP-1 weight loss</a>.)</p>
<h2 id="why-do-glp-1-stories-online-make-me-feel-behind">Why do GLP-1 stories online make me feel behind?</h2>
<p>It&#39;s 11:40 p.m. You open TikTok &quot;for one minute.&quot; Your feed serves you a woman who&#39;s &quot;down 60 in 5 months,&quot; a guy flexing at his goal weight and someone calling the medicine a &quot;miracle.&quot; You&#39;re on week 14 and the scale moved four pounds this month. You close the app feeling like you&#39;re doing it wrong.</p>
<p>A few things are going on:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>The feed picks the dramatic stories.</strong> Recommendation systems push posts people watch and share. A big transformation gets more views than &quot;month 4: honestly, kind of slow.&quot;</li>
<li><strong>You see their highlight, not their whole year.</strong> Nobody posts the week they had the flu, the dose they couldn&#39;t get or the plateau.</li>
<li><strong>Your timeline never matches theirs.</strong> Different drug, different dose, different start date, different body.</li>
<li><strong>Late at night, everything feels worse.</strong> That&#39;s not science, just a gentle reminder that 11:40 p.m. is not a great time to judge your progress.</li>
</ul>
<p>None of that makes the stories fake. It just means the format is selective. Our post on <a href="/blog/before-after-glp-1-stories-limitations/">why before-and-after stories don&#39;t tell the whole story</a> explains how a photo differs from a clinical result. This one is about how to <em>feel</em> while you scroll.</p>
<h2 id="how-should-i-read-a-glp-1-story">How should I read a GLP-1 story?</h2>
<p>Run a quick five-question check before a post gets to live in your head:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Question</th>
<th>Why it matters</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody><tr>
<td>Which medicine, and what dose?</td>
<td>&quot;On a GLP-1&quot; can mean very different drugs and doses</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>How long have they been on it?</td>
<td>Month 2 and month 14 aren&#39;t comparable</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>What else changed?</td>
<td>New job, gym, surgery, illness, breakup</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Is anything being sold?</td>
<td>Links, discount codes, &quot;DM me&quot;</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>What&#39;s missing?</td>
<td>Side effects, plateaus, cost, setbacks</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>
<p>If you can&#39;t answer most of these, the post is a mood, not data. Enjoy the mood. Leave the measuring out of it.</p>
<h2 id="how-can-i-tell-if-a-glp-1-post-is-sponsored">How can I tell if a GLP-1 post is sponsored?</h2>
<p>In the U.S., the Federal Trade Commission&#39;s Endorsement Guides (updated in 2023) set the rules for influencers and brands. In plain terms:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Connections must be disclosed.</strong> If a creator gets paid, gets free products or has a business relationship with a brand, that should be disclosed clearly in the post itself. A disclosure buried in a link, a hashtag cloud or a comment doesn&#39;t count as clear.</li>
<li><strong>Exceptional results need context.</strong> If an ad shows someone&#39;s unusually big result and the advertiser can&#39;t show people generally get similar results, the ad has to clearly disclose the generally expected results.</li>
</ul>
<p>So when a post from a telehealth company or product shows a dramatic before-and-after, look for the &quot;#ad&quot; or &quot;paid partnership&quot; label and for what&#39;s typical. If you can&#39;t find either, adjust how much weight you give it. Our guide on <a href="/blog/medical-influencer-glp-1-sponsorship-disclosure/">evaluating a medical influencer&#39;s sponsorship disclosure</a> covers this in detail, and <a href="/blog/glp-1-social-media-misinformation/">red flags in viral GLP-1 tips</a> helps with posts that include dosing &quot;hacks.&quot;</p>
<p>A good rule: stories are for connection. Dosing and treatment changes are for your prescriber.</p>
<h2 id="how-do-i-make-my-instagram-feed-less-triggering">How do I make my Instagram feed less triggering?</h2>
<p>Instagram has several built-in tools. Menu names can shift a little between app updates, but here&#39;s what Instagram describes:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Not interested.</strong> Tap the three dots on a suggested post and choose <em>Not interested</em>. Instagram says it will try to show you less of that kind of content, and choosing it in Explore also aims to reduce similar suggestions in Reels and Search.</li>
<li><strong>Mute.</strong> You can mute an account&#39;s posts, Stories or Notes without unfollowing. They aren&#39;t notified. This is perfect for the friend whose daily weigh-in posts you love in theory.</li>
<li><strong>Hidden Words.</strong> In <em>Settings</em>, under the suggested content section, add words, emojis or hashtags you don&#39;t want to see in recommended posts, like &quot;before and after&quot; or &quot;weigh-in.&quot;</li>
<li><strong>Following feed.</strong> Switch the feed dropdown to <em>Following</em> to see posts from accounts you chose, in time order, without as many algorithm surprises.</li>
</ol>

2a:Tf54,<h2 id="how-do-i-reset-my-tiktok-for-you-feed">How do I reset my TikTok For You feed?</h2>
<p>TikTok&#39;s controls, as described in its newsroom:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Not interested.</strong> Choose <em>Not interested</em> on a video to skip more from that creator or with the same sound.</li>
<li><strong>Filter keywords.</strong> Add words or hashtags to filter videos that use them out of your For You and Following feeds.</li>
<li><strong>Manage topics.</strong> Turn some broad topics up or down in your For You feed. TikTok says this changes how often a topic shows up, not whether it disappears.</li>
<li><strong>Refresh your For You feed.</strong> Reset recommendations so your feed acts as if you just signed up. TikTok says your follows and existing filters stay in place.</li>
</ol>
<p>Do this once and your feed starts to change within days. You&#39;re teaching the algorithm what kind of company you want.</p>
<h2 id="which-glp-1-stories-are-worth-following">Which GLP-1 stories are worth following?</h2>
<p>Not all content makes you feel worse. Look for creators who:</p>
<ul>
<li>Talk about the hard parts too, like side effects, cost and slow months.</li>
<li>Say which medicine they&#39;re on and how long they&#39;ve used it, without telling <em>you</em> what dose to take.</li>
<li>Celebrate things beyond the scale: energy, sleep, lab results, hobbies.</li>
<li>Say &quot;ask your doctor&quot; and mean it.</li>
<li>Clearly label sponsorships.</li>
</ul>
<p>Mixed stories are real stories. A feed where people share relief, frustration and plateaus makes everyone feel more normal.</p>
<h2 id="what-do-i-do-right-after-a-post-makes-me-feel-bad">What do I do right after a post makes me feel bad?</h2>
<p>Try this 3-step reset:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Name it.</strong> &quot;That post made me feel behind.&quot; Saying it is half the fix.</li>
<li><strong>Look at your own trend.</strong> Not today&#39;s number, your trend over weeks. If you track in GLPily, your weight chart and progress photos show <em>your</em> line, which is the only one that applies to you.</li>
<li><strong>Close the app on purpose.</strong> Go do one small thing, like drinking water, stretching or texting a friend.</li>
</ol>
<p>If you&#39;re truly worried that your progress is slow, read <a href="/blog/slow-weight-loss-on-glp-1/">slow weight loss on GLP-1</a> and write down questions for your prescriber. Let the reel make you feel less alone, not less successful.</p>
<h2 id="common-questions">Common questions</h2>
<h3 id="why-do-glp-1-transformation-videos-make-me-feel-bad">Why do GLP-1 transformation videos make me feel bad?</h3>
<p>Feeds tend to boost dramatic results, and you only see the highlight. You&#39;re comparing your full, everyday experience to someone else&#39;s best moment, often without knowing their medicine, dose or timeline.</p>
<h3 id="do-influencers-have-to-say-if-a-glp-1-post-is-sponsored">Do influencers have to say if a GLP-1 post is sponsored?</h3>
<p>Under the FTC&#39;s Endorsement Guides, endorsers should clearly disclose material connections, like payment or free products, in the post itself. Ads showing exceptional results should also disclose what results are generally expected.</p>
<h3 id="how-do-i-stop-seeing-before-and-after-posts-on-instagram-or-tiktok">How do I stop seeing before-and-after posts on Instagram or TikTok?</h3>
<p>Use <em>Not interested</em> on posts you don&#39;t want, mute specific accounts and add terms like &quot;before and after&quot; to Instagram&#39;s Hidden Words or TikTok&#39;s keyword filters. TikTok also lets you refresh your For You feed.</p>
<h3 id="should-i-follow-glp-1-accounts-at-all">Should I follow GLP-1 accounts at all?</h3>
<p>If they make you feel supported and informed, yes. Favor accounts that share mixed experiences, label sponsorships and send dosing questions to clinicians. Mute the ones that leave you feeling behind.</p>
