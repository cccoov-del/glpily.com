1a:T71a,<p>Constipation is listed among the possible side effects of some GLP-1 medicines, including semaglutide and tirzepatide injections. It can also have other causes. Do not assume a symptom is harmless or medication-related without considering the full picture with your healthcare professional.</p>
<p>This guide shares general information from NIDDK and MedlinePlus drug information. It is not a substitute for individualized treatment advice.</p>
<h2 id="general-habits-that-may-help">General habits that may help</h2>
<p>NIDDK describes general approaches to constipation that include getting enough fiber, drinking appropriate fluids, regular physical activity and a consistent bathroom routine. Fiber-containing foods can include oats and other whole grains, beans and lentils, fruit, vegetables, and nuts such as almonds. Increase fiber gradually rather than making a sudden major change, since individual tolerance differs.</p>
<p>Fluids help fiber work, but the right amount depends on factors such as health conditions, activity and clinician advice. If you have a fluid restriction or another condition that affects what you should drink, follow your care team’s guidance rather than a generic internet target.</p>
<p>Gentle movement may help some people, but choose activity appropriate for your abilities and health. Ask a clinician or pharmacist before starting laxatives, supplements or other treatments, particularly if symptoms are new, severe or persistent.</p>
<h2 id="keep-a-brief-symptom-record">Keep a brief symptom record</h2>
<p>If you speak with a clinician, it may help to note when symptoms began, how often you have bowel movements, changes from your normal pattern, any pain or vomiting, and what you have already tried. The purpose is to support a conversation, not to self-diagnose.</p>
1b:T414,<h2 id="when-to-get-medical-advice">When to get medical advice</h2>
<p>Contact a healthcare professional if constipation persists, worsens or worries you. See a doctor right away if constipation comes with blood in your stool or bleeding from the rectum, constant abdominal pain, inability to pass gas, vomiting, fever, lower back pain, or weight loss you can&#39;t explain. Severe stomach pain that won&#39;t go away should also be checked promptly, and your medicine&#39;s information lists its own warning signs. Follow the safety instructions for your medicine and local urgent-care guidance.</p>
<h2 id="questions-worth-asking">Questions worth asking</h2>
<ul>
<li>Could my medication or another factor contribute to this symptom?</li>
<li>What changes are suitable for my health history?</li>
<li>Is a fiber supplement or over-the-counter medicine appropriate for me?</li>
<li>Which symptoms mean I need urgent help?</li>
</ul>
<p>A simple symptom log can make it easier to explain what is happening and ask for the support you need.</p>
