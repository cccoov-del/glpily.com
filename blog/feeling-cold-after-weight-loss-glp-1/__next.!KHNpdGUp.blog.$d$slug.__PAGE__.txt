1b:Tc2f,<p>Feeling colder than usual after losing weight can be surprising, and some people mention it while using GLP-1 medicines. But feeling cold is not listed as a side effect in the US labels for Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro or Zepbound, and it isn&#39;t specific to these medicines. Body composition, eating less, changes in energy balance, anemia, thyroid problems, illness and simply the weather can all affect how warm you feel.</p>
<h2 id="why-weight-loss-may-change-how-warm-you-feel">Why weight loss may change how warm you feel</h2>
<p>Body fat helps insulate the body, so having less of it may make cold air feel sharper. Eating less matters too: in a 6-month randomized trial of calorie restriction in adults with overweight, core body temperature fell slightly in the calorie-restriction groups and the body burned somewhat less energy than expected for its size. That trial didn&#39;t involve GLP-1 medicines, but it shows the body can adapt when food intake drops. This kind of adaptation is normal and different from “damaging” your metabolism (more on that in <a href="/blog/glp-1-damaged-metabolism-myth/">the damaged-metabolism myth</a>).</p>
<p>Still, cold intolerance that is persistent or comes on suddenly can have medical causes unrelated to weight change, such as an underactive thyroid (hypothyroidism), anemia, circulation problems like Raynaud phenomenon, or very low food intake. Feeling cold isn&#39;t proof that the medicine is working, or that weight loss is “too fast.”</p>
<h2 id="what-to-notice">What to notice</h2>
<p>Think about whether you feel cold only in certain places or nearly all the time. Also note fatigue, dizziness, paleness, shortness of breath, hair or skin changes, constipation, fingers that turn white or blue in the cold, or how much you&#39;ve been eating. These details help a clinician decide whether nutrition, thyroid function, anemia or something else needs checking. Several of these symptoms overlap with <a href="/blog/fatigue-tiredness-glp-1/">fatigue on GLP-1</a>, so it&#39;s worth mentioning them together.</p>
<h2 id="what-may-help-day-to-day">What may help day to day</h2>
<p>Layering clothing, warm drinks, regular movement and regular meals with enough protein are reasonable comfort measures. They don&#39;t replace an assessment if the symptom persists or comes with other changes. Avoid self-prescribing iron, thyroid supplements or high-dose vitamins based on a social-media explanation; iron, for example, can be harmful if you don&#39;t need it.</p>
<h2 id="when-to-ask-for-medical-advice">When to ask for medical advice</h2>
<p>Contact a clinician if feeling cold is new, persistent or severe, or comes with tiredness, unexplained weakness, faintness, a racing or irregular heartbeat, or if you&#39;re struggling to eat enough. Seek urgent care for confusion, chest pain, difficulty breathing, or fainting.</p>
<p>The honest answer is that feeling colder can happen during weight change, but a GLP-1 medicine is only one possible part of the picture. A proper check is more useful than assuming every change is an expected side effect.</p>
