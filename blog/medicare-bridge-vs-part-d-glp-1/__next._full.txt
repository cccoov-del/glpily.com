15:T1943,<p>On Medicare, the reason a GLP-1 is prescribed decides who pays for it. Prescriptions for <strong>type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular risk reduction, sleep apnea or MASH</strong> go through your regular <strong>Part D plan</strong>. Prescriptions for <strong>weight management alone</strong> may qualify for the temporary <strong>Medicare GLP-1 Bridge</strong>, which charges $50 a month and started July 1, 2026. The two paths have different rules, different costs, and they count differently toward your yearly out-of-pocket cap.</p>
<p>Here&#39;s how it plays out. Two neighbors are at the same pharmacy picking up the same brand of pen. One pays $50 through the Bridge. The other pays their Part D plan&#39;s coinsurance. Nobody made a mistake. Their prescriptions were written for different reasons.</p>
<h2 id="why-does-medicare-have-two-pathways-for-glp-1s">Why does Medicare have two pathways for GLP-1s?</h2>
<p>By law, Medicare Part D can&#39;t cover drugs used specifically for weight loss, as KFF explains. Part D plans <strong>can</strong> cover GLP-1s for other approved uses, like type 2 diabetes. Once Wegovy gained a heart-risk indication and Zepbound gained a sleep apnea indication, those uses became coverable too.</p>
<p>The <strong>Medicare GLP-1 Bridge</strong> fills the weight-management gap for now. CMS describes it as a demonstration that runs &quot;outside the Part D benefit&#39;s coverage and payment flow.&quot; It has its own central processor that handles prior authorization and claims, so your Part D plan isn&#39;t the one making the decision. CMS says the Bridge runs through December 31, 2027, and Bridge prior authorizations stay valid through that date unless you switch GLP-1s.</p>

<p>For 2027, Medicare.gov lists a maximum Part D deductible of $700 and an out-of-pocket threshold of $2,400.</p>
<h2 id="six-scenarios-which-pathway-applies">Six scenarios: which pathway applies?</h2>
<p>These are made-up examples based on the CMS rules. They&#39;re not coverage decisions, and your prescriber and plan make the actual call.</p>
<h3 id="1-rosa-type-2-diabetes-ozempic">1. Rosa: type 2 diabetes, Ozempic</h3>
<p>Rosa takes Ozempic for type 2 diabetes. Her BMI is 36. <strong>Pathway: Part D.</strong> CMS says people with type 2 diabetes aren&#39;t eligible for the Bridge, even if they meet the BMI criteria, and even if their Part D plan doesn&#39;t cover a GLP-1 for diabetes. Her costs follow her plan&#39;s tier and count toward her $2,100 cap.</p>
<h3 id="2-frank-a-past-heart-attack-wegovy-to-lower-heart-risk">2. Frank: a past heart attack, Wegovy to lower heart risk</h3>
<p>Frank had a heart attack, and his BMI is 29. His cardiologist prescribes Wegovy to reduce the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events. <strong>Pathway: Part D.</strong> CMS tells plans to route a GLP-1 prescribed for cardiovascular risk reduction to Part D, &quot;even if weight reduction is also a purpose.&quot; See our <a href="/blog/wegovy-heart-health-select-trial/">SELECT trial explainer</a> for where that indication comes from.</p>
<h3 id="3-linda-moderate-to-severe-sleep-apnea-zepbound">3. Linda: moderate-to-severe sleep apnea, Zepbound</h3>
<p>Linda has moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea and obesity. <strong>Pathway: Part D.</strong> OSA is one of the conditions that excludes someone from the Bridge, and Zepbound for OSA is a use Part D can cover. If her plan doesn&#39;t list it, she and her prescriber can ask the plan for a formulary exception. More on the indication in our <a href="/blog/tirzepatide-sleep-apnea-evidence/">tirzepatide and sleep apnea post</a>.</p>
<h3 id="4-mike-bmi-37-no-diabetes-weight-management">4. Mike: BMI 37, no diabetes, weight management</h3>
<p>Mike&#39;s prescriber wants to treat his obesity, and he has no diabetes, OSA or MASH. <strong>Pathway: the Bridge</strong>, if the prior authorization is approved. He&#39;d pay $50 per monthly fill of Wegovy, Foundayo or the Zepbound KwikPen. Zepbound vials or single-dose pens aren&#39;t included.</p>
<h3 id="5-ann-bmi-28-with-prediabetes">5. Ann: BMI 28 with prediabetes</h3>
<p>Ann has prediabetes and a BMI of 28. <strong>Pathway: the Bridge, if the prescription is for weight management.</strong> One criterion is a BMI of 27 or higher <strong>plus</strong> prediabetes, a prior heart attack or stroke, or symptomatic peripheral artery disease. Without one of those conditions, a BMI of 28 wouldn&#39;t qualify. The <a href="/blog/medicare-glp-1-bridge-eligibility-checklist/">eligibility checklist</a> covers every criterion.</p>
<h3 id="6-joe-already-had-a-glp-1-through-part-d-in-2026">6. Joe: already had a GLP-1 through Part D in 2026</h3>
<p>Joe&#39;s Part D plan covered a GLP-1 for him earlier in 2026. <strong>Pathway: Part D for the rest of 2026.</strong> CMS says anyone who got a listed GLP-1 through Part D in calendar year 2026 can&#39;t use the Bridge in 2026. That includes Ozempic, Mounjaro, Wegovy, Zepbound, Rybelsus, Trulicity, Victoza, Saxenda and Foundayo. Medicare.gov says such people can keep getting their GLP-1 through their plan. CMS says the lookback rule for 2027 hasn&#39;t been decided.</p>

1c:Te0e,<h2 id="is-50-on-the-bridge-always-cheaper-than-part-d">Is $50 on the Bridge always cheaper than Part D?</h2>
<p>Usually, but not always. Run the numbers:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Bridge:</strong> $50 × 12 months = $600 a year. None of it counts toward your Part D cap.</li>
<li><strong>Part D:</strong> You pay your plan&#39;s cost-sharing, but every dollar counts toward the $2,100 cap for 2026. Once you hit the cap, covered Part D drugs cost <strong>$0</strong> for the rest of the year.</li>
</ul>
<p>Say you already take other expensive Part D drugs and reach the cap by spring. A GLP-1 covered under Part D would then cost nothing, while a Bridge GLP-1 would still cost $50 a month. In most cases, though, the reason for the prescription decides the pathway, not which one is cheaper. Medicare.gov also notes that Bridge copays don&#39;t show up on your Explanation of Benefits and can&#39;t be spread out with the Medicare Prescription Payment Plan. Keep your own receipts.</p>
<h2 id="how-do-you-find-out-which-pathway-you-39-re-on">How do you find out which pathway you&#39;re on?</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Ask your prescriber</strong> what diagnosis the prescription is written for. That&#39;s what routes it.</li>
<li><strong>For Part D,</strong> look up the drug in your plan&#39;s formulary and check the tier and codes. Our guide on <a href="/blog/read-health-plan-formulary-glp-1/">reading a formulary</a> explains how.</li>
<li><strong>For the Bridge,</strong> call 1-800-MEDICARE (TTY 1-877-486-2048) to check eligibility or prior authorization status. Approved people get a letter confirming coverage under the Bridge.</li>
<li><strong>Write it down:</strong> the pathway, the approval date and how long it lasts. A dose change or a switch to a different GLP-1 can mean new paperwork. Logging your refills and dose changes in one place, whether that&#39;s a notebook or an app like GLPily, makes those calls easier.</li>
</ol>
<p>Program details can change, especially for a demonstration program. Check the CMS and Medicare.gov pages before making a decision about costs. For the full program overview, read <a href="/blog/medicare-glp-1-bridge-2026/">Medicare GLP-1 Bridge in 2026</a>.</p>
<h2 id="common-questions">Common questions</h2>
<h3 id="does-the-medicare-glp-1-bridge-cover-ozempic-or-mounjaro">Does the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge cover Ozempic or Mounjaro?</h3>
<p>No. The Bridge covers Wegovy (pen or tablet), Foundayo and the Zepbound KwikPen. Ozempic and Mounjaro are diabetes medicines covered through regular Part D plans when they&#39;re on the plan&#39;s formulary.</p>
<h3 id="does-the-50-bridge-copay-count-toward-the-2-100-part-d-out-of-pocket-cap">Does the $50 Bridge copay count toward the $2,100 Part D out-of-pocket cap?</h3>
<p>No. CMS says no part of the $50 copay counts toward your Part D true out-of-pocket costs. The Part D deductible doesn&#39;t apply, and Extra Help doesn&#39;t lower it.</p>
<h3 id="if-my-part-d-plan-doesn-39-t-cover-zepbound-for-sleep-apnea-can-i-use-the-bridge-instead">If my Part D plan doesn&#39;t cover Zepbound for sleep apnea, can I use the Bridge instead?</h3>
<p>No. CMS says people with moderate-to-severe OSA aren&#39;t eligible for the Bridge, even if their plan doesn&#39;t cover the drug. You and your prescriber can ask your Part D plan for a coverage exception and appeal if it&#39;s denied.</p>
<h3 id="when-does-the-medicare-glp-1-bridge-end">When does the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge end?</h3>
<p>CMS says the Bridge started July 1, 2026, and was extended through December 31, 2027. Check the CMS page for later changes.</p>
