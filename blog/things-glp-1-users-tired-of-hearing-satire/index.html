12 Things GLP-1 Users Are Tired of Hearing (A Satirical List)

A clearly labelled satire: twelve comments people on GLP-1 medicines hear far too often, what they really mean, and what to say instead. Laugh, then send it to your uncle.

♡By GLPily Editorial Team
Oct 27, 2026

'How are you?' is always fine ♡

On this page

  1. The list
  2. Bonus round: the comments that are actually fine
  3. Why we wrote this
  4. Common questions

This is satire. It's a humorous, opinionated list of things GLP-1 users are tired of hearing, written by the GLPily editorial team. It's not a survey, not a quote from any real person and not a claim that everyone hears these exact lines. The joke is on unsolicited commentary, never on people looking for care. Each item comes with a "translation" and a kinder line to use instead.

Grab a snack (or don't, nobody's watching). Here we go.

The list

1. "Isn't that cheating?"

Translation: "I believe weight loss only counts if it's miserable."

Fun fact: Cheating needs a game. Health care doesn't have a scoreboard, and the labels for GLP-1 weight medicines say they're used with a reduced-calorie diet and more physical activity. We wrote a whole opinion piece on the cheating question, because apparently it needed one.

Say instead: "How's it going for you?"

2. "Are you allowed to eat that?"

Translation: "I've appointed myself hall monitor of your lunch."

There's no GLP-1 food police, though there is a person on the medicine who would like to finish their sandwich in peace.

Say instead: Nothing. Pass the napkins.

3. "So how much have you lost?"

Translation: "Please share private health data at this barbecue."

Numbers belong to the person, their scale and their prescriber. If they want to share, they will.

Say instead: "You seem really happy lately."

4. "My cousin's coworker's neighbor stopped and gained it all back."

Translation: "Here's a story with four layers of hearsay and zero context."

Sure, what happens after stopping a GLP-1 is a fair question. It's also one with actual research behind it. We cover it in what happens when you stop semaglutide. The neighbor isn't in the study.

Say instead: "I hope it's going well for you."

5. "Isn't Ozempic just for diabetics?"

Translation: "I learned about this from a headline in 2023."

Close, but not the whole story. Ozempic is FDA-approved for adults with type 2 diabetes, including to lower certain heart and kidney risks. Wegovy contains the same ingredient, semaglutide, and has its own approvals, including long-term weight management and lowering heart-event risk in some adults. Different brand, different label, same family. Our Mounjaro vs Ozempic explainer untangles more names.

Say instead: "Oh, interesting. What's it like?" (Only if they seem to want to talk.)

6. "You'll have to take it forever, you know."

Translation: "I've confused 'chronic condition' with 'personal failure.'"

Lots of people take medicines for blood pressure, cholesterol or thyroid long-term, and nobody says "forever" in that ominous voice. How long someone stays on treatment is up to them and their clinician.

Say instead: Nothing about their future. Ask about their weekend.

7. "Watch out for Ozempic face!"

Translation: "I'd like to comment on your face now."

Facial changes after weight loss are a real topic, and we covered what's real and what's hype about "Ozempic face". It is still a strange thing to say to someone over dessert.

Say instead: "You look great." Or, radical idea, say nothing about their face at all.

8. "Can I try one of your shots?"

Translation: "I'd like to borrow your prescription medicine like a lipstick."

This one isn't just awkward. The FDA-approved labels for Ozempic and Wegovy say never to share a pen between people, even if the needle is changed, because of the risk of spreading blood-borne infections. And a prescription is written for one person. The FDA has also warned about the risks of unapproved copies sold online.

Say instead: "Do you like your doctor? Maybe I should ask mine about it."

9. "I could never inject myself."

Translation: "Let me make your treatment about my fear of needles."

Totally fair to feel that way! It's just not the most helpful thing to say to someone who does it every week. If needles worry you, our guide on needle anxiety is surprisingly gentle.

Say instead: "That takes some guts. Respect."

10. "I just did it the old-fashioned way."

Translation: "Please applaud my metabolism."

Wonderful, truly. Bodies differ a lot, and a 2020 international consensus statement noted that body-weight regulation isn't entirely under voluntary control. Your way worked for your body. Theirs is working for theirs.

Say instead: "Whatever works for you, I'm glad it's working."

11. "You're not eating much. Are you sick?"

Translation: "I noticed your plate before I noticed your mood."

Sometimes this is genuine worry, and that's sweet. But a small plate isn't a symptom. If someone really does seem unwell, ask privately and kindly, and encourage them to call their prescriber.

Say instead: "Everything OK? No pressure, I'm just checking."

12. "I saw a video that says you should [insert dose hack here]."

Translation: "A stranger with a ring light has a theory about your medicine."

Dosing is between a person, their label and their prescriber, full stop. The FDA has reported people ending up in the hospital after measuring doses wrong. If you see a viral hack, our red flags in viral GLP-1 tips guide is your friend.

Say instead: "Saw something online, but your doctor knows best."

Bonus round: the comments that are actually fine

To be fair to everyone trying their best, here are lines most people are happy to hear:

Comment Why it works
"How are you feeling?" Opens a door without pushing anyone through it
"I'm proud of you for taking care of yourself." Praises the effort, not the body
"Want to go for a walk after dinner?" An invitation, not an inspection
"Let me know if you want to talk about it." Lets the person decide
"Do you want leftovers to take home?" Peak love language, honestly

Why we wrote this

Because people on GLP-1 medicines hear a lot of commentary, and humor helps. The more serious version, with research on how weight bias affects health care, lives in our post on being judged for using GLP-1. Want more fun? The GLP-1 internet bingo card is a good next stop.

If you're the one who's said a few of these, don't panic. Nobody's keeping score (that's kind of the theme). Just swap in "How are you feeling?" next time.

Common questions

Is this list based on real quotes?

No. It's satire written by the GLPily editorial team. The lines are made up and exaggerated to make a point about unsolicited comments. They aren't quotes from real people or survey results.

What's the best thing to say to someone on a GLP-1?

"How are you feeling?" usually works, because it lets the person decide how much to share. Skip questions about weight numbers, food and dose.

Is it really unsafe to share a GLP-1 pen?

Yes. The FDA-approved labels for Ozempic and Wegovy say a pen must never be shared between people, even if the needle is changed, because of the risk of passing on blood-borne infections.

How do I respond if someone keeps commenting?

Pick one short, calm line, like "I'd rather not talk about it," and repeat it as needed. You don't owe anyone a longer explanation.
