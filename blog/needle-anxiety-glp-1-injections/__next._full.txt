17:T1427,<p>How do you get past needle anxiety for a weekly GLP-1 shot? Start by learning exactly what your pen does, then practice one calming technique that fits your reaction (applied tension if you feel faint, slow breathing if you feel panicky), and build a short, predictable shot-day routine. Fear of needles is common and treatable, and it is not a test of willpower.</p>
<p>You know the moment. The pen is on the counter, you have read the leaflet twice, your thumb is hovering, and your brain is suddenly very interested in reorganizing the spice drawer. Let&#39;s make that moment shorter.</p>
<h2 id="is-needle-fear-really-that-common">Is needle fear really that common?</h2>
<p>Very. A 2019 systematic review in the <em>Journal of Advanced Nursing</em> (McLenon and Rogers, 119 studies) found needle fear in 20% to 30% of young adults and 20% to 50% of adolescents, with fear generally easing with age. It was also a real barrier: 16% of adult patients in the included studies had skipped a flu shot because of it.</p>
<p>In people with long-term conditions, a 2021 scoping review in <em>PLOS ONE</em> found reported needle fear in diabetes ranging from 0.2% to 80%, a huge spread that mostly reflects different ways of measuring it. The takeaway is simple: whatever you feel, plenty of people on weekly injections feel it too.</p>
<h2 id="what-does-a-glp-1-pen-actually-feel-like">What does a GLP-1 pen actually feel like?</h2>
<p>A big chunk of needle fear is the unknown. The official Instructions for Use (IFU) that come with each pen are surprisingly reassuring once you read them with this in mind. A few examples, straight from the current labels:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Device</th>
<th>What you do</th>
<th>What you notice</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody><tr>
<td>Zepbound single-dose pen</td>
<td>Place the clear base flat on the skin, unlock, press and hold the purple button up to 10 seconds</td>
<td>First click means the injection started, second click means it finished; a gray plunger shows when done</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Wegovy single-dose pen</td>
<td>Press the needle cover firmly against the skin and hold until the yellow bar stops moving</td>
<td>You hear 2 clicks during the injection</td>

</tr>

<p>The 4 mm is the needle length, and the 32G number describes how thin it is: in needle gauges, a higher number means a thinner needle.</p>
<p>The Zepbound IFU also says something people often miss: <strong>another person can give the injection</strong>, and the back of the upper arm is a site to use only when someone else injects. If self-injecting is the hardest part, a partner, friend or nurse can do it while you learn.</p>
<p>Always follow the IFU for your exact device, because pens differ, and ask your pharmacist or prescriber&#39;s office to walk you through it before your first dose. That one conversation removes a lot of &quot;what if.&quot;</p>
<h2 id="two-calming-techniques-the-nhs-teaches">Two calming techniques the NHS teaches</h2>
<p>Guy&#39;s and St Thomas&#39; NHS Foundation Trust in London publishes a needle-phobia guide that matches two techniques to two kinds of reaction.</p>
<h3 id="if-you-feel-faint-applied-tension">If you feel faint: applied tension</h3>
<p>Some people&#39;s blood pressure dips at the sight or thought of needles, which can cause lightheadedness or fainting. Applied tension pushes it back up:</p>
<ol>
<li>Sit somewhere comfortable.</li>
<li>Tense the muscles in your arms, upper body and legs.</li>
<li>Hold for 10 to 15 seconds, or until you feel warmth rising in your face.</li>
<li>Release and sit normally for 20 to 30 seconds.</li>
<li>Repeat until you have done it 5 times.</li>
</ol>
<p>The guide suggests practicing three times a day for about a week before facing the needle. Skip tensing your face and head if you get headaches, and be careful with any body part that has health problems. Research backs this up: a 2011 study in <em>Health Psychology</em> found applied tension reduced fainting-type symptoms in blood donors, especially women with stronger needle fear.</p>
<p>If you have ever fainted around needles, tell your prescriber&#39;s office and inject sitting or lying down. Our post on <a href="/blog/dizziness-on-glp-1/">dizziness on GLP-1</a> covers other reasons you might feel lightheaded.</p>
<h3 id="if-you-feel-panicky-slow-belly-breathing">If you feel panicky: slow belly breathing</h3>
<p>If your heart races or your chest feels tight but you do not feel faint:</p>
<ol>
<li>Sit upright but relaxed; let your shoulders and jaw drop.</li>
<li>Put one hand low on your belly.</li>
<li>Take a long, slow, gentle breath in through your nose and out through your mouth, breathing down into your belly only as deeply as is comfortable.</li>
<li>Do 5 breaths.</li>
</ol>
<p>Same plan: practice three times a day for a week, then use it on shot day. The NHS guide notes both techniques are safe in pregnancy and with most medical conditions.</p>

20:Tfe4,<h2 id="build-a-fear-ladder">Build a fear ladder</h2>
<p>The same NHS guide recommends a &quot;fear ladder&quot;: list needle-related steps from easiest to hardest and climb one rung at a time. A GLP-1 version might be:</p>
<ol>
<li>Read the IFU for your pen.</li>
<li>Watch the manufacturer&#39;s official instruction video for your device.</li>
<li>Hold the capped pen and look at its parts.</li>
<li>Do the setup steps without injecting, as far as your IFU allows, with your pharmacist&#39;s OK.</li>
<li>Have someone else give the first injection while you watch.</li>
<li>Do your own injection with a support person in the room.</li>
<li>Do it on your own.</li>
</ol>
<p>Move up only when the current rung feels manageable. Going slowly is still going.</p>
<h2 id="design-a-shot-day-routine-that-runs-on-autopilot">Design a shot-day routine that runs on autopilot</h2>
<p>Anxiety loves open questions (&quot;when should I do it, where, what if…&quot;). A routine closes them.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Same day, same time.</strong> Pick a calm slot, not right before a stressful meeting.</li>
<li><strong>Same setup.</strong> Same chair, same playlist, same order of steps. Familiar beats brave.</li>
<li><strong>A distraction ready.</strong> A podcast, a funny video, counting backward from 100 by sevens.</li>
<li><strong>A small reward.</strong> A walk, a favorite tea, ten minutes of a show.</li>
<li><strong>A reminder you trust.</strong> GLPily&#39;s shot log can remind you on your shot day, rotate injection sites for you and keep a history, so you are not doing mental math while nervous. For more on systems, see <a href="/blog/how-to-track-glp-1-shots/">how to track your GLP-1 shots</a>.</li>
</ul>
<p>What not to do: change your dose, needle, injection technique or schedule to dodge the fear. Those are prescriber decisions. If bruising or bumps after a shot are part of what worries you, <a href="/blog/glp-1-injection-site-reactions/">injection site reactions on GLP-1</a> explains what is typical.</p>
<h2 id="when-anxiety-is-blocking-treatment">When anxiety is blocking treatment</h2>
<p>If you keep postponing doses, tell your prescriber plainly. It is useful information, not a confession. They can review technique, consider whether a different device fits you better, or discuss whether a non-injectable option is suitable. Oral GLP-1 options exist, including Rybelsus for type 2 diabetes and the newer oral weight-management options covered in our guides to <a href="/blog/glp-1-pills-vs-injections/">GLP-1 pills vs injections</a> and <a href="/blog/oral-wegovy-vs-injection/">oral Wegovy vs the injection</a>. Whether one suits you depends on your diagnosis, coverage and history.</p>
<p>For stronger fear, cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) is the usual recommended treatment for needle phobia, according to the NHS guide.</p>
<h2 id="common-questions">Common questions</h2>
<h3 id="do-glp-1-injections-hurt">Do GLP-1 injections hurt?</h3>
<p>The NHS needle-phobia guide notes that a needle usually hurts only a little. GLP-1 pens use short, thin needles; the Ozempic and Wegovy FlexTouch labels list 32G 4 mm pen needles. If injections hurt a lot or keep bruising, ask your pharmacist to check your technique.</p>
<h3 id="can-someone-else-give-me-my-glp-1-shot">Can someone else give me my GLP-1 shot?</h3>
<p>Yes. The Zepbound Instructions for Use say another person can inject, and that the back of the upper arm should be used only when someone else gives the injection. Check your own pen&#39;s instructions.</p>
<h3 id="what-if-i-faint-around-needles">What if I faint around needles?</h3>
<p>Tell your prescriber&#39;s office, inject sitting or lying down, and practice applied tension for a week beforehand. If you faint and are injured, or do not recover quickly, get medical help.</p>
<h3 id="is-there-a-glp-1-that-is-not-a-shot">Is there a GLP-1 that is not a shot?</h3>
<p>Yes, some are tablets. Whether a pill is an option for you depends on what it is approved for, your health history and coverage, so ask your prescriber.</p>
