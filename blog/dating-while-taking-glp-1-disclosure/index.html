Dating While Taking a GLP-1: Do You Have to Tell Them?

First dates, fifth dates, moving in: how much to share about your GLP-1 treatment, with scripts for each stage and the few label facts that do become a couple's business.

Dating while taking a GLP-1? You don't have to tell anyone on a first date, or on a fifth one. Your medication is private health information, and you get to decide when it's relevant. For most people that moment comes when trust has built up, or when the treatment starts to affect shared plans like meals, travel, sex and contraception, or having kids. This guide walks through each dating stage, with scripts you can borrow.

Do you have to tell someone you're dating that you take a GLP-1?

No. There's no rule, legal or social, that says you have to share what's in your medicine cabinet with someone you just met. People don't open with their antidepressant, their asthma inhaler or their thyroid pill either.

What does make sense is sharing when it becomes relevant to the relationship:

  • You're spending a lot of time together, and they're noticing small meals or skipped drinks.
  • You're staying over, and your weekly pen or daily pills are in your bag.
  • You're having sex and relying on birth control pills.
  • You're talking seriously about a future together, including pregnancy.

Before that point, privacy isn't dishonesty. It's just the normal pace of getting to know someone.

A stage-by-stage disclosure guide (with scripts)

Stage Do they need to know? What you might say
First date No "I'm not super hungry. Want to split something?"
A few dates in Optional "I eat small these days. It's a health thing I'm working on with my doctor."
Staying over Optional, but useful "That's my medication. I take it weekly. Happy to tell you more if you're curious."
Exclusive / sexually active Worth talking about if it affects contraception "One of my meds can affect how well birth control pills work, so let's talk about a backup."
Planning a future or kids Yes, practically "My medicine has specific instructions for pregnancy planning. Can we look at that together?"

You can share the what without the why. "I take a medication that affects my appetite" is complete and honest. You don't have to talk about your weight, your history or your numbers unless you want to.

Making early dates easier with a smaller appetite

Many first dates are built around food and drinks, which isn't ideal if you fill up fast or are pacing alcohol. A few easy fixes:

  • Pick activity dates. Coffee and a walk, a museum, mini golf, a bookstore, a market, a cooking class where you only nibble. They're all good for conversation and low on pressure.
  • Order shareables. Small plates make a light appetite invisible.
  • Have a drink plan. A 2025 joint advisory from four nutrition and obesity medicine societies suggests keeping alcohol to a minimum on GLP-1 treatment, because it can worsen nausea and reflux. Ask your prescriber what's sensible for you. A mocktail or sparkling water with lime works in any bar. Our non-alcoholic drinks guide has ideas.
  • Have a no-explanation line ready. "I had a late lunch" is a perfectly fine thing to say. So is "I'm good, thanks!"

When it becomes a couple's business: the label facts

Most of your treatment is your business. A few label instructions affect a partner directly, and they're worth knowing before things get serious.

Birth control pills and some GLP-1s. The Zepbound label tells people using oral hormonal contraceptives to switch to a non-oral method, or add a barrier method like condoms, for 4 weeks after starting and 4 weeks after each dose increase. The Foundayo label gives similar advice for 30 days after starting and after each increase. Not every GLP-1 label has this instruction, so check yours. See tirzepatide and birth control pills for the details.

Pregnancy planning. The Wegovy label says to stop the medicine at least 2 months before a planned pregnancy, because semaglutide stays in the body for a long time. The Zepbound label says weight loss isn't recommended during pregnancy and the medicine should be stopped once pregnancy is recognized. If a future together includes kids, that's a conversation for you, your partner and your prescriber. Our GLP-1 and fertility guide covers what's known.

Bringing these up doesn't mean telling your whole story. "My medicine affects how well the pill works for a few weeks at a time, so let's use condoms too" is a plain, practical sentence.

How to tell a partner (if and when you want to)

  1. Pick a calm moment, not mid-dinner when you've just pushed your plate away.
  2. Lead with what it means for them, if anything: "It's why I eat small," or "It's why we need a backup method."
  3. Set your boundary on details. "I'm happy to answer questions, but I'd rather not talk numbers."
  4. Invite support, specifically. "It helps if you don't comment on my plate," or "Can you be okay with ordering apps to share?"

Reading their reaction

Their response tells you a lot.

Green flags: curiosity without judgment. "Thanks for telling me." Asking what would help. Respecting a "not tonight" on questions.

Yellow flags: jokes that land badly, or questions about your weight. People often repeat what they've heard online. A calm correction is worth one try.

Red flags: calling it "cheating" or "the easy way," pressuring you to stop or change your dose, monitoring what you eat, or using your treatment against you in an argument. Those reactions say something about the relationship, not about whether your care is legitimate. Our piece on the stigma around weight-loss medication explains where those attitudes come from.

And if a partner ever tries to control your medicine, your food or your body, that's a safety issue, not a dating quirk. In the US, the National Domestic Violence Hotline is at 1-800-799-7233, or text START to 88788.

Common questions

Should I tell my date I'm on Ozempic or Wegovy?

Only when you want to, or when it becomes relevant, like meals, staying over, contraception or planning a future. On early dates, "I'm not that hungry" is enough.

Is it dishonest not to tell someone I'm dating?

No. Health information is private, and people share it as trust builds. It becomes important to discuss when it affects your partner directly, for example contraception or pregnancy planning.

Can a GLP-1 affect birth control?

Some labels say so. Zepbound's label advises a non-oral method or a backup barrier method for 4 weeks after starting and after each dose increase, and Foundayo's says 30 days. Check your own label with your pharmacist.

What if my partner thinks GLP-1 medicines are "cheating"?

You can explain once, calmly, that obesity is treated as a chronic disease and that this is your medical care. If they keep shaming or pressuring you, take that seriously as information about the relationship.
