18:Tb74,<p>GLP-1 medicines can cause nausea, vomiting or diarrhea. When fluid losses are significant, or you are drinking much less than usual, dehydration can develop. US prescribing information for Ozempic, Wegovy and Zepbound warns that acute kidney injury has been reported, sometimes needing dialysis, mostly in people whose gastrointestinal side effects led to dehydration. In the trials it was uncommon: for example, 0.5% of people on Zepbound versus 0.2% on placebo. That does not mean everyone taking a GLP-1 medicine is at high kidney risk, but persistent fluid loss deserves attention.</p>
<h2 id="what-dehydration-can-feel-like">What dehydration can feel like</h2>
<p>MedlinePlus lists signs such as thirst, a dry or sticky mouth, less or darker urine, headache, muscle cramps, and, when more serious, dizziness, confusion, a fast heartbeat or very little urine. These symptoms are not specific, and more serious dehydration can affect blood pressure, heart rate and kidney function. Older adults and people with kidney disease, heart conditions, or medicines that affect fluid balance may need individualized guidance.</p>
<h2 id="why-just-drink-more-is-not-always-enough">Why &quot;just drink more&quot; is not always enough</h2>
<p>If <a href="/blog/vomiting-on-glp-1-warning-signs/">vomiting</a> or <a href="/blog/diarrhea-glp-1-medicines/">diarrhea</a> continues, replacing fluids may be difficult, and the right approach depends on your symptoms and medical situation. If you have been told to limit fluids, do not override that advice. Avoid treating a universal online water target as a substitute for individualized guidance; everyday habits are covered in our <a href="/blog/hydration-on-glp-1/">hydration guide</a>. A clinician may need to assess hydration, kidney function, other medicines and whether the treatment plan should change; the labels advise monitoring kidney function in people with significant fluid-losing side effects.</p>
<h2 id="when-to-get-medical-help">When to get medical help</h2>
<p>Contact a healthcare professional promptly if you cannot keep fluids down, have persistent vomiting or diarrhea, urinate much less than usual, or feel increasingly dizzy or weak. Seek urgent care for fainting, confusion, severe weakness, very little or no urine, or other signs of serious illness. Do not wait for routine blood work if symptoms suggest significant dehydration.</p>
<h2 id="keep-a-useful-record">Keep a useful record</h2>
<p>Note when symptoms began, how often vomiting or diarrhea occurs, whether drinks stay down, changes in urination, and any known kidney condition or fluid restriction. Bring your medicine list to the conversation. These details help a clinician assess risk far better than a vague report of &quot;feeling dehydrated.&quot;</p>
<p>The aim is to catch fluid loss early and address the cause, not to panic over every dry mouth or try to manage serious symptoms at home.</p>
