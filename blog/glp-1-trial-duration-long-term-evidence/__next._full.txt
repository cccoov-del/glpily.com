15:T16a3,<p>Most GLP-1 weight-loss approvals rest on trials lasting about 68 to 72 weeks, which is roughly a year and a half. Longer studies now exist, including a two-year semaglutide trial, a three-year tirzepatide analysis in people with prediabetes, and a heart outcomes trial that followed people for over three years on average, and each timeline answers a different kind of question.</p>
<p>If you are planning to take a GLP-1 for years, that gap between &quot;a year and a half of data&quot; and &quot;the rest of your life&quot; is worth understanding. Not to scare you; just so you know which questions are well answered and which are still being worked on.</p>

<p>These durations are not random. Each was chosen to fit its endpoint (the outcome a trial is designed to judge; see <a href="/blog/glp-1-trial-endpoint-explained/">GLP-1 trial endpoints explained</a>). Weight takes months to plateau, so weight trials run past a year. Heart attacks and strokes are rarer, so SELECT needed 17,604 people and years of follow-up to count enough of them.</p>
<h2 id="what-can-a-68-or-72-week-trial-tell-you">What can a 68- or 72-week trial tell you?</h2>
<p>Quite a lot, honestly:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>How much weight people lose on average, and how many reach 5%, 10% or 15%.</strong> In STEP 1, 86.4% of the semaglutide group lost at least 5% by week 68.</li>
<li><strong>The common side effects and when they happen.</strong> Nausea and diarrhea were the most common with semaglutide and were typically mild to moderate and temporary. SURMOUNT reports describe gastrointestinal effects happening mostly during dose escalation.</li>
<li><strong>Whether the curve flattens.</strong> Both trials show weight loss slowing to a plateau, which is useful context if you are in one now (see <a href="/blog/glp-1-weight-loss-plateau/">GLP-1 weight loss plateau</a>).</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="what-can-a-68-week-trial-not-tell-you">What can a 68-week trial <em>not</em> tell you?</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Whether the effect lasts for many years.</strong> It tells you about week 68, not year six.</li>
<li><strong>What happens after stopping.</strong> Unless the design includes an off-treatment period, the trial ends while everyone is still on the drug.</li>
<li><strong>Rare harms.</strong> If a side effect happens to 1 in 10,000 people per year, a trial of 2,000 people for 18 months may never see it. Those signals usually come from bigger, longer studies and post-marketing surveillance.</li>
<li><strong>Outcomes that take years to develop</strong>, like heart attacks, kidney failure or cancer.</li>
</ul>
<p>A good example of the last point: GLP-1 labels carry a boxed warning that these medicines cause thyroid C-cell tumors in rodents, and that it is unknown whether they do so in humans. That &quot;unknown&quot; is partly a time-and-numbers problem: a rare cancer needs very large, very long observation to rule in or out. Our <a href="/blog/glp-1-thyroid-cancer-warning-explained/">thyroid warning explainer</a> covers what that warning does and does not mean.</p>
<h2 id="what-did-the-longer-trials-add">What did the longer trials add?</h2>
<p><strong>Two years (STEP 5).</strong> Weight loss with semaglutide was -15.2% at week 104, close to the 68-week result in STEP 1. That supports durability while on treatment, at least to two years. Note the size, though: 304 participants, which means the result is less precise than a 2,000-person trial.</p>
<p><strong>Three years (SURMOUNT-1, prediabetes group).</strong> Of SURMOUNT-1&#39;s 2,539 participants, 1,032 also had prediabetes, and they stayed on their assigned dose for 176 weeks. Weight change was -12.3%, -18.7% and -19.7% on the three tirzepatide doses versus -1.3% on placebo. Type 2 diabetes was diagnosed in 1.3% of the tirzepatide groups versus 13.3% on placebo. The analysis reported no new safety signals.</p>
<p>Then the clever bit: a 17-week off-treatment period. After stopping, 2.4% of the tirzepatide groups had type 2 diabetes versus 13.7% on placebo. So part of the benefit persisted briefly after stopping, but the number of diagnoses in the tirzepatide groups also edged up once treatment ended. A short trial could never have shown that; a trial that ended at week 176 without the off period could not either. (More in <a href="/blog/glp-1-prediabetes-diabetes-risk/">GLP-1 medicines and prediabetes</a>.)</p>
<p><strong>Three-plus years of events (SELECT).</strong> Heart outcome trials are long by nature. SELECT followed people for a mean of 39.8 months, and its result (fewer major cardiovascular events) is the kind of thing you simply cannot learn in 68 weeks.</p>

1c:Tce9,<h2 id="does-a-longer-trial-always-mean-better-evidence">Does a longer trial always mean better evidence?</h2>
<p>Not automatically. Long trials have their own problems:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>People stop treatment.</strong> In SELECT, 31% of the Wegovy group and 27% of the placebo group permanently stopped the study drug, according to the Wegovy label. The longer a trial runs, the more the &quot;drug&quot; group becomes a mix of people on and off the drug.</li>
<li><strong>Life happens.</strong> Over years, participants change other medicines, diets and doctors. That is realistic but noisier.</li>
<li><strong>Fewer people may be followed.</strong> The three-year SURMOUNT-1 result applies to the prediabetes subgroup, not the whole trial.</li>
</ul>
<p>So duration is one dial, not a quality score. A big 72-week trial and a small two-year trial each bring something.</p>
<h2 id="how-to-read-long-term-claims-in-the-news">How to read &quot;long-term&quot; claims in the news</h2>
<p>When an article says &quot;long-term results,&quot; ask:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>How long, exactly?</strong> Weeks or years, and was that a fixed length or an average follow-up?</li>
<li><strong>On or off the drug?</strong> Results &quot;during treatment&quot; and &quot;after stopping&quot; are different questions. (Our guide to <a href="/blog/glp-1-trial-extension-study-explained/">extension studies</a> covers the &quot;after&quot; part.)</li>
<li><strong>Everyone, or a subgroup?</strong> The three-year tirzepatide data came from 1,032 people with prediabetes.</li>
<li><strong>What kind of outcome?</strong> Weight, a lab value, or real clinical events?</li>
</ol>
<p>And keep the week number glued to every figure you repeat. &quot;Lost 20%&quot; is a different statement from &quot;lost 20% at week 72.&quot;</p>
<h2 id="where-your-own-timeline-fits-in">Where your own timeline fits in</h2>
<p>Trials end. Your treatment may not. Keeping a long, boring, honest record (weights, doses, side effects, labs) is how you and your clinician build your personal &quot;long-term data.&quot; If you log those in GLPily, the trends stay in one place across months and years instead of scattered across appointment notes.</p>
<h2 id="common-questions">Common questions</h2>
<h3 id="how-long-were-the-wegovy-and-zepbound-weight-loss-trials">How long were the Wegovy and Zepbound weight-loss trials?</h3>
<p>The main weight trials were 68 weeks for semaglutide 2.4 mg (STEP 1) and 72 weeks for tirzepatide (SURMOUNT-1). Longer trials and follow-ups now exist, up to 176 weeks for tirzepatide in people with prediabetes.</p>
<h3 id="is-there-long-term-safety-data-on-glp-1-medicines">Is there long-term safety data on GLP-1 medicines?</h3>
<p>There are multi-year trials and years of post-marketing experience, especially for diabetes use. Rare or very slow-developing effects remain under study, which is why labels and FDA communications keep being updated.</p>
<h3 id="why-don-39-t-companies-just-run-10-year-trials">Why don&#39;t companies just run 10-year trials?</h3>
<p>Keeping thousands of people on placebo for a decade raises ethical and practical problems, and many participants would stop or switch treatment. Long-term questions are usually answered by combining long outcome trials with real-world data.</p>
