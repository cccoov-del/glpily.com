17:T1446,<p>When friends compare GLP-1 weight loss, you don&#39;t have to play. You can cheer for them, keep your own numbers private and change the topic with one easy line. And if you&#39;re quietly wondering why your best friend lost more on the &quot;same shot,&quot; clinical trials show that results vary a lot, even among people on the same medicine at the same dose.</p>
<p>This post is about the in-person version: brunch, the office kitchen, the family group hug that turns into a weigh-in. (If your comparison happens mostly on your phone, see our guide to <a href="/blog/enjoy-glp-1-stories-without-comparison/">enjoying GLP-1 stories online without comparing</a>.)</p>
<h2 id="why-does-comparing-glp-1-weight-loss-with-friends-feel-so-bad">Why does comparing GLP-1 weight loss with friends feel so bad?</h2>
<p>Picture it. You&#39;re at brunch. Your coworker started the same month as you. She says, &quot;I&#39;m down 30! What about you?&quot; You&#39;re down 12. Suddenly your eggs taste like homework.</p>
<p>Comparing feels natural. You started around the same time, maybe you even talked each other into calling the doctor. But a pound count is a very small window into a very big picture. It leaves out:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Which medicine and which dose.</strong> &quot;The shot&quot; can mean very different drugs, and doses go up in steps over months.</li>
<li><strong>How long each person has been on it.</strong> Starting &quot;around the same time&quot; can mean six weeks apart, which matters early on.</li>
<li><strong>Starting point.</strong> Ten pounds is a different share of body weight for different people.</li>
<li><strong>Health conditions and other medicines.</strong> Diabetes, thyroid conditions, sleep, stress and many prescriptions can all play a part.</li>
<li><strong>Life.</strong> Night shifts, a new baby, a breakup, a broken ankle.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="same-medicine-same-dose-do-people-really-get-different-results">Same medicine, same dose: do people really get different results?</h2>
<p>Yes, and the trial numbers make it very clear.</p>
<p>In STEP 1, a large trial of weekly semaglutide 2.4 mg (a Wegovy maintenance dose) in 1,961 adults without diabetes, people on the medicine lost an <strong>average of 14.9%</strong> of their body weight over 68 weeks. But look at how that average breaks down:</p>

<thead>
<tr>
<th>Weight lost at week 68</th>
<th>Share of people on semaglutide</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody><tr>
<td>5% or more</td>
<td>86.4%</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>10% or more</td>
<td>69.1%</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>15% or more</td>
<td>50.5%</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>
<p>So about half of the people reached 15%, and about half didn&#39;t. Everyone in that group got the same medicine, the same dose and the same lifestyle program.</p>
<p>In SURMOUNT-1, a trial of tirzepatide in 2,539 adults without diabetes, the average loss at 72 weeks was 15.0%, 19.5% and 20.9% on the 5 mg, 10 mg and 15 mg doses. On the 15 mg dose, 57% lost 20% or more of their body weight, which means 43% didn&#39;t.</p>
<p>That&#39;s the point. Your friend can be at the top of that spread and you can be in the middle, and <strong>you can both be responding</strong>. Our explainer on <a href="/blog/average-weight-loss-vs-responder-rates/">average weight loss vs responder rates</a> goes deeper into how to read these two kinds of numbers. If you&#39;re worried your result is low, read <a href="/blog/why-some-people-respond-less-to-glp-1/">why some people respond less to GLP-1 medicines</a> and take your questions to your prescriber, not to brunch.</p>
<h2 id="what-can-i-say-when-a-friend-asks-how-much-i-39-ve-lost">What can I say when a friend asks how much I&#39;ve lost?</h2>
<p>You don&#39;t owe anyone a number. Choose a line for how close you are to the person:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Light:</strong> &quot;Honestly, I&#39;m trying not to count out loud. How are <em>you</em> feeling?&quot;</li>
<li><strong>Warm:</strong> &quot;I&#39;m so happy for you! I&#39;m going at my own pace, and I&#39;m good with that.&quot;</li>
<li><strong>Firm:</strong> &quot;I keep my numbers between me and my doctor. Tell me about the trip!&quot;</li>
<li><strong>Funny:</strong> &quot;My scale and I are in a private relationship.&quot;</li>
</ul>
<p>If someone asks again, give the same line again with a smile. You&#39;re not dodging. You&#39;ve answered.</p>
<h2 id="what-if-the-comparing-comes-from-family">What if the comparing comes from family?</h2>
<p>Family comparing tends to be louder and more public. Think of the mom who announces at dinner, &quot;Your cousin lost 40 pounds on that stuff, why not you?&quot;</p>
<p>Try:</p>
<ul>
<li>&quot;We&#39;re different people on different plans. I&#39;m happy for her.&quot;</li>
<li>&quot;I&#39;d love for us to stop ranking bodies at dinner.&quot;</li>
<li>Later, in private: &quot;When you compare me to Jess, I feel like I&#39;m failing at something that&#39;s going fine. Can you just ask how I&#39;m doing instead?&quot;</li>
</ul>
<p>That last one works because it says how you feel and asks for something specific. Most family members compare out of excitement, not cruelty. They just need a new script.</p>

1f:Tfd9,<h2 id="how-do-i-stop-comparing-myself-even-quietly">How do I stop comparing myself, even quietly?</h2>
<p>Sometimes nobody says anything, and you&#39;re the one keeping score. A few ideas:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Change what you measure.</strong> If pounds make you spiral, look at a slower, broader picture: waist measurements, energy, how stairs feel, blood pressure or A1C if your doctor tracks them. Our guide on <a href="/blog/how-to-track-glp-1-progress/">tracking GLP-1 progress beyond the scale</a> has a list.</li>
<li><strong>Compare you to you.</strong> Your three-month trend says far more about you than anyone else&#39;s week does. And remember that weight changes day to day, so one weigh-in tells you little.</li>
<li><strong>Notice the &quot;but.&quot;</strong> &quot;I&#39;m happy for her, <em>but</em>...&quot; is where comparison sneaks in. Stop the sentence before the &quot;but.&quot;</li>
<li><strong>Mute the topic, not the friend.</strong> You can love someone and still ask, &quot;Can we talk about anything but weight today?&quot;</li>
</ol>
<p>If your weight loss really does seem slow, read <a href="/blog/slow-weight-loss-on-glp-1/">slow weight loss on GLP-1</a> and bring your notes to your next appointment. GLPily&#39;s weight chart and daily check-in can make that conversation easier, because you&#39;ll have your own trend in front of you instead of someone else&#39;s number in your head.</p>
<h2 id="how-can-i-support-a-friend-without-turning-it-into-a-contest">How can I support a friend without turning it into a contest?</h2>
<p>If you&#39;re the one doing well, you can make it easier for others too.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Ask how they feel, not how much they&#39;ve lost.</strong> &quot;How&#39;s your energy?&quot; opens a real conversation.</li>
<li><strong>Celebrate wins that aren&#39;t numbers.</strong> &quot;You seem so much happier on hikes&quot; beats &quot;You&#39;re catching up to me!&quot;</li>
<li><strong>Don&#39;t share their news.</strong> &quot;Guess who&#39;s on Mounjaro too&quot; is not your story to tell.</li>
<li><strong>Avoid tips that are really doses.</strong> &quot;Just go up to the next dose&quot; is a call for their prescriber, not for friends.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="common-questions">Common questions</h2>
<h3 id="why-did-my-friend-lose-more-weight-on-the-same-glp-1">Why did my friend lose more weight on the same GLP-1?</h3>
<p>Trials show results spread widely, even on the same medicine and dose. In STEP 1, about half of the people on semaglutide 2.4 mg lost 15% or more, and about half didn&#39;t. Dose, time on treatment, starting weight, health conditions and other medicines all play a role.</p>
<h3 id="is-it-ok-to-not-tell-friends-how-much-weight-i-39-ve-lost">Is it OK to not tell friends how much weight I&#39;ve lost?</h3>
<p>Absolutely. Your numbers are private health information. &quot;I keep my numbers between me and my doctor&quot; is a complete answer.</p>
<h3 id="does-losing-less-than-a-friend-mean-my-glp-1-isn-39-t-working">Does losing less than a friend mean my GLP-1 isn&#39;t working?</h3>
<p>Not necessarily. A friend&#39;s result isn&#39;t a benchmark for yours. If you&#39;re worried, ask your prescriber what counts as a meaningful response for you and bring your own trend over several months.</p>
