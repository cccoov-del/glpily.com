19:Tb46,<p>Nausea is listed among the possible side effects of semaglutide and tirzepatide injections. Experiences vary by medication and person. The current official medication guide for your specific prescription is the best source for known side effects and warnings.</p>
<p>This article offers general organization and food ideas. It cannot tell you what is causing your nausea or replace advice from your prescriber.</p>
<h2 id="keep-food-choices-simple-and-flexible">Keep food choices simple and flexible</h2>
<p>When nausea makes food less appealing, a large meal can feel like too much. Some people prefer smaller portions or familiar foods for a while. Examples might include crackers, toast, rice, noodles, potatoes or a small simple meal, depending on what you tolerate and your nutrition needs. General MedlinePlus guidance on nausea also suggests several small meals instead of a few large ones and avoiding greasy or very spicy foods. These are examples, not a treatment plan, and one choice will not suit everyone.</p>
<p>Eat at a comfortable pace and avoid forcing yourself through a meal that makes you feel worse. If you are struggling to eat enough, symptoms persist, or you are unsure what to do, contact your healthcare professional for personalized advice. Ask before using a supplement, over-the-counter medicine or major dietary change to manage symptoms.</p>
<h2 id="track-the-pattern-so-you-can-describe-it">Track the pattern so you can describe it</h2>
<p>A brief note can capture when nausea occurs, how long it lasts, whether vomiting occurs, what you were able to eat or drink, and questions you want to ask. Do not use a log to decide on your own to change, skip or stop medication.</p>
<h2 id="know-when-symptoms-need-medical-attention">Know when symptoms need medical attention</h2>
<p>Read the official medication guide for warning signs and instructions. Stomach pain that won&#39;t go away (it may spread to your back), vomiting that is repeated or keeps you from holding down fluids, signs of dehydration such as urinating much less than usual or feeling dizzy, or an allergic reaction such as swelling of the face, lips or throat or trouble breathing require prompt medical attention; emergency symptoms should be treated as an emergency. Do not wait for a tracker or article to diagnose the problem.</p>
<h2 id="questions-to-bring-to-an-appointment">Questions to bring to an appointment</h2>
<ul>
<li>How long have symptoms been happening?</li>
<li>How often do they occur, and are they getting worse?</li>
<li>Can I keep fluids down and maintain adequate food intake?</li>
<li>Which symptoms mean I should call you or seek urgent care?</li>
<li>Are there food or medication considerations specific to my situation?</li>
</ul>
<p>The goal of tracking is to communicate clearly with your healthcare professional, not to handle a medical concern alone.</p>
