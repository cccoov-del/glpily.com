15:T103c,<p>To report a suspected GLP-1 side effect to FDA, use MedWatch: go to FDA&#39;s online reporting page, choose the <strong>Consumer/Patient</strong> form (Form FDA 3500B), describe the product and what happened, and submit. You can also call 1-888-INFO-FDA (1-888-463-6332) and press 2 for reporting instructions, or fax a paper form. If you gather your details first, the whole thing goes quickly.</p>
<p>Before any of that, one rule: <strong>your health comes before the paperwork.</strong> A MedWatch report is not a way to get medical help.</p>
<h2 id="when-should-you-get-care-instead-of-filing-a-report">When should you get care instead of filing a report?</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Emergency</strong> (trouble breathing, severe allergic reaction, severe or persistent belly pain, fainting): call <strong>911</strong>. MedWatch&#39;s own reporting page says the same.</li>
<li><strong>Possible overdose or wrong amount</strong>: call <strong>Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>Mental health crisis</strong>: call or text <strong>988</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>Anything else worrying</strong>: contact your prescriber or pharmacist. Don&#39;t stop or change your dose on your own.</li>
</ul>
<p>You can file a report later, once you&#39;re safe. FDA won&#39;t review it fast enough to help you that day, and MedWatch doesn&#39;t give medical advice.</p>
<h2 id="what-can-you-report-to-medwatch">What can you report to MedWatch?</h2>
<p>MedWatch accepts reports about human medical products, including prescription drugs, for:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Serious reactions</strong> or other adverse events you suspect are linked to the medicine</li>
<li><strong>Product quality problems</strong> (a pen that won&#39;t click, a cloudy solution, damaged packaging)</li>
<li><strong>Therapeutic failure</strong> (the product didn&#39;t seem to work as expected)</li>
<li><strong>Product use errors</strong> (wrong dose, mix-ups between products)</li>
</ul>
<p>This applies to brand-name GLP-1s and to compounded versions. FDA specifically encourages patients and health professionals to report problems with compounded or unapproved GLP-1 drugs to MedWatch.</p>
<p>You don&#39;t need to be sure the medicine caused the problem. A report means &quot;I suspect a link,&quot; which is exactly what FDA wants to hear about. (Why a report isn&#39;t proof is covered in our sibling post on <a href="/blog/adverse-event-report-causation-glp-1/">adverse-event reports and causation</a>.)</p>

1c:T16ce,<h2 id="how-do-you-file-a-medwatch-report-online-step-by-step">How do you file a MedWatch report online? (step by step)</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Go to FDA&#39;s MedWatch online reporting page</strong> (search &quot;MedWatch online reporting&quot; or go to accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/MedWatch).</li>
<li><strong>Under &quot;Begin Online Report,&quot; choose Consumer/Patient.</strong> That opens the plain-language Form 3500B. (Health professionals use Form 3500.)</li>
<li><strong>Describe the problem.</strong> What happened, when, how serious, and what was done about it. Plain words are fine; you don&#39;t need medical terms.</li>
<li><strong>Describe the product.</strong> Name, strength, how you used it, dates, lot number. If it was compounded, say so and name the pharmacy.</li>
<li><strong>Add details about the person affected.</strong> Age, sex, weight, relevant conditions, other medicines. You can report for a family member you care for.</li>
<li><strong>Add your contact information.</strong> It&#39;s optional, but FDA encourages it so it can follow up. You can ask FDA not to release your contact details to the manufacturer.</li>
<li><strong>Need a break? Save it.</strong> You&#39;ll get a Report ID. With that ID and the report date, you can come back and finish within <strong>3 days</strong> of starting.</li>
<li><strong>Review and submit.</strong></li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Important:</strong> once submitted, a report can&#39;t be retrieved or edited. If you forgot something, start a new report and repeat the product, event summary, reporter and patient details along with the new information.</p>
<h3 id="prefer-phone-or-paper">Prefer phone or paper?</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Phone:</strong> 1-888-INFO-FDA (1-888-463-6332), then press 2 for MedWatch reporting instructions.</li>
<li><strong>Paper:</strong> download Form FDA 3500B from FDA&#39;s MedWatch forms page and follow the instructions on it to mail or fax it. FDA lists its MedWatch fax number as 1-800-FDA-0178.</li>
<li><strong>Your clinician:</strong> a health professional can file a report or add clinical details from your records, but you don&#39;t need them to. You can report yourself.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="what-happens-after-you-submit-a-medwatch-report">What happens after you submit a MedWatch report?</h2>
<p>Less drama than you might hope, more useful than you might think:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>It goes into FDA&#39;s database.</strong> Since March 2026 that&#39;s the Adverse Event Monitoring System (AEMS), which replaced FAERS.</li>
<li><strong>A safety evaluator reviews it,</strong> often a pharmacist, doctor or nurse, and compares it with similar reports.</li>
<li><strong>FDA monitors trends</strong> and investigates when appropriate.</li>
<li><strong>If a real problem emerges,</strong> possible actions include safety alerts, label updates, a Medication Guide, manufacturer inspections, design or packaging changes, recalls or further safety studies.</li>
</ol>
<p>What usually <em>doesn&#39;t</em> happen: a personal ruling on whether the drug caused your problem, or medical advice. Your report becomes one data point among many. That sounds small, but FDA&#39;s own reporting page points out that a single report &quot;may be the critical action that prompts a modification in use or design of the product.&quot;</p>
<h2 id="spot-the-trick">Spot the trick</h2>
<h3 id="1-i-reported-it-to-fda-so-i-don-39-t-need-to-tell-my-doctor">1. &quot;I reported it to FDA, so I don&#39;t need to tell my doctor.&quot;</h3>
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>Backwards. FDA uses reports for safety monitoring, not to treat you. Your prescriber or pharmacist needs to know, and should hear first.</details>

<h3 id="2-i-39-m-not-100-sure-the-glp-1-caused-it-so-i-shouldn-39-t-report">2. &quot;I&#39;m not 100% sure the GLP-1 caused it, so I shouldn&#39;t report.&quot;</h3>
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>Suspicion is enough. MedWatch is for suspected problems. Working out cause is FDA's job, using many reports and other data.</details>

<h3 id="3-thousands-of-people-reported-this-so-fda-must-have-confirmed-it">3. &quot;Thousands of people reported this, so FDA must have confirmed it.&quot;</h3>
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>Report counts are signals, not confirmations. FDA says the existence of a report does not establish causation. Look for what FDA actually concluded, for example in a safety communication. Our guide to <a href="/blog/read-fda-safety-alert-glp-1/">reading an FDA safety alert</a> helps.</details>

<p><strong>Scoring:</strong> 3 out of 3 and you&#39;re ready to file a clean, useful report. FDA&#39;s safety evaluators thank you in advance.</p>
<h2 id="common-questions">Common questions</h2>
<h3 id="can-patients-report-side-effects-to-fda-or-only-doctors">Can patients report side effects to FDA, or only doctors?</h3>
<p>Patients and caregivers can report directly. MedWatch has a consumer form (Form FDA 3500B). A health professional can help, but doesn&#39;t have to.</p>
<h3 id="will-fda-respond-to-my-medwatch-report">Will FDA respond to my MedWatch report?</h3>
<p>Don&#39;t expect a personal reply or medical advice. FDA uses reports to monitor safety across many people, and including your contact details lets it reach you if it needs more information.</p>
<h3 id="can-i-report-a-side-effect-from-compounded-semaglutide-or-tirzepatide">Can I report a side effect from compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide?</h3>
<p>Yes. FDA encourages reports about compounded and unapproved GLP-1 products. Include the pharmacy name and any label details you have.</p>
<h3 id="is-my-medwatch-report-private">Is my MedWatch report private?</h3>
<p>Contact details are optional, and you can ask FDA not to release them to the manufacturer. FDA describes MedWatch reporting as confidential and secure.</p>
