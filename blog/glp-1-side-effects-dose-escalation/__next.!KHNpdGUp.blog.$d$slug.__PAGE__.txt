1a:Tb5d,<p>You finally felt settled on your current dose, then the next step-up arrived and the nausea, fullness or reflux came back. That&#39;s a common experience, and it&#39;s actually one of the reasons GLP-1 medicines are started low and increased gradually. Still, &quot;common&quot; doesn&#39;t mean every symptom after a dose increase is fine to push through.</p>
<h2 id="why-step-ups-and-side-effects-go-together">Why step-ups and side effects go together</h2>
<p>GLP-1 medicines slow stomach emptying and act on appetite signals, and the body tends to react most when the dose changes. The data line up with that:</p>
<ul>
<li>In pooled STEP 1–3 data for semaglutide 2.4 mg (Wegovy), digestive side effects were mostly mild to moderate and happened most often during or shortly after dose escalation.</li>
<li>The US Zepbound (tirzepatide) label says most nausea, vomiting and diarrhea events occurred during dose escalation and decreased over time. In a SURMOUNT trial analysis, when people needed anti-nausea or anti-diarrhea medicine, they most often first used it during the escalation phase.</li>
<li>The US Wegovy label tells prescribers to follow its escalation schedule specifically to reduce the risk of gastrointestinal side effects.</li>
</ul>
<p>Every product has its own schedule, step sizes and timing, and some labels describe options if a step isn&#39;t tolerated. There&#39;s no universal dosing plan for all GLP-1 medicines, which is exactly why your prescriber&#39;s instructions matter.</p>
<h2 id="a-symptom-after-a-step-up-isn-39-t-always-the-medicine">A symptom after a step-up isn&#39;t always the medicine</h2>
<p>Timing is useful information, not a diagnosis. A stomach bug, dehydration, a new prescription or an underlying digestive condition can show up in the same week as a dose change. Noting what changed and when helps your clinician sort it out.</p>
<h2 id="two-things-that-can-matter-more-during-escalation">Two things that can matter more during escalation</h2>
<p><strong>Fluids.</strong> Vomiting and diarrhea can lead to dehydration, and the Wegovy label notes reports of acute kidney injury, mostly in people with digestive side effects that caused fluid loss. It asks clinicians to monitor kidney function in people with these symptoms, especially when starting and increasing the dose. If you can&#39;t keep fluids down, that&#39;s a reason to get help, not to wait. Our guide to <a href="/blog/glp-1-dehydration-kidney-health/">GLP-1 medicines and dehydration</a> goes deeper.</p>
<p><strong>Birth control pills with tirzepatide.</strong> The US Mounjaro and Zepbound labels advise people using oral hormonal contraceptives to switch to a non-oral method, or add a barrier method, for 4 weeks after starting and for 4 weeks after each dose increase. This is specific to tirzepatide labels, so ask your prescriber or pharmacist what applies to your medicine.</p>

2d:T667,<h2 id="what-to-do-if-symptoms-get-worse">What to do if symptoms get worse</h2>
<p>Jot down which symptom changed, when it started, how severe it feels, and whether you can eat, drink and get through your day. Contact your prescriber if symptoms persist, keep coming back, or interfere with daily life.</p>
<p>Seek urgent medical care if you can&#39;t keep fluids down, have severe or persistent stomach pain, feel faint or confused, or notice signs of serious dehydration such as very little urine.</p>
<h2 id="why-not-just-slow-down-or-stop-on-your-own">Why not just slow down or stop on your own?</h2>
<p>Dose decisions belong with your prescribing clinician, who can weigh the label, your treatment goal, other medicines and your health history. Don&#39;t estimate partial doses by counting pen clicks, split doses, take extra, or stretch the interval without instructions. Devices and dosing differ, and guesswork can make things less safe, not more.</p>
<h2 id="questions-that-may-help">Questions that may help</h2>
<ul>
