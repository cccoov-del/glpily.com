1c:T1c11,<p>Good GLP-1 group chat etiquette comes down to one idea: share your experiences, not instructions. Talk about what happened to you. Leave doses, product swaps and medical calls to prescribers and pharmacists. And agree on a few simple rules so the group stays kind, private and safe.</p>
<p>Whether it&#39;s three coworkers who started the same month, a family thread or a 200-person community chat, these ten rules help. There&#39;s also a pinned message at the end that you can copy.</p>
<h2 id="why-do-glp-1-group-chats-need-rules">Why do GLP-1 group chats need rules?</h2>
<p>Picture the chat at 9:47 p.m. Someone posts: &quot;Feeling awful after my increase, should I skip next week?&quot; Within minutes there are eleven replies. Two say skip. One says halve it. One posts a screenshot of a TikTok. One offers to sell their leftover vials. Nobody asks when the symptoms started.</p>
<p>Everyone means well. That&#39;s what makes it tricky. A group chat feels like friends, and friends want to help fast. But people in the chat may be on different medicines, doses, forms (pens, vials, pills) and health conditions. Advice that worked for one person can be risky for another.</p>
<h2 id="the-10-rules-of-glp-1-group-chat-etiquette">The 10 rules of GLP-1 group chat etiquette</h2>
<h3 id="1-use-this-happened-to-me-not-you-should">1. Use &quot;this happened to me,&quot; not &quot;you should&quot;</h3>
<p>&quot;I felt queasy for two days after my increase&quot; is a story. &quot;You should wait a week before increasing&quot; is a prescription. Stories help people feel less alone. Prescriptions belong to prescribers.</p>
<h3 id="2-no-dose-math-for-other-people-ever">2. No dose math for other people, ever</h3>
<p>This is the big one. The FDA has received reports of people taking far too much compounded semaglutide, in some cases 5 to 20 times the intended dose, after mix-ups between milligrams, milliliters and &quot;units&quot; on a syringe. In one example, a patient meant to draw 5 units drew 50. Some needed medical care or a hospital stay.</p>
<p>If someone asks &quot;how many units is 0.5 mg?&quot;, the only safe group answer is &quot;Please ask your pharmacist or whoever prescribed it.&quot; Our explainer on <a href="/blog/mg-ml-units-injectable-medication-confusion/">mg, mL and units</a> shows why.</p>
<h3 id="3-never-share-swap-or-sell-medicine-or-pens">3. Never share, swap or sell medicine or pens</h3>
<p>The FDA-approved labels for Ozempic and Wegovy say a pen must <strong>never be shared between people, even if the needle is changed</strong>, because of the risk of spreading blood-borne infections. On top of that, a prescription is for one person, and the FDA warns that some medicines sold outside licensed pharmacies are counterfeit or of unknown quality.</p>
<p>So: no &quot;I have an extra pen,&quot; no &quot;anyone want my leftover vial,&quot; no group buys from a website someone found. Moderators should delete these posts. Our guide to <a href="/blog/counterfeit-glp-1-medicines-online-offers/">counterfeit GLP-1 offers online</a> explains the risks.</p>
<h3 id="4-say-which-product-you-39-re-talking-about">4. Say which product you&#39;re talking about</h3>
<p>&quot;I&#39;m on it too!&quot; isn&#39;t very helpful. Brand pens, pills, compounded vials and different medicines can have very different instructions. If you share an experience, add context: &quot;I&#39;m on a brand-name pen, month 4.&quot; You don&#39;t have to share your dose. Just don&#39;t let people assume your situation matches theirs.</p>
<h3 id="5-link-sources-not-screenshots">5. Link sources, not screenshots</h3>
<p>If you share a fact, link where it came from. The best places are the drug&#39;s official prescribing information (on DailyMed or FDA.gov), FDA safety pages or a published study. A screenshot of a viral post isn&#39;t a source. Our <a href="/blog/glp-1-social-media-misinformation/">red flags in viral GLP-1 tips</a> guide helps you spot the risky ones.</p>
<h3 id="6-ask-before-you-screenshot">6. Ask before you screenshot</h3>
<p>What&#39;s said in the group stays in the group. Don&#39;t forward someone&#39;s photos, weight, symptoms or messages without asking first. Health information shared in a group chat usually isn&#39;t protected the way your doctor&#39;s records are, so treat it carefully. (Our post on <a href="/blog/hipaa-health-apps-myths/">HIPAA and health apps</a> explains what that law does and doesn&#39;t cover.)</p>
<h3 id="7-no-weight-scoreboards">7. No weight scoreboards</h3>
<p>Celebrate wins, but skip the leaderboard. Results vary a lot, even on the same medicine and dose. &quot;Who&#39;s lost the most?&quot; helps nobody. &quot;What helped your nausea?&quot; helps everyone. More on that in when friends compare GLP-1 weight loss.</p>
<h3 id="8-let-people-vent-without-fixing-them">8. Let people vent without fixing them</h3>
<p>Sometimes &quot;this week was rough&quot; just needs &quot;that sounds hard, I&#39;m here.&quot; Not every post is asking for advice. If you&#39;re unsure, ask: &quot;Do you want ideas or just company?&quot;</p>
<h3 id="9-know-when-it-39-s-not-a-group-chat-problem">9. Know when it&#39;s not a group-chat problem</h3>
<p>Some things should leave the chat right away:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Severe stomach pain, ongoing vomiting, signs of an allergic reaction or trouble breathing:</strong> get medical help right away. Call the prescriber, and call 911 or go to an emergency room if it&#39;s severe.</li>
<li><strong>Possible wrong dose or overdose:</strong> call Poison Control at <strong>1-800-222-1222</strong> (24/7) or use webPOISONCONTROL online, and contact the prescriber. See <a href="/blog/wrong-dose-glp-1-what-to-do/">think you took the wrong amount?</a></li>
<li><strong>Questions about pregnancy, surgery, new medicines or changing a dose:</strong> prescriber or pharmacist.</li>
</ul>
<p>A great group reply is: &quot;That sounds like one for your doctor today. Want help finding the number?&quot;</p>
<h3 id="10-mute-freely-no-hard-feelings">10. Mute freely, no hard feelings</h3>
<p>A busy chat can take over your day. Muting notifications isn&#39;t rude. It&#39;s self-care. Check in when it suits you, and if a group makes you anxious more than it helps, it&#39;s OK to leave.</p>
<h2 id="a-pinned-message-you-can-copy">A pinned message you can copy</h2>
<p>Admins, paste this at the top of your chat:</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Welcome! Our group rules:</strong></p>
<ol>
<li>Share your experience, not instructions. &quot;This happened to me,&quot; not &quot;you should.&quot;</li>
<li>No dose math or dose advice for others. Ask your pharmacist or prescriber.</li>
<li>No selling, swapping or sharing pens, vials or pills. Posts like this get removed.</li>
<li>Link real sources (labels, FDA, studies), not screenshots of viral posts.</li>
<li>Ask before sharing anyone&#39;s photos, numbers or messages outside the group.</li>
<li>No weight leaderboards. Cheer wins, skip comparisons.</li>
<li>Emergencies: call 911. Possible overdose: Poison Control 1-800-222-1222. Then tell your prescriber.</li>
<li>Be kind. Mute when you need to.</li>
</ol>
</blockquote>

<h2 id="how-can-i-keep-my-own-notes-outside-the-chat">How can I keep my own notes outside the chat?</h2>
<p>Group chats scroll away fast. If something worked for you, like a meal that settled your stomach or a symptom pattern after dose day, write it down somewhere you&#39;ll find it. Then you can bring real details to your next appointment. If you use GLPily, the symptom log and journal sit next to your shot log, and the Pily AI helper answers questions from your own logs. (It still won&#39;t replace your prescriber, just like the group chat won&#39;t.)</p>
<h2 id="common-questions">Common questions</h2>
<h3 id="is-it-ok-to-ask-a-glp-1-group-chat-about-side-effects">Is it OK to ask a GLP-1 group chat about side effects?</h3>
<p>Yes, asking how others handled common side effects can be really comforting. Just treat the answers as stories, not instructions, and call your prescriber about severe, ongoing or worrying symptoms.</p>
<h3 id="can-i-share-my-extra-glp-1-pen-with-a-friend">Can I share my extra GLP-1 pen with a friend?</h3>
<p>No. The FDA-approved labels for Ozempic and Wegovy say a pen must never be shared, even with a new needle, because of infection risk. Prescription medicines are for the person they&#39;re prescribed to.</p>
<h3 id="what-should-a-group-do-if-someone-mentions-an-overdose">What should a group do if someone mentions an overdose?</h3>
<p>Tell them to call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 right away (or 911 for severe symptoms) and to contact their prescriber. Don&#39;t try to work out doses in the chat.</p>
<h3 id="should-a-group-chat-ban-talking-about-doses">Should a group chat ban talking about doses?</h3>
<p>Many groups do well with a simple rule: share what medicine you&#39;re on if you like, but never give or ask for dose advice. Point those questions to a pharmacist or prescriber.</p>
