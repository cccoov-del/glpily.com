1d:T651,<p>Heartburn is a burning feeling in the chest or throat that happens when stomach acid flows back up into the esophagus. Some people notice reflux symptoms while taking GLP-1 medicines, which affect digestion and stomach emptying. A symptom appearing during treatment does not automatically prove the drug caused it, but a new pattern is worth documenting and discussing if it persists.</p>
<h2 id="what-the-evidence-shows">What the evidence shows</h2>
<p>Reflux and indigestion are listed side effects. In the Wegovy weight-management trials, gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) was reported by 5% of people on 2.4 mg versus 3% on placebo, and indigestion (dyspepsia) by 9% versus 3%. In the Zepbound trials, GERD was reported by 4–5% versus 2%, and dyspepsia by 9–10% versus 4%. A 2025 meta-analysis of 55 placebo-controlled trials (about 106,000 participants) found GLP-1 receptor agonists probably increase the risk of GERD reported as an adverse event, but in absolute terms that was about 4 extra cases per 1,000 people. So it is a real effect, just not one most people get.</p>
<h2 id="why-treatment-may-change-the-pattern">Why treatment may change the pattern</h2>
<p>GLP-1 medicines can delay stomach emptying and commonly cause gastrointestinal effects such as nausea, abdominal discomfort, belching or indigestion. A larger meal, lying down soon after eating, fatty foods, alcohol, certain medicines, or an existing reflux condition may contribute too. People vary: one person&#39;s trigger may not affect another, and avoiding a long list of foods is rarely necessary without a clear pattern.</p>
1e:T741,<h2 id="practical-comfort-steps">Practical comfort steps</h2>
<p>Consider whether smaller meals feel more comfortable than large ones (some <a href="/blog/what-to-eat-on-glp-1/">food ideas here</a>), eat slowly, and avoid lying flat right after eating if that worsens symptoms. Notice personal triggers instead of following a strict internet list. If symptoms happen at night, record the timing and discuss it with a clinician or pharmacist. Do not take over-the-counter medicines indefinitely without checking whether they suit your health history and other prescriptions; MedlinePlus notes that heartburn more than twice a week may point to GERD, which is worth treating properly.</p>
<h2 id="what-deserves-medical-review">What deserves medical review?</h2>
<p>Tell your prescriber about heartburn that is new, persistent, disrupting sleep, making eating difficult, or requiring frequent medicine. Seek urgent care for chest pressure or pain that could be cardiac, trouble or pain with swallowing, vomiting blood, black stools, fainting, or severe persistent abdominal pain. Do not assume chest discomfort is &quot;just reflux,&quot; particularly if it is new, crushing or intense: heartburn and a heart attack can be confused, so get help right away.</p>
<h2 id="what-information-helps-an-appointment">What information helps an appointment?</h2>
<p>Write down when symptoms started, how often they occur, whether they relate to meals or injection timing, what other symptoms come with them, and what remedies you have tried. Note any history of reflux or digestive disease. Your clinician can review possible causes, interactions and treatment options. Do not change a GLP-1 dose or schedule on your own because of heartburn.</p>
<p>The aim is a workable treatment plan, not enduring discomfort or making drastic food rules based on one bad evening.</p>
