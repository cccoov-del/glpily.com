18:T1107,<p>GLP-1 medicines slow down how quickly the stomach empties—it&#39;s one of the ways they help you feel full sooner and for longer. The US labels for Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro and Zepbound all describe this &quot;delayed gastric emptying&quot; effect.</p>
<p>That doesn&#39;t mean everyone taking one has gastroparesis. Gastroparesis is a diagnosed condition in which the stomach empties too slowly without anything physically blocking it. Its symptoms overlap with ordinary medication side effects and with other digestive conditions, which is why it&#39;s so easy to confuse online.</p>
<h2 id="what-symptoms-are-worth-discussing">What symptoms are worth discussing?</h2>
<p>Persistent nausea, repeated vomiting, feeling full after a few bites, fullness that lingers long after meals, belly discomfort or trouble getting enough food and fluids are all worth raising with a healthcare professional. Symptoms alone can&#39;t diagnose gastroparesis; testing may be needed to rule out other causes. People with diabetes can also have delayed stomach emptying for reasons unrelated to GLP-1 treatment—NIDDK lists diabetes as a risk factor for gastroparesis.</p>
<p>If nausea is your main issue, our guide to <a href="/blog/nausea-on-glp-1/">nausea on GLP-1</a> has practical food tips.</p>
<h2 id="what-the-labels-and-research-actually-say">What the labels and research actually say</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Labels:</strong> The US labels say these medicines are <em>not recommended</em> for people with severe gastroparesis, and that gastrointestinal side effects can sometimes be severe. Since market launch, ileus (a stalled bowel) and intestinal obstruction have also been reported—post-marketing reports that can&#39;t establish how often they happen or prove cause.</li>
<li><strong>Randomized trials:</strong> A 2025 <em>Gastroenterology</em> meta-analysis of 55 placebo-controlled trials (about 106,000 people) found GLP-1 medicines probably had little or no effect on gastroparesis and most other serious GI events, while gallstones and reflux were more common.</li>
<li><strong>Observational data:</strong> A 2023 research letter in <em>JAMA</em>, using US insurance claims, reported higher rates of gastroparesis diagnoses among people using semaglutide or liraglutide for weight loss than among people using another weight-loss medicine. Studies like this can flag a possible signal but can&#39;t prove the medicine caused it, and rare long-term events are hard for trials to catch.</li>
</ul>
<p>So the fair summary is: slower stomach emptying is expected; a true gastroparesis diagnosis is a separate question that needs a clinician.</p>
<h2 id="surgery-sedation-and-anesthesia">Surgery, sedation and anesthesia</h2>
<p>Because food can stay in the stomach longer, the US labels now note rare reports of pulmonary aspiration (stomach contents entering the lungs) in people on these medicines who had general anesthesia or deep sedation—sometimes even after fasting. The labels ask patients to tell their healthcare providers before any planned surgery or procedure. Your care team will decide how to prepare; don&#39;t change your medicine schedule on your own.</p>
<h2 id="who-needs-an-individualized-conversation">Who needs an individualized conversation?</h2>
<p>Tell your prescriber if you have a history of gastroparesis, previous stomach or bowel surgery, ongoing vomiting or any condition that affects how your gut moves. Share medicines that slow digestion too. A clinician can decide whether symptoms need testing and what a safe next step looks like—please don&#39;t adjust a dose or self-diagnose from a checklist.</p>
<h2 id="when-to-seek-prompt-care">When to seek prompt care</h2>
<p>Get urgent medical help if you are vomiting repeatedly and can&#39;t keep fluids down, have severe or worsening belly pain, your abdomen becomes very swollen, you can&#39;t pass stool or gas, or you notice signs of dehydration such as very little urine or dizziness. These can point to several conditions besides gastroparesis, including a bowel blockage. See <a href="/blog/severe-stomach-pain-glp-1/">severe stomach pain on GLP-1</a> for more warning signs.</p>
<p>Slowed stomach emptying is part of how the medicine works; persistent or severe symptoms deserve a proper assessment. You don&#39;t have to figure out the diagnosis alone.</p>
