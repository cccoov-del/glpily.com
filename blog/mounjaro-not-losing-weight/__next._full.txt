17:T1563,<p>Seeing people post dramatic weight-loss numbers while your own scale barely moves can feel awful, especially if you started treatment hoping something would finally feel different.</p>
<p>If Mounjaro or another GLP-1 medicine doesn&#39;t seem to be doing much, it doesn&#39;t automatically mean you&#39;ve failed. It also doesn&#39;t mean you should change your dose on your own. Individual responses vary, and a clinician can help review what&#39;s happening.</p>
<p>In the US, Mounjaro is approved for type 2 diabetes, and the same medicine (tirzepatide) is approved for weight management as Zepbound. In some other countries, Mounjaro itself is licensed for weight management. Check the label for your product and country.</p>
<h2 id="first-timeline-matters">First, timeline matters</h2>
<p>A first few weeks, a longer plateau and little change after an adequate treatment period are different situations. Clinical trials assess outcomes over months, not a handful of weigh-ins. Weight can also fluctuate with fluids, digestion, hormones and other factors.</p>
<p>Mounjaro is started at a low dose, and any increases follow the schedule in its prescribing information, which is designed partly to reduce digestive side effects. The starting phase is not a guarantee of immediate weight loss. Ask your prescriber how and when they plan to review your progress.</p>
<h2 id="why-can-responses-differ">Why can responses differ?</h2>
<p>A systematic review published in 2026 examined low or no weight-loss response to incretin medicines. The literature has explored factors such as baseline characteristics, metabolic markers, possible genetic differences, adherence and time on treatment. These are research areas, not a checklist that can diagnose why one person&#39;s weight is changing slowly.</p>
<h3 id="1-you-39-re-early-in-the-treatment-course">1. You&#39;re early in the treatment course</h3>
<p>Some people notice appetite changes quickly; others don&#39;t. Long clinical trials cannot be turned into a promise about what should happen in the first month. If you&#39;re still in the starter phase, ask your prescriber how they want to evaluate the overall course.</p>
<h3 id="2-appetite-or-food-noise-hasn-39-t-changed-much">2. Appetite or food noise hasn&#39;t changed much</h3>
<p>In a small 2026 interview study in <em>JAMA Network Open</em> (30 adults), most participants described quieter food noise or appetite; interview studies show themes, not how common an experience is, and they can&#39;t tell you what to expect individually. An absence of noticeable appetite change isn&#39;t, on its own, a reason to change treatment yourself; raise it with your prescriber.</p>
<h3 id="3-an-early-drop-has-slowed">3. An early drop has slowed</h3>
<p>Weight loss often isn&#39;t a straight line. A short stall is not necessarily a long-term plateau, and the scale reflects more than fat mass alone. Looking at a trend over time can provide more context than comparing two mornings.</p>
<h3 id="4-practical-barriers-may-be-getting-in-the-way">4. Practical barriers may be getting in the way</h3>
<p>Cost, supply, treatment interruptions and side effects can make long-term care harder. Tell the clinician about interruptions. Don&#39;t compensate for a missed dose or alter a schedule based on an online post; follow the specific product information and ask a pharmacist or prescriber if you&#39;re unsure.</p>
<h3 id="5-your-wider-health-situation-may-need-review">5. Your wider health situation may need review</h3>
<p>Other medicines and medical factors can matter. This is not a reason to self-diagnose a “slow metabolism” or stop treatment. A clinician can decide whether your history, other therapies or treatment goal need to be reviewed.</p>
<h2 id="what-not-to-do">What not to do</h2>
<p>Don&#39;t increase a dose early, combine weight-loss medicines, severely restrict food, or buy unapproved products online. People posting their results may not share dose history, diabetes status, health conditions, interruptions or how long they have been treated. Their experience cannot tell you what to do next.</p>
<h2 id="what-to-bring-to-your-next-appointment">What to bring to your next appointment</h2>
<ul>
<li>Treatment start date and any interruptions.</li>
<li>A weight trend rather than one isolated reading.</li>
<li>Appetite, cravings, side effects and quality-of-life changes.</li>
<li>Barriers related to cost, supply or tolerability.</li>
<li>Questions about how response will be assessed and when to review the plan.</li>
</ul>
<p>Get medical help right away for severe stomach pain that won&#39;t go away (sometimes spreading to the back), swelling of the face, lips, tongue or throat or trouble breathing, upper-stomach pain with fever or yellow skin or eyes, or vomiting or diarrhea that won&#39;t stop (risk of dehydration and kidney problems). If you also use insulin or a sulfonylurea, learn the signs of low blood sugar.</p>
<h2 id="your-progress-is-more-than-a-comparison-post">Your progress is more than a comparison post</h2>
<p>Social media makes extreme results more visible than ordinary or slow journeys. Clinical trials describe averages, while people have a range of outcomes. You deserve a clear conversation about whether treatment is helping and what options exist.</p>
