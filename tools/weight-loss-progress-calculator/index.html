How it’s calculated

Total change = current − start % of starting weight = total change ÷ start × 100 % of the way to goal = (start − current) ÷ (start − goal) × 100 Average per week = total change ÷ weeks since your start date

Worked example: start 240 lb, now 212 lb, goal 180 lb. Total change is −28 lb, or −11.7% of 240. You’ve covered 28 of the 60 lb between start and goal — 47% of the way — with 32 lb to go. If you started 20 weeks ago, that’s an average of −1.4 lb a week.

Reading your numbers

An average hides the shape of the journey. Weekly changes are rarely even — faster some weeks, flat or even up in others — and an average since the start blends all of them together. To see what the last few weeks look like, put your weigh-ins into the weight trend calculator.

“% of the way” compares you only with your own start and goal. It isn’t a score, and there’s no right speed.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate my weight loss progress?

Subtract your starting weight from your current weight for the total change, divide it by the starting weight for the percentage, and divide what you’ve lost by the distance from start to goal for “% of the way”.

What does “% of the way to goal” mean?

The share of the distance between your starting weight and your goal that you’ve already covered. From 240 lb toward 180 lb, being at 210 lb is 50% of the way.

Why is my weekly average lower than at the start?

The average since the start mixes faster and slower weeks, and many people see bigger changes early on. It’s common for weekly changes to vary a lot.

Should I weigh myself every day or once a week?

Either can work. Daily weigh-ins plus a trend line make any single reading matter less; weekly weigh-ins are quicker. What matters most is consistency: same scale, same time of day, similar clothing.

Do I need a start date?

Only for the average per week. Everything else works from the three weights alone.
