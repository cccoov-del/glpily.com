Protein calculator for weight loss

How it’s calculated

Low end = weight (kg) × 1.2 g High end = weight (kg) × 1.6 g Per meal = daily amount ÷ meals per day (pounds to kg: lb × 0.45359237)

Worked example: 180 lb is 81.6 kg. 81.6 × 1.2 = 98 g and 81.6 × 1.6 = 131 g, so the range is about 98–131 g of protein a day — roughly 33–44 g per meal over three meals.

For comparison, the general recommended dietary allowance (RDA) for adults is 0.8 g per kg — about 65 g for the same person. A 2025 joint advisory from four US lifestyle-medicine, nutrition and obesity organizations notes that higher targets, such as 1.2–1.6 g/kg a day, have been proposed during active weight reduction.

At higher body weights

Grams per kilogram of current weight get large quickly — 1.6 g/kg at 300 lb is over 200 g a day. The 2025 advisory points out that for people with obesity it’s unclear whether targets should be based on actual, adjusted or ideal body weight, because actual weight can significantly overestimate needs; it also mentions an absolute target of 80–120 g a day as a practical alternative. Ask your clinician or dietitian which approach fits you.

Frequently asked questions

How much protein do I need a day to lose weight?

There’s no single number for everyone. A range often cited for people who are losing weight is 1.2–1.6 g per kg of body weight a day, compared with the general adult RDA of 0.8 g/kg. Information, not advice — your clinician or a dietitian can confirm a target for you.

Should I spread protein across meals?

Many people find a daily amount easier to reach with some protein at each meal rather than most of it at dinner. The calculator shows an even split, but it doesn’t have to be exact.

Can I eat too much protein?

The same advisory notes that prolonged intakes at or above about 2 g per kg a day should be avoided. If you have kidney disease or another medical condition, ask your clinician before changing how much protein you eat.

Should I use my current weight or my goal weight?

Experts don’t agree on this for people with a higher body weight, which is why the advisory mentions adjusted weights and absolute targets. Use whichever your clinician or dietitian recommends.

My appetite is small. How do people get enough protein?

Some people start meals with the protein part, choose protein-dense foods like Greek yogurt, eggs, fish or tofu, and eat smaller meals more often. A dietitian can suggest options that suit you.
