How it’s calculated

% change = (current weight − starting weight) ÷ starting weight × 100

A negative result means your weight has gone down; a positive one means it has gone up.

Worked example: starting at 220 lb and now at 198 lb: 198 − 220 = −22 lb, and −22 ÷ 220 × 100 = −10%. You’ve lost 10% of your starting weight.

The same idea in kilograms: 95 kg → 88 kg is −7 kg, and −7 ÷ 95 × 100 = −7.4%.

Why a percentage is more useful than pounds

Twenty pounds is a bigger share of a 160-lb starting weight (12.5%) than of a 320-lb one (6.25%). A percentage puts your change in proportion to your body, which is why it’s the usual way to compare progress over time — and why it’s kinder than comparing your numbers with anyone else’s.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate my weight loss percentage?

Subtract your starting weight from your current weight, divide by your starting weight and multiply by 100. From 250 lb to 230 lb: −20 ÷ 250 × 100 = −8%.

What is 5% of my body weight?

Multiply your starting weight by 0.05. For 200 lb that’s 10 lb; for 90 kg it’s 4.5 kg. The table above has more.

Which starting weight should I use?

The weight on the day you started tracking — and keep it fixed. Moving the starting point (to a later or a higher weight) changes the percentage, so your numbers stop being comparable.

My percentage slipped back this week. Is that normal?

Yes — weight moves up and down from day to day with water, salt, meal timing and hormones. Look at the direction over several weeks rather than a single weigh-in; the weight trend calculator smooths out the noise.

Does it work for weight gain?

Yes. A gain shows as a positive percentage and “You’ve gained…”.
