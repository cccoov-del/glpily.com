How it’s calculated

One pound is defined as exactly 0.45359237 kilograms — the international avoirdupois pound, fixed in 1959 and used for body weight in the US. So:

kilograms = pounds × 0.45359237

Worked example: 200 lb × 0.45359237 = 90.718474 kg, which rounds to 90.72 kg.

In your head: halve the pounds, then take away 10% of that. 200 lb → 100 → 100 − 10 = 90 kg. The exact answer is 90.72 kg, so the shortcut is within about 1%.

Frequently asked questions

How many kg is 1 lb?

Exactly 0.45359237 kg — about 0.45 kg, or roughly 454 grams. That’s a little under half a kilo.

How do I convert lbs to kg in my head?

Divide by 2.2. Or halve the number and subtract 10% of the result: 180 lb → 90 → 90 − 9 = 81 kg (the exact answer is 81.65 kg).

Why is “pound” abbreviated “lb”?

It comes from the Latin libra, a Roman unit of weight. “lbs” is an informal plural; both mean the same avoirdupois pound.

Why does my weight look so different in kilograms?

It’s the same weight in a different unit. A kilogram is about 2.2 times a pound, so the number in kg is always smaller — a 10-lb change is about 4.5 kg.

Should I track my weight in lb or kg?

Whichever your scale shows — using the same unit, scale and time of day matters more than which unit it is. If you need both, the kg to lbs converter works the other way round.
