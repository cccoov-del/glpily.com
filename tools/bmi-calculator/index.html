BMI is a screening number, not a diagnosis — it doesn’t measure body fat. Not for children, teens or pregnancy.

How it’s calculated

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)² Imperial: BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height (in)²

Worked example: 200 lb at 5 ft 9 in (69 in): 703 × 200 ÷ (69 × 69) = 140,600 ÷ 4,761 = 29.5 — the overweight range.

Metric: 90 kg at 1.70 m: 1.70 × 1.70 = 2.89, and 90 ÷ 2.89 = 31.1 — obesity class I.

The calculator uses the exact metric conversion, so its imperial results can differ from the 703 shortcut in the second decimal.

What BMI can’t tell you

  • It doesn’t measure body fat. BMI can’t tell muscle from fat or bone, so a muscular person can land in the “overweight” range with little body fat.
  • It doesn’t show where fat is. The WHO calls BMI a surrogate marker of fatness; measurements such as waist circumference add information.
  • It means different things for different people. People of South Asian, Chinese, other Asian, Middle Eastern, Black African or African-Caribbean background have health risks at lower BMIs — the NHS uses 23–27.4 as overweight and 27.5 or more as obesity for them. At the same BMI, body fat also tends to differ with age and sex.
  • It isn’t for children, teens or pregnancy. Under-18s (under-20s in US charts) are assessed with age- and sex-specific BMI percentiles, and BMI isn’t used to judge weight during pregnancy.
  • It’s a screening number, not a diagnosis. A clinician looks at BMI alongside your history, measurements, blood pressure and lab results.

Frequently asked questions

What is a healthy BMI?

For most adults, 18.5 to 24.9 is described as the healthy range. It’s a population guideline: it can’t account for muscle, ethnicity or age, and your clinician will look at more than BMI.

How do I calculate BMI with pounds and inches?

Multiply your weight in pounds by 703, then divide by your height in inches squared. For 160 lb at 5 ft 5 in (65 in): 703 × 160 ÷ 4,225 = 26.6.

Is BMI accurate for muscular people?

Not very. Muscle is dense, so athletes and people who lift can have a “high” BMI with little body fat. Waist measurements or a body-composition test give a better picture.

Why are the BMI cut-offs lower for some ethnic groups?

People from some backgrounds have a higher chance of health problems at a lower BMI, so the NHS uses lower thresholds — 23 and 27.5 — for people of South Asian, Chinese, other Asian, Middle Eastern, Black African or African-Caribbean background.

Can I use this BMI calculator for my child?

No. Children and teens need a BMI-for-age percentile, which compares them with others of the same age and sex. Ask your pediatrician or use a child-specific calculator.
